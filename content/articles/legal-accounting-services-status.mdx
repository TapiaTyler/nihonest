> **Editorial review needed:** This guide explains the immigration category, not eligibility to enter a regulated profession. Confirm qualification, registration, reserved-work, and practice rules with the profession's Japanese regulator.

## What this status authorizes

Legal/Accounting Services ([法律・会計業務](/glossary/horitsu-kaikei-gyomu), **hōritsu, kaikei gyōmu**) covers legal or accounting work that Japanese law requires a qualified professional to perform. It is a narrow, qualification-gated status—not a general route for anyone employed by a law firm, accounting firm, tax office, or compliance department.

The applicant must hold the applicable qualification recognized under Japanese law and perform the corresponding regulated services. A degree, overseas professional license, job title, employer, or client list cannot replace that requirement.

## The eleven professions in the current criteria

The current landing criteria name:

- Attorney ([弁護士](/glossary/bengoshi), **bengoshi**)
- Judicial scrivener (司法書士, **shihō shoshi**)
- Land and house investigator (土地家屋調査士, **tochi kaoku chōsashi**)
- Registered foreign lawyer (外国法事務弁護士, **gaikokuhō jimu bengoshi**)
- Certified public accountant (公認会計士, **kōnin kaikeishi**)
- Foreign certified public accountant (外国公認会計士, **gaikoku kōnin kaikeishi**)
- Certified tax accountant ([税理士](/glossary/zei-rishi), **zeirishi**)
- Labor and social security attorney (社会保険労務士, **shakai hoken rōmushi**)
- Patent attorney (弁理士, **benrishi**)
- Maritime procedure agent (海事代理士, **kaiji dairishi**)
- Administrative scrivener ([行政書士](/glossary/gyosei-shoshi), **gyōsei shoshi**)

This list controls the status analysis. Similar English titles, an overseas qualification, or experience doing related work do not establish that the person holds one of these Japanese-law qualifications.

## Overseas qualifications and foreign-law practice

An overseas lawyer or accountant should identify the exact Japanese recognition and registration route before planning immigration. For example, “registered foreign lawyer” is itself one of the listed Japanese-law categories; being admitted abroad is not the same as completing that registration.

Do not use this article as a licensing checklist. Regulators determine eligibility, examinations, experience, registration, professional entities, permitted titles, and scope of practice. Immigration then examines whether the proposed Japanese activity fits the recognized qualification.

## Adjacent roles may use another status

The following titles do not automatically use Legal/Accounting Services:

- paralegal, legal assistant, or litigation-support employee;
- compliance, risk, contracts, policy, or corporate-governance specialist;
- bookkeeping, finance, audit-support, payroll, or ordinary accounting employee;
- legal translator, interpreter, researcher, or international coordinator; and
- consultant or manager who does not personally perform reserved professional services.

Depending on the actual duties, qualifications, contract, and organization, professional office work may instead require Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services. Genuine operation or management of a business may point to Business Manager, but that status does not let an unqualified person manage reserved legal or accounting work that Japanese law requires a listed qualification to perform.

## Evidence and organization responsibilities

The current Legal/Accounting Services evidence list requires proof that the applicant holds one of the eleven Japanese qualifications, such as a license or certificate copy. The procedure and the person's circumstances determine the rest of the submission. Plan to document:

- the exact profession, qualification, registration, and any conditions on practice;
- the regulated services the person will actually perform;
- employment, partnership, engagement, office, or practice arrangements;
- remuneration, duration, worksite, and responsible organization or clients; and
- procedure-specific identity, residence, tax, and compliance records.

An employer or practice should use the correct profession name and describe duties precisely. It may support a Certificate of Eligibility or another application through an eligible representative, but it does not issue or guarantee immigration permission or the professional authorization.

## Period and renewal

Available periods are **five years, three years, one year, or three months**. The granted period is an individual decision.

Renewal is possible while the qualifying professional authorization, regulated activity, organization or practice, and residence compliance continue. A suspended or ended registration, move to a different profession, substantial duty change, or shift into unregulated consulting, management, or support work can require a new status analysis rather than a routine extension.

## Side work and independent practice

The status is limited by the authorized professional activity. A second engagement within the same regulated profession still has to comply with professional rules and immigration evidence requirements. Teaching, writing, translation, consulting, business management, or other paid work does not become covered merely because it relates to law or accounting.

Before accepting other work, check the professional regulator's rules, employment or partnership terms, confidentiality and conflict duties, tax treatment, and whether immigration permission for activity outside the status or a change of status is required.

## Questions to resolve before applying

1. Which of the eleven Japanese professions applies?
2. Is the required Japanese qualification and registration already effective?
3. Which proposed tasks are legally reserved to that profession?
4. Which tasks are support, consulting, management, or another kind of work?
5. What organization, practice, clients, contracts, remuneration, and worksites are involved?
6. Does any additional activity require separate permission or a different status?
7. Which current procedure and evidence checklist apply?
