> **Current as of September 12, 2026:** Since April 1, 2026, riders aged 16 or older can receive traffic-violation notices for covered bicycle offenses. The change did not turn every warning into a ticket, and serious conduct can still follow criminal procedures.

## Treat a bicycle as a vehicle

A Bicycle ([自転車](/glossary/jitensha), **jitensha**) is generally a light vehicle under the Road Traffic Act. The roadway is the default, using the left side in the required manner. A bicycle may use a sidewalk only under defined legal conditions, and pedestrians retain priority there. Slow down and stop when necessary to avoid obstructing or endangering them.

Obey traffic lights, stop signs, railway-crossing rules, one-way or bicycle signs, and intersection duties. Turn on the required light at night. Do not ride after drinking alcohol, use a phone while riding, carry passengers unlawfully, ride side by side where prohibited, or use unsafe brakes or equipment. All riders should wear a bicycle helmet under the current national effort obligation; child-safety and passenger equipment need particular care.

Check the current NPA rulebook before riding. A rented or shared bicycle does not transfer the operator's legal duties to the rental company.

## Confirm what kind of cycle you have

An ordinary bicycle, qualifying pedal-assist bicycle, nonconforming powered cycle, moped, and specified small motorized bicycle do not use identical rules. A product marketed as an e-bike may legally be a motor vehicle if its assistance or propulsion exceeds the bicycle standard. That can trigger licensing, registration, number plate, helmet, insurance, and roadway requirements.

Do not rely only on the product name, overseas specification, removable throttle, or shop description. Confirm Japanese classification before purchase or use.

## Understand the April 2026 enforcement system

The Traffic-violation Notice ([青切符](/glossary/aokippu), **ao kippu**, commonly “blue ticket”) system has applied to riders aged 16 or older since April 1, 2026 for covered violations. Police guidance distinguishes ordinary instruction or warnings, covered notice procedures, and criminal handling for serious conduct such as dangerous alcohol-related or obstructive behavior.

Do not memorize a social-media list of fines as if it were the whole law. The conduct, age, danger, consequences, and current classification matter. Follow the notice instructions and seek qualified help if you do not understand the allegation or process.

## Register, insure, and park locally

When buying or receiving a bicycle, complete the applicable Anti-theft Registration ([防犯登録](/glossary/bohan-toroku), **bōhan tōroku**) and preserve the purchase, registration, transfer, and frame-number records. Used bicycles and moves can require cancellation, transfer, or new local handling.

Bicycle liability-insurance duties vary by prefecture and can also be addressed through household, school, employer, credit-card, or dedicated policies. Confirm who is insured, which bicycle and riders are covered, liability limits, personal injury, legal support, exclusions, and duplicate coverage. Registration is not insurance.

Use designated bicycle parking and observe building, station, school, employer, and municipal rules. A locked bicycle can still be removed from a prohibited area, with retrieval procedures and fees set locally.

## After a bicycle collision

Stop, prevent further danger, help injured people, call 119 for urgent medical help, and report the Traffic Accident ([交通事故](/glossary/kotsu-jiko), **kōtsū jiko**) to police at 110. This applies even when the collision involves only a pedestrian, another bicycle, or apparently minor damage. Preserve identities, location, photographs, witnesses, bicycle information, police details, medical records, and insurance notices.
