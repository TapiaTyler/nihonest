> **Editorial review needed:** This guide explains the umbrella. Eligibility and permitted activity must be checked against the exact designation and current program page.

## The label is only the beginning

Designated Activities ([特定活動](/glossary/tokutei-katsudo), **tokutei katsudō**) is not one program with one set of rights. The Minister of Justice designates a particular activity for the individual. Two people displaying the same English status name can therefore have different authorized activities, work conditions, family options, periods, and renewal rules.

When a residence card is issued, read it together with the passport and the designation document. Some short designated programs, including Digital Nomad, do not issue a residence card at all. Never infer permission from the umbrella label alone.

## Choose the exact route

### Continued job hunting after Japanese study

For defined graduates of qualifying Japanese institutions who began job hunting before graduation and continue afterward. School type, credential, recommendation, prior search, finances, and the relationship between study and prospective work can matter.

[Learn more about continued job hunting →](/articles/continued-job-hunting-after-study)

### J-Find Future Creation Individual

For recent graduates of qualifying highly ranked overseas universities who meet the age, degree, graduation-date, funds, and activity conditions for job hunting or entrepreneurial preparation.

[Learn more about J-Find →](/articles/future-creation-j-find)

### Digital Nomad

A non-extendable six-month route for qualifying foreign-facing remote work. It has nationality or citizenship, income, insurance, client or employer, and repeat-use restrictions and does not authorize employment by a Japanese organization.

[Learn more about Digital Nomad →](/articles/digital-nomad-designated-activities)

### Long Stay for sightseeing and recreation

A savings-funded, no-work route for qualifying adults from visa-waiver countries or regions. It has separate rules for an accompanying spouse and does not permit dependent children under the program.

[Learn more about long-stay sightseeing →](/articles/long-stay-sightseeing-designated-activities)

### Working Holiday

A bilateral, holiday-first program with incidental employment. Nationality, residence, age, quota, funds, insurance, participation count, and application-place rules belong to the applicable agreement and Japanese mission.

[Learn more about Working Holiday →](/articles/working-holiday-in-japan)

### Start-up preparation

A supported preparation route for founders working through an approved municipality or private organization before a possible Business Manager application. Approval of one stage does not guarantee the next.

[Learn more about the Start-up route →](/articles/startup-visa)

### Medical Stay

Treatment expected to exceed ninety days can involve a medical Designated Activities route, while shorter treatment may use a short-stay process. The medical plan, hospital, coordinator, funding, insurance, and any attendant must be analyzed under the correct branch.

[Learn more about Medical Stay →](/articles/medical-stay-visa)

## Rules that cannot be shared across the umbrella

Do not copy any of these from one route to another:

- whether work is permitted and which employer, client, or activity is covered;
- whether outside-activity permission is available;
- whether a spouse, child, parent, attendant, or other person may accompany;
- the initial period, cumulative maximum, extension, or repeat-entry rule;
- whether a residence card and municipal registration apply;
- whether a school, employer, municipality, coordinator, or other organization must support the case; or
- the document list and application channel.

The general status table lists several possible periods, including an individually designated period, but the child program controls the practical period and whether continuation is possible.

## A safe verification sequence

1. Name the exact designated activity, not merely “Designated Activities.”
2. Identify whether the person is applying from abroad, changing status, or extending an existing designation.
3. Confirm the program's current official page and any nationality-specific Japanese mission instructions.
4. Read the designation document and residence records for the actual authorized activity.
5. Check work, family, residence-card, municipal, tax, insurance, and departure consequences separately.
6. Reassess before changing activities, organizations, clients, family circumstances, or the intended length of stay.
