## Withholding is an advance collection

Tax withholding at source ([源泉徴収](/glossary/gensen-choshu), **gensen chōshū**) means the salary payer calculates and sends an applicable amount of income tax to the national government when salary is paid. The amount shown on a payslip is not necessarily the final annual tax.

Withholding can differ based on which employer receives the employee's declarations, the payment frequency, the tax table used, residence classification, and other facts. A second employer may withhold differently from the main employer.

Check each payslip for gross salary, taxable and non-taxable items, social insurance, national income-tax withholding, resident-tax collection, and take-home pay. These are separate amounts with separate correction routes.

## What year-end adjustment does

Year-end adjustment ([年末調整](/glossary/nenmatsu-chosei), **nenmatsu chōsei**) lets a qualifying employer reconcile income tax withheld from covered salary against the annual tax calculation it can perform through payroll.

Employees are commonly asked for current-year declarations and evidence concerning matters such as dependants, a spouse, insurance premiums, the basic exemption, and other covered deductions. Use the form for the stated tax year and report changes rather than copying an older form without checking it.

Year-end adjustment is not a universal substitute for a final return. It may not settle:

- salary or income that the adjusting employer cannot include;
- work for multiple employers under the applicable filing rules;
- freelance, business, investment, rental, or overseas income;
- deductions that require or are claimed through a return;
- a year in which the person was not eligible for adjustment; or
- a tax-residence, treaty, remittance, or foreign-tax-credit issue.

## If you changed employers during the year

A new employer can generally need the prior employer's salary and withholding information to include qualifying earlier salary in year-end adjustment. The prior Salary Income Withholding Tax Slip ([給与所得の源泉徴収票](/glossary/kyuyo-shotoku-no-gensen-choshu-hyo), **kyūyo shotoku no gensen chōshūhyō**) is the normal record.

Ask the former employer promptly. NTA guidance generally requires the employee copy by January 31 of the following year, or within one month after a mid-year departure from that employment. If the new employer cannot confirm the prior salary and withholding, it cannot simply guess the figures for year-end adjustment.

Keep the slip even after giving payroll the information. You may need it for a return, municipal procedure, loan, immigration evidence, or later correction.

## Supporting relatives outside Japan

Claiming a deduction connected to a relative living outside Japan can require defined relationship and remittance documents. Depending on the relative's age and circumstances, study-abroad evidence, disability facts, or evidence of a specified annual remittance amount can also matter. Foreign-language documents generally need a Japanese translation.

Do not assume that sending money to a shared family account establishes the deduction for every relative. Preserve transfers identifying the recipient and purpose, civil-status documents, translations, and the NTA instructions for the relevant year.

## Review the annual withholding slip

When you receive the slip, compare it with your payslips and year-end forms. Confirm the identity and payer details, total salary, employment-income deduction result, deductions, withholding amount, and other populated fields.

The employee copy should not display a My Number. Store it securely because it contains detailed financial and personal information.

Ask payroll about a clerical error, but use the tax office or a licensed tax accountant for questions about the legal treatment of income or deductions. If a correction, missing employer, additional income, or unclaimed deduction remains, investigate a Final Tax Return ([確定申告](/glossary/kakutei-shinkoku), **kakutei shinkoku**).
