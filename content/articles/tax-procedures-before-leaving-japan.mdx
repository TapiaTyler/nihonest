> **Editorial review needed:** “Departure” has a specific tax meaning, and national and local procedures differ. Confirm the departure date, tax residence, filing duty, responsible tax office, January 1 municipality, business status, and continuing Japanese income.

## Decide whether the move ends tax residence

A vacation or temporary assignment abroad is not necessarily a tax departure. Begin with the [tax-residence guide](/articles/tax-residence-and-taxable-income-in-japan) and document whether you will cease having a Japanese domicile or residence.

If procedures remain after you lose that domicile or residence, choose the national income-tax route before leaving:

- appoint a tax agent in Japan and file the applicable Final Tax Return ([確定申告](/glossary/kakutei-shinkoku), **kakutei shinkoku**) through that person during the ordinary following-year period; or
- if no tax agent is appointed, complete the required quasi-final return ([準確定申告](/glossary/jun-kakutei-shinkoku), **jun-kakutei shinkoku**) and payment before departure.

A national tax agent ([納税管理人](/glossary/nozei-kanrinin), **nōzei kanrinin**) can be an individual resident in Japan or a Japanese corporation. The responsible filing office is based on the taxpayer's place for tax payment, not the agent's address.

## Handle resident tax separately

Moving-out notification does not itself settle Individual Resident Tax ([住民税](/glossary/jumin-zei), **jūminzei**). Prior-year income, the January 1 municipality, current assessment, salary collection, unpaid installments, and a later bill all matter.

Ask the municipal tax desk:

- whether an assessment already exists or will be issued later;
- whether the employer will collect remaining installments;
- whether you must pay before leaving or appoint a local tax agent;
- which address and person will receive notices; and
- how a correction or refund will be handled from abroad.

The national tax-agent notice does not automatically appoint the agent for municipal tax.

## Close or preserve every related record

Before losing access to an employer, phone number, bank, My Number Card, address, or accounting system, obtain:

- Salary Income Withholding Tax Slips from every employer;
- current-year salary, side-income, expense, remittance, and foreign-tax records;
- prior returns and year-end-adjustment documents;
- resident-tax assessments and payment history;
- agent notices and proof of submission;
- business, blue-return, consumption-tax, and invoice-registration records; and
- secure ways for the agent and authorities to contact you.

Closing an individual business can require separate closure, blue-return, payroll, consumption-tax, and invoice-system filings. Japanese-source rent, investments, business, or later payments can create obligations after departure.

## Coordinate refunds and final payments

Do not close the only practical refund account or assume an overseas transfer is available for every payment. Agree in writing how the agent will receive notices, obtain missing records, pay tax, handle refunds, and report completion.

Tax departure remains separate from immigration departure, re-entry permission, municipal deregistration, health insurance, pension, housing, banking, and employment. Use the broader [leaving Japan checklist](/articles/leaving-japan-and-closing-out-procedures) alongside this guide.
