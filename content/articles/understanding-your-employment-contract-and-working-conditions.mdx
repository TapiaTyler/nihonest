## Immigration permission and employment rights answer different questions

A residence status can authorize a category of activity, but it does not decide every condition of the job. Japanese labor protections generally apply to a covered worker regardless of nationality. Conversely, signing a lawful employment contract does not establish that immigration authorizes the activity.

Start by asking whether the arrangement is genuinely employment. A Labor Contract ([労働契約](/glossary/rodo-keiyaku), **rōdō keiyaku**) normally involves a worker providing labor under an employer's direction in exchange for wages. Calling the agreement “freelance,” “outsourcing,” “manager,” or “trainee” does not conclusively determine its legal treatment. Direction and control, freedom to refuse or substitute work, working time and place, equipment, remuneration, and business independence can matter.

If the real relationship is unclear, preserve the offer, messages, schedules, instructions, invoices, pay records, and organization chart and ask a Labour Bureau, union, attorney, or other qualified adviser. Do not choose the label that seems most convenient for immigration or tax.

## Collect the documents that work together

Before starting, obtain and compare:

- the job advertisement and offer;
- the Labor Contract;
- the Notice of Working Conditions ([労働条件通知書](/glossary/rodo-joken-tsuchisho), **rōdō jōken tsūchisho**);
- the Rules of Employment ([就業規則](/glossary/shugyo-kisoku), **shūgyō kisoku**) that apply to your worker category and workplace;
- pay, bonus, retirement-allowance, expense, confidentiality, intellectual-property, side-work, remote-work, and disciplinary policies;
- any applicable collective agreement; and
- the immigration documents describing the employer and authorized duties.

These records do not have identical legal roles. A company cannot reduce a statutory minimum by writing a lower term into a contract. Workplace rules also cannot override legislation or an applicable collective agreement. Employers continuously using ten or more workers at a workplace generally must prepare and file rules of employment and make them accessible, but labor protections do not disappear at a smaller workplace.

## Check the written working conditions

The employer must clearly indicate core conditions when hiring. Defined items must be provided in writing or, when the worker requests it, through an accepted electronic method. Confirm at least:

- whether the contract is fixed-term or indefinite and its start date;
- for a fixed term, the renewal criteria and any limit on renewals or total duration;
- the workplace and duties, including the disclosed scope of future changes;
- start and finish times, overtime, breaks, days off, and leave;
- how wages are calculated and paid, the payroll closing date, and payday;
- promotion where applicable; and
- retirement and dismissal rules.

Part-time and fixed-term workers must also receive defined information about pay increases, bonuses, retirement allowance, and the employment-management consultation contact. Ask for an explanation in a language you understand, but retain the exact Japanese document because the translation may be only an aid. Do not sign blank pages or accept a promise that important terms will be filled in later.

## Understand fixed and indefinite terms

A Fixed-term Labor Contract ([有期労働契約](/glossary/yuki-rodo-keiyaku), **yūki rōdō keiyaku**) ends on a stated date unless it is renewed or another rule applies. Check whether renewal is automatic, possible, conditional, or not expected, and which performance, business, workload, ability, conduct, and renewal-limit criteria are disclosed.

The ordinary maximum for one fixed-term contract is generally three years. Defined contracts involving specified expert knowledge, workers aged sixty or older, or a project with a necessary completion period can follow different limits. This is not the same as how repeated renewals are treated.

An Indefinite Labor Contract ([無期労働契約](/glossary/muki-rodo-keiyaku), **muki rōdō keiyaku**) has no agreed ending date. “Indefinite” does not mean the job can never end, nor does it automatically mean the title, pay, or benefits become identical to another employee category.

## Do not treat probation as a rights-free period

Confirm the probation length, evaluation criteria, pay, duties, and what happens at its end. A probation clause does not let an employer ignore wage, safety, discrimination, or dismissal rules. If the actual conditions differ from the written notice, record the difference and raise it promptly.

## Check deductions, penalties, and documents held by the employer

A contract must not predetermine a penalty or fixed damages merely because the worker breaches the contract. This does not erase responsibility for proven loss under applicable law, so seek advice before assuming either that a charge is valid or that no liability is possible.

Ask for every proposed wage deduction in writing. Do not surrender control of a passport or residence card as security for employment. An employer may inspect documents for lawful verification, but immigration identity records remain the resident's documents.

## Before accepting the job

1. Compare the advertisement, offer, written notice, contract, and workplace rules.
2. Mark every blank, inconsistency, translation uncertainty, and oral promise.
3. Confirm the real employer, worksite, supervisor, duties, worker category, and any dispatch arrangement.
4. Confirm immigration fit separately from contract quality.
5. Save dated copies outside employer-controlled systems.
6. Ask questions before beginning work or relocating, when correction is usually easier.
