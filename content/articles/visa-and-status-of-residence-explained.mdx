## Six connected—but separate—things

Informal phrases such as “work visa” can hide which decision or document matters. A typical long-term entry involves several separate concepts:

1. **A route and intended activity.** Work, study, family relationship, business, or another basis determines which immigration category to examine.
2. **Certificate of Eligibility ([在留資格認定証明書](/glossary/certificate-of-eligibility), *zairyū shikaku nintei shōmeisho*).** For many long-term routes, a representative in Japan applies for a COE before the overseas visa application. It supports later examination; it is not permission to enter.
3. **Visa ([査証](/glossary/sasho), *sashō*).** A Japanese embassy or consulate issues the visa used to seek entry. A visa is not issued at the Japanese port of entry and does not guarantee landing.
4. **Landing Permission ([上陸許可](/glossary/joriku-kyoka), *jōriku kyoka*).** Immigration decides at the border whether to permit landing. Plans and documents should remain consistent with the activity previously presented.
5. **Status of Residence ([在留資格](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku), *zairyū shikaku*).** This controls the activity or personal position under which the person may remain.
6. **Period of Stay ([在留期間](/glossary/zairyu-kikan), *zairyū kikan*).** This is the granted time limit. It may differ from the period requested or the maximum listed for a route.

Eligible mid- to long-term residents also receive a Residence Card ([在留カード](/glossary/zairyu-card), *zairyū kādo*) at landing or after address registration, depending on the port. The card records important permission details; it does not replace the underlying rules.

## A COE is useful, not conclusive

The COE indicates that the proposed activity was assessed against a landing-condition category. It can reduce the material examined during the consular process, but the Ministry of Foreign Affairs expressly warns that it does not guarantee a visa. A visa likewise does not guarantee landing.

The ordinary COE validity period is currently three months. Check the issue date and current official guidance before arranging travel. Pandemic-era validity extensions were temporary and should not be treated as the normal rule. Since March 2023, an emailed COE or printed copy can be presented for visa and landing procedures.

If important facts change after the COE was issued—such as the employer, school, activity, relationship, or intended entry timing—ask the sponsor, responsible consular post, or Immigration Services Agency (ISA) what must be updated. Do not assume an old approval covers a new plan.

## Short entry is not a shortcut to long-term work

Some nationalities can enter for a short visit without first obtaining a visa, provided the stay and activity meet the applicable rules. That exemption does not itself authorize income-earning work or turn a visit into a long-term residence route. If the plan includes paid work or a stay beyond the permitted short period, identify the correct route before traveling.

## After landing, follow the permission actually granted

Read the status and period granted at landing rather than relying on the period you expected. Before working, studying, or beginning another activity, confirm that it fits the granted status and any separate permission.

When circumstances later change:

- use an Extension of Period of Stay ([在留期間更新許可](/glossary/zairyu-kikan-koshin-kyoka), *zairyū kikan kōshin kyoka*) to request more time on the current basis;
- use a Change of Status of Residence ([在留資格変更許可](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku-henko-kyoka), *zairyū shikaku henkō kyoka*) when the future activity or position belongs under a different status;
- file any required resident notification after covered employer, school, contract, spouse, or address changes; and
- arrange re-entry correctly before temporary international travel.

Each is a separate procedure. Filing one does not silently complete or approve the others.

## What to verify for your own case

- the exact activity or relationship supporting residence;
- the current ISA page and evidence list for the status and application context;
- the embassy or consulate responsible for the overseas visa application;
- COE, passport, visa, and planned-entry validity dates;
- the status and period actually granted at landing; and
- any separate work, licensing, municipal, tax, or notification obligations.

Requirements can vary by route, nationality, organization category, family circumstances, and application location. When facts do not fit the published examples, obtain case-specific guidance rather than forcing them into an informal visa label.
