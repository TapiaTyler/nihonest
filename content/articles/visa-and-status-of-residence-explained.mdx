> **Draft coverage:** This article maps the route and the questions to verify. It is not yet a review-ready eligibility or document checklist.

## What this route covers

A Japanese visa is generally used to seek entry at the border; a status of residence defines the activity or personal position under which a person may stay after landing. A Certificate of Eligibility supports many long-term visa applications, but it is neither a visa nor a guarantee of landing permission. Everyday speech often calls every route a visa, so always identify which document and decision an instruction actually concerns.

## Who should examine it

- Anyone comparing long-term routes before applying
- People trying to understand the COE, overseas visa, landing, and residence-card sequence

## Evidence to plan for

- The exact activity, relationship, or position that will support residence
- The intended length of stay and whether paid activity is planned
- The checklist from the responsible Japanese mission and ISA status page

## Important boundaries

Confirm whether you need a visa before travel, whether a COE is expected, and which status of residence fits the real activity. Do not assume visa exemption for a short visit authorizes work or a later long-term stay.

## Before relying on this draft

Open the official sources linked below and use the current application page for the exact procedure you will file: Certificate of Eligibility, overseas visa, change of status, or extension. Requirements can differ by nationality, employer or host category, family circumstances, and the Japanese mission handling the application. Keep the planned activity consistent across contracts, forms, supporting records, and explanations.
