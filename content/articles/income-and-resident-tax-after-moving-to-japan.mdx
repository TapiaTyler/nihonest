> **Editorial review needed:** National income tax and individual resident tax use related income information but different authorities, calendars, declarations, and collection methods. Confirm local procedures with the municipality responsible for your case.

## Put two tax calendars on your timeline

National Income Tax ([所得税](/glossary/shotoku-zei), **shotokuzei**) generally follows income for the calendar year from January 1 through December 31. Salary tax may be collected through withholding ([源泉徴収](/glossary/gensen-choshu), **gensen chōshū**), reconciled through qualifying year-end adjustment, or settled through a Final Tax Return ([確定申告](/glossary/kakutei-shinkoku), **kakutei shinkoku**).

Individual Resident Tax ([住民税](/glossary/jumin-zei), **jūminzei**) is local. It generally uses prior-year income and is imposed and collected by the municipality connected to the person's residence on January 1 of the relevant assessment year.

This means income earned this year can affect a resident-tax bill collected later. A small first-year bill after arriving from abroad does not mean resident tax will remain small.

## Understand the first-year delay

A common sequence is:

1. You arrive and begin earning salary during one calendar year.
2. Income tax is withheld from salary during that year.
3. The employer may perform year-end adjustment, or you may file a national return after year end.
4. The municipality uses the prior-year income information to calculate the later resident-tax assessment.
5. Collection commonly begins through salary deductions or municipal payment notices.

The exact result depends on the arrival date, January 1 address, prior income, employer reporting, deductions, declarations, and local rules. Ask the municipality what it has recorded rather than assuming that no notice means no liability.

## Check how resident tax will be collected

Under salary special collection ([特別徴収](/glossary/tokubetsu-choshu), **tokubetsu chōshū**), an employer deducts the resident-tax installments from salary and pays the municipality. Under ordinary collection ([普通徴収](/glossary/futsu-choshu), **futsū chōshū**), the person pays using municipal notices or an available local payment channel.

A change of employer, loss of employment, leave, reduced hours, or departure can change the collection path without canceling the assessment. Ask:

- whether deductions will continue through the old or new employer;
- whether remaining installments will be collected at once or converted to ordinary collection;
- which address will receive notices; and
- how payment will be handled if you leave Japan.

Do not treat a payroll label such as “resident tax” as proof that every municipal amount is fully settled.

## Read the documents from different authorities separately

Keep:

- monthly payslips showing national income-tax and resident-tax deductions separately;
- the annual Salary Income Withholding Tax Slip;
- employer year-end-adjustment declarations and deduction certificates;
- any Final Tax Return and proof of submission or payment;
- municipal resident-tax assessment and payment notices; and
- records of job changes, address changes, arrival, and departure.

Income tax questions normally go to the responsible tax office. Individual resident-tax questions normally go to the responsible municipal tax desk. Payroll can explain what it deducted, but the employer cannot give every employee an authoritative determination of tax residence, foreign-income scope, or municipal liability.

## Plan cash flow before the later bill

Budget for resident tax while earning the income, even when collection has not begun. This is particularly important when:

- you arrived partway through a year;
- salary rises substantially;
- a job ends before the collection cycle finishes;
- you move to another municipality;
- you switch between employment and independent work; or
- you plan to leave Japan.

National filing exceptions and municipal declaration rules are not identical. A person who does not need a national return may still need to confirm a resident-tax declaration or missing income information with the municipality.

## Keep other systems separate

Tax payment does not authorize side work under immigration law or an employment contract. Likewise, income-tax and resident-tax treatment do not decide health-insurance or pension enrollment. Review each system on its own facts and keep the resulting records together.
