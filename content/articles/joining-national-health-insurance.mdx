## Enroll through your municipality

Registered residents who are not covered by an applicable employee or other public health-insurance arrangement are generally required to join National Health Insurance (国民健康保険, **kokumin kenkō hoken**). International students staying in Japan for three months or longer commonly fall into this group. Complete resident registration first, then ask the municipal insurance counter what to submit and when coverage begins.

Visa issuance alone does not determine enrollment. Short stays, people outside ordinary resident registration, people covered through employment, and people subject to special arrangements may be treated differently. Confirm with the municipality or the authority responsible for your status rather than copying a student checklist mechanically.

For covered treatment, insurance generally pays 70% and the insured person pays 30% at the point of care. Some services, medicines, checkups, private-room charges, and other costs may not be covered. Ask the provider before non-urgent treatment when cost is unclear.

## Premiums and income reporting

Premium calculations and payment arrangements are municipal. Prior-year income can affect the amount, and a new resident may still need to file an income declaration. Reductions may be available for low-income households, but they are not always automatic. Bring municipal notices to the counter if you do not understand them; ignoring a bill does not cancel enrollment.

> **Official guidance:** Travel insurance and school accident insurance may be useful supplements, but they do not automatically replace enrollment in Japan’s public system.

After enrollment, learn how eligibility is confirmed under the current system and keep your address and status information current. If you move, begin work with employer coverage, or leave Japan, ask both the old and new responsible offices what change or withdrawal procedure applies.
