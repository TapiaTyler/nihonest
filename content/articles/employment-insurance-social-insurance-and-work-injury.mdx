## “Insurance through work” contains different systems

Social Insurance ([社会保険](/glossary/shakai-hoken), **shakai hoken**) is often used informally for employee Health Insurance ([健康保険](/glossary/kenko-hoken), **kenkō hoken**) and Employees’ Pension Insurance ([厚生年金保険](/glossary/kosei-nenkin-hoken), **kōsei nenkin hoken**). Employment Insurance ([雇用保険](/glossary/koyo-hoken), **koyō hoken**) addresses unemployment and other employment-related benefits. Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance ([労災保険](/glossary/rosai-hoken), **rōsai hoken**) addresses covered work and commuting injury.

These systems do not share one eligibility test or solve the same problem. Immigration permission, private insurance, tax, National Health Insurance, and National Pension are separate again.

## Confirm employee health and pension enrollment

Enrollment first depends on whether the employer or workplace is covered and then whether the worker meets the applicable conditions. Corporate workplaces and defined individual establishments have their own rules; part-time coverage can depend on scheduled hours, remuneration, expected duration, student position, employer size, and changes being phased in by law.

Do not rely on one remembered weekly-hours or monthly-pay number. Ask payroll for:

- the enrollment date and insured-person records;
- the health insurer and pension office;
- the standard remuneration used for premiums;
- dependent-family procedures where applicable;
- treatment of bonuses, leave, and multiple workplaces; and
- the loss-of-coverage date and certificate when employment ends.

Employee coverage usually replaces—not duplicates—the ordinary municipal National Health Insurance ([国民健康保険](/glossary/kokumin-kenko-hoken), **kokumin kenkō hoken**) and National Pension ([国民年金](/glossary/kokumin-nenkin), **kokumin nenkin**) route for the same period. Notify the municipality when moving between systems; do not assume the employer closes a municipal record automatically.

## Employment Insurance has a different test

Employment Insurance generally covers a worker expected to remain employed for at least thirty-one days and scheduled for at least twenty hours weekly, subject to exclusions and special categories. Daytime students are commonly excluded unless an exception applies. Older workers, directors who also work as employees, seasonal work, multiple jobs, and changes in scheduled hours require specific review.

Confirm that the employer filed enrollment and check the insured-person number. When employment ends, review the stated separation reason and obtain the Employment-insurance Separation Notice ([離職票](/glossary/rishokuhyo), **rishokuhyō**) when needed. Eligibility, waiting periods, benefit restrictions, and permission to remain or work are separate; Hello Work decides the employment-insurance process, not immigration status.

## Treat a work or commuting injury as its own route

Workers’ compensation generally covers workers in Japan regardless of nationality or whether they are permanent, fixed-term, part-time, or students working part-time. It can address covered injury, illness, disability, death, medical care, and inability to work caused by work or a qualifying commute.

Do not automatically present a residence health-insurance card for an injury that may be occupational. Tell the medical provider the injury may be work-related, notify the employer, preserve the incident details, and contact the Labour Standards Inspection Office when classification or cooperation is uncertain. The worker—not only the employer—has an interest in the claim, and an employer saying it did not enroll does not safely end the inquiry.

For a commute, record the route, purpose, time, transport, deviations, police or accident information, and travel between multiple workplaces. A different route or interruption is not automatically covered or excluded; the statutory commuting test controls.

## Keep one coverage file

Retain:

- employment terms, schedules, and payslips;
- health, pension, employment-insurance, and workers’ compensation identifiers;
- enrollment and loss-of-coverage notices;
- medical records, incident reports, witness details, photographs, and expense receipts;
- municipal enrollment or withdrawal records; and
- documents showing each workplace when working multiple jobs.

Compare deductions with actual enrollment. A line on a payslip is evidence to investigate, not complete proof of coverage or correct calculation.

## When employment begins, changes, or ends

1. Ask which systems cover you and from what date.
2. Check whether municipal health or pension records must be closed or opened.
3. Report changes in hours, workplace, remuneration, family, address, name, or concurrent employment when required.
4. Treat a work or commuting injury through the workers’ compensation route promptly.
5. At separation, collect loss-of-coverage records and the Separation Notice and confirm the next coverage without leaving an unexplained gap.
6. Handle immigration notification, tax records, and residence permission separately.
