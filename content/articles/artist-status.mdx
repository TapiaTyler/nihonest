> **Editorial review needed:** This guide explains the national route and current evidence categories. It cannot decide whether a particular creative practice is Artist, Entertainer, employed specialist work, business management, or another activity.

## What Artist authorizes

Artist ([芸術](/glossary/geijutsu), **geijutsu**) covers income-producing activity in music, fine art, literature, and other artistic fields. ISA gives composers, painters, and writers as examples. The statutory boundary excludes activity belonging to Entertainer ([興行](/glossary/kogyo), **kōgyō**).

The route can be relevant to an established composer, painter, sculptor, writer, author, illustrator, or fine-art photographer. These are discovery examples, not an approved-occupation list. Immigration examines the proposed activity and the evidence supporting it.

## A contract is not always required

The current Artist procedure separates two situations.

- With a contract, evidence should identify the activity, contract period, position, and remuneration. If several organizations or clients are involved, retain the material for each relationship.
- Without a contract, the applicant prepares a concrete account of the activity, its duration, and the expected income arising from it.

Independent does not mean evidence-free. Describe what will be created, where the work will occur, how it reaches an audience or customer, when projects happen, who pays, and how the activity can support the proposed stay.

## Professional standing and artistic record

Current checklists may ask for a detailed résumé and material showing achievement in the artistic field. Depending on the practice, this can include recommendations, press coverage, awards, selection history, publications, recordings, exhibitions, commissions, catalogs, or other work records.

A portfolio can demonstrate the nature and level of the work, but it does not alone establish future activity, income, or the correct residence category. Tie each record to the proposed practice rather than submitting an unexplained collection of images or links.

## Compare nearby creative routes

### Performance and commercial production

Entertainer covers performance and other entertainment activity, and its official procedure also addresses advertising, broadcast or film production, commercial photography, and commercial recording. An artistic identity does not automatically move those activities to Artist.

### Employed design and media work

A designer or other creative specialist working under a Japanese organization contract may need comparison with Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services. The employee's real duties, specialist knowledge, qualifications, remuneration, and contracting organization matter.

### Unpaid artistic study

Cultural Activities ([文化活動](/glossary/bunka-katsudo), **bunka katsudō**) covers specified unpaid artistic activity or study. It is not a substitute for a paid artistic practice.

### Operating a business

Business Manager can become relevant when the principal activity is genuinely operating or managing a qualifying business. Calling a person a studio owner or creative director does not establish that route, and management status does not silently authorize every production activity.

Use [Which residence status fits creative and media work?](/articles/choosing-a-residence-status-for-creative-and-media-work) before relying on any one label.

## Build a coherent activity record

Prepare a timeline covering commissions, contracts, exhibitions, publication or release plans, work locations, counterparties, fees, royalties, and expected payment dates. Explain how past work supports the proposed activity and how projected income was calculated. Keep the description consistent across the COE application, contracts, client letters, financial evidence, and later visa application.

For variable or international income, identify the payer, currency, contract, payment channel, and relationship to the activity performed in Japan. Immigration classification, tax treatment, business registration, copyright, and client-contract rules are separate questions.

## Period and renewal

Available periods are **five years, three years, one year, or three months**. The period granted is an individual decision, not a choice guaranteed by the application.

For an extension, expect to show that the artistic activity continues and that the financial and residence record remains coherent. Current Artist materials can require activity and income evidence as well as tax certificates. Keep contracts, invoices, payment records, project calendars, publications, exhibitions, and other outputs during the stay.

If the principal activity changes—for example, from independent artistic creation to commercial production, company employment, or business operation—an extension of the same label may not be the correct procedure. Examine a change of status before beginning the new principal activity.

## Additional and side activity

Artist is activity-limited. Teaching, employed design, event production, commercial photography, acting, online content, or client work outside the authorized artistic activity is not automatically covered. Compare the proposed work with the current permission and check Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted ([資格外活動許可](/glossary/shikakugai-katsudo-kyoka), **shikakugai katsudō kyoka**) before it starts where necessary.

## Questions to resolve before applying

1. What exact artistic activity will be performed in Japan?
2. Is it independent, contracted, or a combination?
3. What projects, clients, venues, publishers, galleries, or distributors are confirmed?
4. How is expected income supported rather than merely estimated?
5. What professional record demonstrates sustained artistic activity?
6. Could any principal work instead be performance, commercial production, journalism, employed specialist work, or business management?
7. Which current checklist applies to the intended application procedure?
