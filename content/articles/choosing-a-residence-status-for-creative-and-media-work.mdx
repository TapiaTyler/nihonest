> **Editorial review needed:** This comparison identifies routes to examine. Creative titles are unusually broad, so obtain the contracts, organizations, clients, income model, work locations, and duty breakdown before drawing a conclusion.

## Start with the activity, not “creative visa”

Japan does not have one residence status for every creative profession. A photographer, filmmaker, designer, writer, or content creator can perform activities with different legal character. Ask what the person will actually do in Japan, for whom, under which contract, and how they will be paid.

## Main routes to compare

### Employed specialist design or media work

Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services ([技術・人文知識・国際業務](/glossary/gijutsu-jinbun-kokusai), **gijutsu, jinbun chishiki, kokusai gyōmu**) is a route to examine for specialized design, digital-product, advertising, and related work under a contract with an organization in Japan. ISA lists designer as an example, but graphic, UI, UX, web, motion, game, VFX, or digital-media titles remain duty-dependent.

Confirm what the person researches, plans, designs, produces, and decides; which specialist knowledge or foreign-cultural sensitivity the work requires; how education or experience relates; and whether routine production or service work dominates.

[Learn more about Engineer/Specialist status →](/articles/engineer-specialist-humanities-international-services-status)

### Income-producing artistic practice

Artist ([芸術](/glossary/geijutsu), **geijutsu**) covers income-producing artistic activity outside Entertainer. It can be relevant to composers, painters, writers, illustrators, fine artists, and other established creators. Both contracted and independent activity can be examined, but a concrete plan, expected income, and professional record matter.

[Learn more about Artist status →](/articles/artist-status)

### Performance and commercial production

Entertainer ([興行](/glossary/kogyo), **kōgyō**) covers performance, entertainment, and professional sports. Official materials also address advertising activity, broadcast or film production, commercial photography, and commercial recording or filming. Do not limit this comparison to actors and singers.

[Learn more about Entertainer status →](/articles/entertainer-status)

### Foreign-news journalism

Journalist ([報道](/glossary/hodo), **hōdō**) covers reporting and other journalistic activity under contract with a foreign news organization. Dispatch, employment, and qualifying freelance contracts can be relevant. Domestic media work or independent documentary content does not become Journalist from style or subject alone.

[Learn more about Journalist status →](/articles/journalist-status)

### Unpaid artistic or cultural study

Cultural Activities ([文化活動](/glossary/bunka-katsudo), **bunka katsudō**) covers specified unpaid artistic activity or specialized cultural study. It is not a route for paid client work, sales, sponsorships, or commercial commissions.

[Learn more about Cultural Activities →](/articles/cultural-activities-visa)

### Operating or managing a creative business

Business Manager ([経営・管理](/glossary/keiei-kanri), **keiei, kanri**) can become relevant when the principal activity is genuinely operating or managing a qualifying company, agency, production business, or studio. Founder, freelancer, producer, and creative director are not automatic management classifications.

[Learn more about Business Manager status →](/articles/business-manager-status)

## Compare common situations

### Graphic, product, UI, UX, or web designer

Examine employed specialist work first when the person works under a Japanese organization contract. Confirm duties, specialist decisions, education or experience, remuneration, and mixed implementation or routine production. Independent fine-art creation or genuine company operation raises different comparisons.

### Illustrator, writer, composer, or fine artist

Examine Artist when the principal activity is income-producing artistic creation. Confirm the practice, commissions or plan, professional achievement, expected income, and boundary from commercial entertainment production.

### Actor, musician, singer, dancer, model, or professional athlete

Examine Entertainer. Identify the current activity category, contract chain, schedule, venues, organization, remuneration, and applicant experience. A recording or appearance can involve different evidence from another performance category.

### Film, broadcast, advertising, or commercial-video worker

Examine Entertainer's commercial production provisions alongside any specialist employment route. Record whether the person performs, directs, shoots, records, edits, produces, designs, or manages; the production and client; and the contractual organization.

### Photographer

Do not route from the title alone.

- Fine-art practice can point to Artist.
- Foreign-news photojournalism can point to Journalist.
- Commercial photography is expressly addressed in Entertainer materials.
- In-house product, studio, or communications work can require an employed specialist analysis.

Wedding, event, portrait, and independent client photography need a fact-specific review of activity, contracts, clients, income model, and the existing status.

### Reporter, correspondent, or photojournalist

Examine Journalist when journalistic work is performed under the required contract with a foreign news organization. Confirm the outlet, agreement, assignments, editorial use, duration, and remuneration.

### Game, animation, VFX, or multimedia worker

Break the role into art, design, programming, production, performance, recording, marketing, and management duties. A studio's industry does not place every worker under the same status.

### YouTuber, streamer, influencer, or independent content creator

There is no reliable title-only route. Identify platform income, sponsorships, advertising, client production, performance, journalism, artistic creation, foreign and Japanese counterparties, work locations, and business operation. Also analyze whether the person already holds a status that authorizes the activity.

## Mixed work and several clients

Create a duty and income inventory rather than choosing the most attractive label. For each activity, record the output and approximate percentage of working time; client, employer, publisher, outlet, venue, or platform; contract type, location, and payment source; whether the activity is principal, incidental, or occasional; and the route that appears to cover it and why.

Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted ([資格外活動許可](/glossary/shikakugai-katsudo-kyoka), **shikakugai katsudō kyoka**) can be relevant to additional activity, but it should not be treated as a way to replace the person's principal status with a different full-time practice.

## Evidence before route selection

Gather the full contracts, organization information, work locations, detailed duties, schedule, remuneration, education and experience, portfolio or publication record, confirmed projects, and projected income. Then compare the official status definitions and current procedure-specific checklists.

A portfolio answers “what has this person made?” It does not by itself answer “what activity will this person perform in Japan, under which legal relationship, and how will the stay be supported?”

## Questions that prevent common mistakes

1. Is the work employment, freelance contracting, independent creation, performance, reporting, or management?
2. Which organization or clients are in Japan, and which are overseas?
3. What will the person produce and where will each activity occur?
4. Is income salary, appearance fee, commission, royalty, advertising, sponsorship, platform revenue, or business profit?
5. Do film, commercial photography, advertising, broadcast, or recording provisions apply?
6. Does reporting have the required foreign-news-organization contract?
7. Is unpaid study being confused with paid creation?
8. Does the plan contain several activities that require separate authorization analysis?
