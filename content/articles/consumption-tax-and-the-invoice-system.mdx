> **Editorial review needed:** Consumption-tax status can change through sales, payroll, elections, entity facts, or invoice registration. Thresholds and transitional measures must be checked for the exact taxable period before registering or cancelling.

## Treat consumption tax as a separate system

Consumption Tax ([消費税](/glossary/shohi-zei), **shōhizei**) is not calculated by adding a line to the individual income-tax return. A sole proprietor's taxable period is generally the calendar year, but taxable-person status can depend on earlier periods and elections.

The general NTA framework looks at taxable sales exceeding ¥10 million in the base period, ordinarily the second preceding year for a sole proprietor. A specified-period test can also use the first six months of the preceding year and, under its conditions, salary and related payments instead of taxable sales.

Those are not the only triggers. A voluntary taxable-business election, qualified-invoice registration, entity formation, inheritance, or another special rule can change the result. Determine status for each period rather than assuming that a new or small business is automatically exempt.

## Understand invoice registration before agreeing to it

Under the invoice system ([インボイス制度](/glossary/inboisu-seido), **inboisu seido**), only a registered qualified invoice issuer ([適格請求書発行事業者](/glossary/tekikaku-seikyusho-hakko-jigyosha), **tekikaku seikyūsho hakkō jigyōsha**) can issue qualified invoices used for the purchaser's consumption-tax credit under the system.

Registration is a tax decision, not just a formatting request from a client. A registered issuer generally becomes a taxable business required to file consumption-tax returns while registration is effective, even when base-period sales are below the ordinary threshold. Registration information is published by the NTA; for a sole proprietor, defined public information generally includes the name, registration number, and registration date.

Before registering, model prices, whether amounts are tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive, customer needs, recordkeeping, input-tax-credit evidence, cash flow, filing cost, transitional relief, and the effect of later cancellation.

## Issue and keep the required records

When a registered issuer is required to issue a qualified invoice, the document and retained copy must contain the required information. Purchasers also face record-retention conditions for input credits. Ordinary invoices, receipts, credit notes, refunds, platform statements, and mixed taxable or exempt transactions can require coordinated records.

Do not copy another business's registration number or assume a platform issued the invoice on your behalf. Verify responsibility in the contract and the NTA registration publication.

## Cancellation is not always immediate tax exemption

Cancelling qualified-invoice registration does not necessarily restore exempt-business status immediately. Current transitional rules can preserve taxable-person treatment for later periods. Elections and simplified-tax choices can also have timing and minimum-duration effects.

Consult the current NTA period-specific guide or a licensed tax accountant before registering, choosing taxable status, selecting a calculation method, cancelling, closing the business, or leaving Japan. The income-tax, resident-tax, and immigration consequences remain separate.
