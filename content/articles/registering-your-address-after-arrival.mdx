## Report where you live

After deciding your residence, a mid- to long-term resident must notify the municipal office for that address within 14 days. This is commonly called a moving-in notification ([転入届](/glossary/tennyu-todoke), **tennyū todoke**). If the distinction between a prefecture, municipality, or ward is unfamiliar, use the [Japan geography and local-government overview](/articles/understanding-japan-geography-government-and-language) before choosing an office. Bring your Residence Card ([在留カード](/glossary/zairyu-card), **zairyū kādo**). If your passport says the residence card will be issued later, bring that passport instead. Ask the municipality about any additional documents, especially for family members.

This does not apply identically to every person holding a Japanese visa. Temporary Visitors, people granted three months or less, and people under Diplomat or Official status are outside the ordinary mid- to long-term resident category. They should follow the instructions of the responsible authority or protocol office instead of assuming this municipal process applies.

The office completes Resident Registration ([住民登録](/glossary/jumin-toroku), **jūmin tōroku**) in the Basic Resident Register and updates the address on the residence card when applicable. A Certificate of Residence ([住民票](/glossary/juminhyo), **jūminhyō**) is a municipal record or issued copy used for some later procedures; ask whether you actually need one and which details it must show before paying for a copy. Check the registration result before leaving. Your school may also require updated information; follow its secure submission process.

Moving-in notification and issuance of a certificate are different services. Certificate fees are set locally and can differ by record type and by counter, mail, or participating convenience-store channel. Check the municipality's current schedule and request only the fields the receiving organization actually needs; there is no single national price for every municipal certificate.

## Use the municipal visit well

Procedures for National Health Insurance, National Pension, and other local services may be handled during the same visit or at another counter. Depending on the municipality, the destination may be called City Hall ([市役所](/glossary/shiyakusho), **shiyakusho**) or a Ward Office ([区役所](/glossary/kuyakusho), **kuyakusho**). Counter locations, forms, translations, appointments, and accepted supporting records vary, so consult the municipality’s official site.

> **Official requirement:** The 14-day period runs from establishing your residence, not simply from the day you entered Japan.

Your first address must be a place where you actually reside. Before relying on a hotel, short booking, or other temporary accommodation, ask the municipality whether it is registrable and confirm that you can receive official mail there. This matters because later procedures—including My Number notification and many private contracts—use the registered address.

Keep any receipt or guidance the office provides. When moving to another municipality, a Moving-out Notification ([転出届](/glossary/tenshutsu-todoke), **tenshutsu todoke**) is generally part of the sequence; a Change-of-address Notification ([転居届](/glossary/tenkyo-todoke), **tenkyo todoke**) is used for a move within the same municipality. Confirm the timing and submission method with both responsible offices. Updating only the school, landlord, bank, or postal service does not replace the required municipal and immigration-related notifications.
