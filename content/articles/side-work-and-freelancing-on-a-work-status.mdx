> **Draft legal guidance:** Side work is fact-specific. Confirm the proposed duties, contracts, status, and permission with the Immigration Services Agency before beginning paid work. Immigration permission does not override an employment contract, professional-license rule, or tax obligation.

## Start with the activity, not the word “freelance”

Japan authorizes activities through a status of residence. “Freelance,” “side job,” and “second job” describe working arrangements, not immigration categories. Write down the actual services, clients, place of work, contract type, hours, and expected income before asking which rule applies.

For example, a developer employed under Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services ([技術・人文知識・国際業務](/glossary/gijutsu-jinbun-kokusai), **gijutsu, jinbun chishiki, kokusai gyōmu**) might want to build websites for another client. Similar-looking software duties may be within the authorized status, but that is not automatic: the real duties, client or organization, contract, and continuing main employment all matter.

## Three checks determine the next step

1. **Is the activity already within your status?** If it fits the activity authorized by the current work status, Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted may not be the applicable procedure. A new qualifying contract can still trigger an immigration notification.
2. **Is it outside your status?** Income-producing business or paid activity outside the present scope generally requires advance Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted ([資格外活動許可](/glossary/shikakugai-katsudo-kyoka), **shikakugai katsudō kyoka**). Do not start while merely planning or awaiting permission.
3. **Does the new activity become your main activity?** A substantial shift may require a change of status rather than side-work permission. Ask ISA before reorganizing work around the new activity.

The familiar blanket “28 hours per week” rule should not be copied from Student or Dependent guidance to an ordinary sponsored professional. ISA can grant permission for an individually identified activity, and its conditions control.

## What ISA examines

Official criteria include whether the side activity interferes with the principal authorized activity, whether you are actually performing that principal activity, whether the proposed work is eligible and lawful, whether conduct is good, and—where the current status is based on a contract with a Japanese organization—whether that organization consents. Adult-entertainment-related activity is excluded.

Prepare the current residence card and passport, contracts and work descriptions for both roles, proposed schedule, compensation, client or organization details, and any explanation showing the main job will continue. Use the current ISA checklist for the exact application.

## Employer, notification, and tax checks remain separate

Review your employment agreement and work rules for approval, confidentiality, intellectual-property, conflict-of-interest, working-time, and equipment restrictions. If a covered contract ends or a new covered contract begins, a Notification Concerning an Affiliated Organization ([所属機関に関する届出](/glossary/shozoku-kikan-todokede), **shozoku kikan ni kansuru todokede**) may be due within 14 days.

Keep invoices, contracts, expenses, payment records, and any withholding statements from the first day. A freelancer or sole proprietor ([個人事業主](/glossary/kojin-jigyo-nushi), **kojin jigyōnushi**) may need a final income-tax return ([確定申告](/glossary/kakutei-shinkoku), **kakutei shinkoku**) even when the main employer handles year-end adjustment. Tax treatment depends on residence, income category, deductions, withholding, and other facts; ask the tax office or a qualified tax professional.

## Before accepting the project

- obtain a written scope and contract;
- confirm immigration treatment and receive any required permission first;
- obtain any employer consent required by contract or immigration criteria;
- calendar any 14-day notification deadline;
- establish bookkeeping and tax advice; and
- re-check if duties, clients, hours, or the main job change.
