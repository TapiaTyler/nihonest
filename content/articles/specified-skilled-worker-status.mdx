## What the program covers

Specified Skilled Worker ([特定技能](/glossary/tokutei-gino), **tokutei ginō**) authorizes qualifying employment in designated industrial fields under a compliant employment contract. The worker, field, duty category, skills standard, Japanese-language route, employer, remuneration, and—under category (i)—support arrangements must fit together.

As of the official field page dated June 1, 2026, acceptance is available in seventeen fields:

- nursing care;
- building cleaning;
- linen supply;
- industrial-product manufacturing;
- construction;
- shipbuilding and marine industry;
- automobile repair and maintenance;
- aviation;
- accommodation;
- automobile transportation;
- railway;
- agriculture;
- fisheries;
- food and beverage manufacturing;
- food service;
- forestry; and
- wood industry.

Field names and implementation dates can change. A field appearing on a policy announcement does not necessarily mean every new application route, examination, and procedure is already operational. Use the live field page and the field ministry's current guidance.

## Category (i)

Specified Skilled Worker (i) is for work requiring the field's defined level of knowledge or experience. The applicant generally must:

- be at least eighteen years old;
- pass the applicable skills examination;
- meet the applicable Japanese-language standard;
- have a compliant employment contract and remuneration at least equivalent to a Japanese worker doing comparable work;
- work for an accepting organization that meets the general and field-specific requirements; and
- receive the required support under a compliant support plan.

A qualifying completion of Technical Intern Training (ii) can create examination exemptions when the completed occupation and proposed SSW work have the required relationship. It is not an automatic conversion, and the current transition rules must be checked.

Category (i) uses an individually designated period, with each grant not exceeding **three years**. The aggregate stay is generally limited to **five years**, subject to current exceptions and calculation rules. Family accompaniment is generally not available merely from holding category (i), although exceptional family circumstances can require separate guidance.

## Category (ii)

Specified Skilled Worker (ii) requires the higher, proficient-skill standard for a field in which category (ii) is available. Completing category (i) does not automatically produce category (ii); a person may need higher examinations and required work or supervisory experience. A person who independently meets the category (ii) rules does not necessarily have to hold category (i) first.

Available periods are **three years, two years, one year, or six months**. Category (ii) does not use category (i)'s general five-year aggregate ceiling. A qualifying spouse and children may be eligible for Dependent status when the relationship and support requirements are met.

Do not assume all seventeen category (i) fields offer category (ii). Nursing care, for example, is not a category (ii) field because Japan has the separate Nursing Care status for registered Certified Care Workers. Check the current page for the exact field.

## Field duties control

An industry label is not enough. Each field defines its permitted duty categories, principal work, related work, examinations, employment model, and employer conditions. A hotel, restaurant, factory, farm, construction company, airport, or care facility can contain jobs that do and do not fit the field.

Related tasks may be allowed only incidentally where Japanese workers performing the principal work normally do them. An employer cannot use a small amount of covered work to make an unrelated position eligible.

## Specified Skilled Worker is not Skilled Labor

Skilled Labor ([技能](/glossary/gino), **ginō**) is a separate status for nine special industrial-skill categories, such as foreign-cuisine specialists, foreign-specific crafts or construction, pilots, sports instructors, and sommeliers. It ordinarily uses category-specific experience or achievement evidence and does not use the SSW field, examination, accepting-organization, or category (i) support framework.

Similar workplaces can produce different answers. A specialist foreign-cuisine chef and a food-service SSW worker may work in restaurants but perform activity under different legal tests. A Skilled Labor pilot and an SSW aviation worker are not interchangeable. Compare actual duties and the complete route rather than choosing from the English titles.

## Employer and support responsibilities

The accepting organization must satisfy immigration, employment, social-insurance, tax, field, and any council or cooperation requirements. It must use a compliant employment contract and cannot impose prohibited discriminatory treatment or costs.

For category (i), the organization must provide required support itself or properly entrust support to a registered support organization. The plan addresses matters such as pre-entry guidance, arrival and departure assistance, housing and daily-life setup, procedures, Japanese learning, consultation, interaction with Japanese residents, support after involuntary separation, and regular meetings. Outsourcing does not erase the accepting organization's own responsibilities.

Requirements can differ by field, including direct-employment or permitted-dispatch models, staffing limits, licenses, council participation, and operational reporting. Some sending countries also require their own procedures; Japanese immigration permission does not replace them.

## Changing employers or fields

SSW workers can change employment when the new arrangement qualifies, but a new contract does not silently update immigration authorization. ISA's current Q&A directs a worker changing jobs to apply for a change of status. Changing fields or duty categories can also require new examinations or evidence.

Do not begin work for the new employer until the applicable permission and procedure allow it. Employment ending does not necessarily require immediate departure if a lawful new arrangement is pursued within the period of stay, but inactivity, notifications, job-search circumstances, and deadlines need prompt case-specific review.

## Evidence and renewal

Applications commonly require:

- field, duty-category, skills, language, or exemption evidence;
- the employment contract, remuneration comparison, hours, and workplace;
- accepting-organization eligibility and field-specific records;
- the category (i) support plan and support-provider evidence;
- tax, social-insurance, residence, and compliance records; and
- aggregate-stay and prior-program history.

Renewal is not automatic. The worker must continue to fit the field and category, and the employer, contract, support, reporting, and compliance circumstances remain reviewable.

## Questions to resolve before applying

1. Is the field currently accepting applications for the intended category?
2. Which exact duty category and principal tasks apply?
3. Which skills and Japanese-language tests—or valid exemptions—apply?
4. Does the employer meet both general and field-specific conditions?
5. Who will deliver every required category (i) support item?
6. What aggregate category (i) time has already been used?
7. Are sending-country procedures also required?
8. Is Skilled Labor, Nursing Care, Technical Intern Training, or another status actually the closer route?
