> **Editorial review needed:** Language criteria can change by employer, program, profession, and immigration route. Confirm the current advertisement and the responsible authority before relying on a test level.

## There is no universal Japanese level for working in Japan

The useful question is not simply “Do I need N2?” Separate five different layers:

1. **The work:** what must you understand, say, read, and write to perform the actual duties safely and reliably?
2. **The employer:** which abilities are required, preferred, or trainable, and how will the employer assess them?
3. **Immigration or program rules:** does the particular status, designated activity, points route, or program name accepted evidence?
4. **Professional regulation:** does a licence, national examination, or eligibility process impose a separate rule?
5. **The evidence:** what does the certificate or assessment actually measure—and what does it leave untested?

An employer's preference is not automatically immigration law. The reverse is equally important: an employer cannot waive an express legal, licensing, examination, or program condition. Determine the [status of residence](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku) from the real duties and arrangement, then assess language as a separate layer.

## Translate a job advertisement into concrete questions

Words such as *conversational*, *business level*, *fluent*, *native-level*, N2, and N1 are not complete job descriptions. Before accepting the label, ask:

- Is the level required, preferred, advantageous, or only one way to show ability?
- Which language is used in interviews, meetings, specifications, email, customer contact, records, training, safety instructions, and emergencies?
- Does the employer accept an interview, work sample, education record, another test, or demonstrated experience instead of one certificate?
- Which test module, score or level, test date, and verification document are accepted?
- Will the role, client base, worksite, or language environment change after hiring?

A bilingual advertisement can still hide Japanese-language duties in internal records, incident response, regulators, customers, or later assignments. Ask about the complete communication environment, not only the headline.

## Understand what JLPT shows

The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test ([日本語能力試験](/glossary/nihongo-noryoku-shiken), **Nihongo Nōryoku Shiken**, or JLPT) has five levels. N5 is the entry level and N1 is the most advanced. Use the official level descriptions rather than converting each level into a one-word label such as “beginner,” “business,” or “fluent.”

JLPT assesses language knowledge, reading, and listening. It does **not** currently test conversation, speaking, composition, or writing. A high result can be valuable evidence, but it does not by itself show that someone can lead a meeting, write a client proposal, explain a safety issue, negotiate, teach a class, or use specialist vocabulary.

Score reports for tests from December 2025 can include a CEFR reference indication under the official scoring rules. That reference does not add productive or interactive skills to the test. The certificate itself does not expire, but an employer, school, or authority receiving it can impose its own recency policy. Ask whether a specific test date is required.

## JFT-Basic answers a different question

The Japan Foundation Test for Basic Japanese ([国際交流基金日本語基礎テスト](/glossary/jft-basic), **Kokusai Kōryū Kikin Nihongo Kiso Tesuto**, or JFT-Basic) focuses on Japanese needed for communication in everyday situations by people mainly coming to Japan for work. Its sections cover script and vocabulary, conversation and expression, listening, and reading.

That makes it useful in named routes, but not evidence of advanced office, technical, clinical, or management communication. For Specified Skilled Worker (i), the general route requires an accepted Japanese test and the relevant field skills test unless an applicable exemption applies. Current rules include higher Japanese thresholds for taxi or bus driving and train-operation work, and an additional nursing-care Japanese assessment for nursing-care work. Check the current field instructions instead of assuming one threshold applies to every SSW occupation.

## BJT focuses on business settings

The Business Japanese Proficiency Test ([ビジネス日本語能力テスト](/glossary/bijinesu-nihongo-noryoku-tesuto), **Bijinesu Nihongo Nōryoku Tesuto**, or BJT) measures Japanese communication proficiency in business settings. It reports a score from 0 to 800 and a level from J5 to J1+. The test includes listening, combined listening and reading, and reading.

Some immigration frameworks expressly recognize named BJT scores. For example, current Highly Skilled Professional points materials recognize specified JLPT levels or BJT scores. A score can contribute evidence or points; it does not establish that the proposed duties fit the status, nor does it replace an employer's assessment of speaking, writing, negotiation, technical vocabulary, or customer work.

## Know the narrow rules that can override a general answer

Do not generalize these examples to every job:

- **Specified Skilled Worker:** the accepted language test, field test, exception, and exemption depend on the current field route.
- **Foreign-trained nursing:** MHLW's examination-eligibility process identifies JLPT N1 in stated education circumstances. Eligibility recognition, the Japanese national examination, licensing, employment, and residence status remain separate steps.
- **Designated Activities No. 46:** the qualifying Japanese-university-graduate route has its own advanced-Japanese evidence. That rule does not apply to every graduate job or every Designated Activities category.
- **Highly Skilled Professional:** current points tables can award points for named JLPT levels or BJT scores, but points do not replace the underlying authorized professional activity.
- **Business Manager:** the current Japanese-capacity criterion must be read in the full revised framework. Do not assume it always means an applicant personally holding a JLPT certificate.
- **Instructor:** foreign-language teaching evidence can involve twelve or more years of education through the designated language in the applicable circumstances. That is not a universal citizenship, “native speaker,” JLPT, TEFL, or teaching-licence rule.

Follow the focused status or profession guide for the full requirements.

## Judge language needs from the tasks

Two employers using the same title can reasonably need different communication. Review the actual work:

- **Software and engineering:** specifications, stand-ups, code review, tickets, incident response, documentation, client meetings, and safety rules.
- **Graphic design, film, photography, and digital media:** briefs, critique, revisions, production handoffs, rights and releases, contracts, clients, and on-set safety.
- **Research and academia:** lab or department communication, papers, teaching, grants, ethics review, administration, and conferences.
- **Office, sales, and consulting:** email, meetings, proposals, negotiation, reporting, regulations, and customer relationships.
- **Hospitality and customer service:** live requests, complaints, payments, accessibility, emergencies, and shift handoffs.
- **Skilled and safety-sensitive work:** instructions, hazards, equipment, records, supervisors, and field assessments.
- **Medical and care work:** licensing, examinations, patient communication, consent, records, emergencies, and safety.
- **Teaching:** institution type, designated language, learner communication, lesson duties, program rules, colleagues, parents, and Japanese-language administration.

Nihonest does not assign an unofficial N-level to these tasks. Use them to ask what the employer needs and how it will be assessed.

## Treat English requirements with the same care

“Native English,” “native-level,” “fluent,” and “business English” can describe different employer expectations. Ask whether the employer means speaking, writing, pronunciation, education through English, teaching experience, a teaching qualification, or a particular test—and whether it is required or preferred.

Teaching routes also differ. The JET Programme's current criteria are program-specific: previous Japanese ability is not a universal ALT eligibility requirement, while CIR applicants need functional Japanese described by the program at approximately JLPT N1 or N2. Those criteria do not govern every direct-hire teacher, dispatched ALT, eikaiwa instructor, international-school teacher, or university lecturer.

If an employer requests English test evidence, check the exact module. TOEIC Speaking and Writing is a separate assessment from TOEIC Listening and Reading; one score label should not be used to imply skills that were not tested.

## For employers: define evidence after defining the work

MHLW's fair-recruitment guidance centers selection on the aptitude and ability needed for the job. A practical language process should:

1. list the communication tasks and consequences of misunderstanding;
2. distinguish required, preferred, and trainable abilities;
3. assess the skills the role actually uses;
4. state accepted alternatives and certificate-recency policies; and
5. avoid using nationality or unrelated personal background as a substitute for language competence.

This does not decide whether an individual advertisement is lawful. Employers and applicants should use official employment support or qualified advice for a specific dispute.

## Applicant checklist

- Identify the real duties, employer, worksite, customers, team, and future assignments.
- Separate hiring preferences from immigration, program, examination, and licence rules.
- Match every certificate to the abilities it actually tests.
- Ask how speaking, writing, field vocabulary, and job performance will be evaluated.
- Confirm accepted documents, scores, levels, test dates, and alternatives in writing.
- Recheck the current authority for any route with a named language threshold.
- Keep language evidence with the job description and immigration or licensing records it supports.
