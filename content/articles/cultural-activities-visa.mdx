## What Cultural Activities covers

Cultural Activities ([文化活動](/glossary/bunka-katsudo), **bunka katsudō**) covers qualifying academic or artistic activity that produces no income, specialized research into culture or skills particular to Japan, or acquiring those skills under expert guidance. It excludes activity assigned to Student or Training.

Official examples include fields such as flower arrangement, tea ceremony, judo, Japanese architecture, painting, dance, cuisine, and traditional music. The legal fit comes from the proposed study or research—not simply from calling an activity traditional, cultural, artistic, or personally meaningful.

## Two common evidence paths

### Independent research or practice

For academic or artistic activity not centered on one instructor, explain the subject, method, locations, schedule, duration, expected output, and why the activity requires residence in Japan. Show the applicant's education, publications, achievements, or prior experience and identify any host, institution, facility, archive, or professional connection.

### Study under an expert

For learning a culture or skill particular to Japan, document the instructor's identity, standing and experience; the relationship with the applicant; the curriculum and schedule; the place of instruction; fees; and the applicant's relevant background. Occasional private lessons or tourism with hobby activities should not be assumed to support long-term Cultural Activities residence.

## Funding without unauthorized work

The principal activity is non-remunerative. The application should show how tuition or instruction, housing, insurance, travel, and ordinary living expenses will be paid for the requested period. Funding may involve personal savings or documented support from another person, but the records should make the source and availability credible.

Do not build the budget around an assumed part-time job. Permission to engage in an activity outside the status ([資格外活動許可](/glossary/shikakugai-katsudo-kyoka), **shikakugai katsudō kyoka**) is a separate, discretionary permission. It must be obtained before covered paid activity begins and must not displace the authorized cultural activity. Remote work, online commissions, performances, sales, and freelance income still require analysis.

## Choosing the neighboring route

- Use **Student** when the principal activity is education at an institution covered by that status.
- Examine **Training** for a qualifying non-employment skills-acquisition program through a receiving organization.
- Examine **Artist**, **Entertainer**, **Engineer/Specialist**, or another work route when the principal activity produces income or is performed under a work relationship.
- A genuinely short workshop, private lesson, research visit, or cultural visit may instead use an eligible short-stay route, subject to nationality and activity rules.

The same subject can appear under different routes. Studying cuisine without income, attending culinary school, working as a foreign-cuisine chef, and operating a restaurant are not one immigration activity.

## Application sequence and evidence

A host, instructor, or eligible representative in Japan commonly supports a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) application. Evidence can include:

- the activity or research plan and requested duration;
- instructor or host credentials and relationship;
- applicant background and achievements;
- schedule, location, fees, and facilities;
- financial-support and living-expense records; and
- translations of foreign-language documents where required.

After a COE is issued, the applicant uses the current checklist of the Japanese embassy or consulate with jurisdiction over their residence. A COE is not a visa and does not guarantee visa issuance or landing permission.

## Period, renewal, and changes

Available periods are **three years, one year, six months, or three months**. Immigration determines the granted period.

Renewal is possible when the qualifying unpaid activity continues and the applicant can document progress, attendance or practice, supervision where relevant, finances, and residence compliance. Keep a practical record of lessons, research, output, meetings, fees, and funding rather than trying to reconstruct it at renewal.

A material change in instructor, host, location, funding, research subject, schedule, or income plan may affect the route. Confirm the immigration procedure before changing the principal activity, beginning paid work, or moving into employment or formal school enrollment.
