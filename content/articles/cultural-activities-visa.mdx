## What this route covers

Cultural Activities ([文化活動](/glossary/bunka-katsudo), **bunka katsudō**) is a status of residence for qualifying academic or artistic activity that does not produce income, or for specialized study of culture or skills particular to Japan. Official examples include research into Japanese culture and learning a traditional art under an expert. It is distinct from Student, Training, Temporary Visitor, and income-producing professional activity.

The planned activity—not personal interest alone—must fit the legal category. A credible application normally needs a specific program: what will be studied or researched, where it will happen, who will supervise it, how long it will last, and how living expenses will be funded without relying on unauthorized work.

## Official requirements at a glance

- The main activity must be unpaid academic or artistic activity, specialized research into culture or skills particular to Japan, or learning those skills under expert guidance.
- The activity must not instead fall under Student or Training status.
- The applicant must show the proposed activity and duration, relevant background, the instructor or host where applicable, and the ability to meet living expenses without unauthorized income.
- MOFA lists a passport, visa application form, photograph, and COE as the core overseas visa documents; the responsible embassy or consulate may require more.
- The available periods of stay are **three years, one year, six months, or three months**. Immigration selects the period; the applicant does not simply choose it.

An electronic COE may be presented in the form accepted by the mission. Without a COE, a visa application may still be possible, but MOFA warns that extensive verification documents and several months of processing may be required.

## Prepare the application path

A host, instructor, or other proxy in Japan commonly supports a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) application. After a COE is issued, the applicant follows the current visa checklist of the Japanese embassy or consulate with jurisdiction over their residence. MOFA lists a passport, application form, photograph, and COE as the core visa documents, while nationality and local circumstances may add requirements.

> **Official distinction:** A COE is not a visa and does not guarantee visa issuance or landing permission. The status and period of stay are decided during immigration procedures.

## Work and changes of activity

Cultural Activities is a non-working status. Do not accept pay, operate an income-producing activity, or assume that remote or freelance work falls outside Japanese rules. Permission for an activity outside the status may be possible in limited circumstances, but it must be confirmed and granted before the activity begins.

Keep records of the program, supervision, attendance or progress, funding, and address. If the activity, host, funding, or duration changes materially, consult the Immigration Services Agency before acting. A short workshop or private visit may belong to another route; paid cultural work may require a work-authorized status.
