> **For employers:** This is an operational orientation. Use the current checklist for the exact status, application, and organization category, and obtain qualified advice where the activity or representation rules are uncertain.

## Sponsorship is several different responsibilities

An employer may design the position, make an offer, prepare organization evidence, help assemble an application, and—in an eligible case—have an authorized person act as the applicant's representative or application intermediary. The Immigration Services Agency issues the Certificate of Eligibility ([在留資格認定証明書](/glossary/certificate-of-eligibility), **zairyū shikaku nintei shōmeisho**).

The employer does not issue or guarantee the COE. A diplomatic mission separately decides the visa application, an immigration inspector decides landing, and ISA separately decides later changes and extensions. Use precise language such as “the company will support the COE application” instead of promising that the company will “give” the worker a visa.

## Define the real job before making immigration promises

Start with the permanent role rather than a convenient title. Record:

- each substantial duty and approximate share of working time;
- the knowledge, skill, license, cultural background, or experience the duty requires;
- the employer, contracting entity, worksite, reporting line, clients, and any dispatch arrangement;
- remuneration, hours, duration, and whether the assignment is temporary;
- onboarding or practical training that differs from the permanent work; and
- side duties, travel, remote work, or assignments for related and unrelated organizations.

Compare those facts with the relevant Status of Residence ([在留資格](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku), **zairyū shikaku**). Do not rename routine service, production, manual, driving, care, or administrative work to resemble a professional category. Likewise, do not reject a valid route merely because the company's English title differs from an official example.

The worker's degree, experience, examination, license, prior service, remuneration, and organization conditions can matter differently by route. A bachelor's degree is not one universal rule, and an impressive qualification does not authorize duties outside the status.

## Recruit and contract lawfully

Use lawful recruitment channels and fair selection. Before employment starts, confirm through the appropriate residence card ([在留カード](/glossary/zairyu-card), **zairyū kādo**), passport, designation document, or other current record that the person may perform the proposed work and that the period has not expired. Temporary Visitor, Student, Dependent, Designated Activities, activity-based work, and unrestricted status-based routes require different checks.

A Certificate of Authorized Employment can clarify what paid activity is authorized in a particular case, but it is not a universal prerequisite for hiring. When the answer remains unclear, pause the assignment and ask ISA or a qualified professional rather than infer permission from a prior employer or residence-status nickname.

Give the worker written employment conditions covering wages, hours, workplace, duties, term, renewal, deductions, leave, and separation under the applicable labor rules. MHLW guidance calls for explanations the worker can understand and prohibits discriminatory treatment in working conditions based on nationality. Do not retain the worker's passport or residence card as company property.

## Divide the COE packet by responsibility

Use the current ISA page for the selected status, filing type, and organization category. Maintain a dated checklist separating:

- applicant identity, education, experience, license, and history records;
- employer registration, finances, business, staffing, and tax records;
- the job description, contract, remuneration, worksite, and route-specific explanation;
- corporate-relationship or transfer records where applicable;
- translations, issue-date limits, originals, and records that may be requested later; and
- facts that changed after preparation began.

The applicant remains responsible for the application made about them. Give the worker an opportunity to review the complete factual submission and keep an agreed copy. Resolve inconsistent names, dates, qualifications, duties, salary, employer entities, and work locations; do not coach an applicant to conceal or invent facts.

An accepting organization's employee may qualify as a COE representative, and approved staff may use application-intermediary or online procedures under stated conditions. That does not authorize every employee, recruiter, consultant, or unlicensed paid service to represent applicants. Confirm the current authority before assigning submission work.

## Plan for visa, travel, and changed facts

After COE issuance, tell the worker which records the company will transmit, what remains for the consular visa application, and which originals or copies should travel in carry-on baggage. Do not book around an assumed visa or landing result without a contingency plan.

Create an escalation route for delayed issuance, additional-document requests, visa refusal, changed start date, changed salary or duties, different worksite, company restructuring, inability to travel, and withdrawal of the offer. A COE based on facts that are no longer true should not simply be reused without authoritative review.

## Onboard the person as both a worker and a new resident

Before the first day, identify a responsible HR contact and provide understandable information about payroll, tax withholding, employment and social insurance, pension and health-insurance enrollment, working time, leave, workplace safety, harassment reporting, emergencies, housing boundaries, and language support.

Help the worker understand which steps belong to the employer and which belong to the resident. Address registration, My Number, personal immigration notifications, re-entry, family procedures, and many municipal actions remain the resident's own legal processes even when HR provides practical support.

Check the granted status, period, work restrictions, and any designation against the planned role. Do not alter government records or assume an apparent mismatch is harmless.

## File the employer's applicable reports

MHLW generally requires employers to report covered foreign-worker hiring and separation information through Hello Work, with the form and deadline depending in part on employment-insurance coverage. Diplomatic and Official status holders and Special Permanent Residents are outside that particular reporting population.

ISA also has an affiliated-organization notification framework for the start and end of acceptance under listed work statuses. Current ISA guidance explains that an organization required to file the MHLW foreign-worker employment notification does not duplicate the corresponding organization-side filing with ISA. Determine which rule applies to the employer's facts rather than filing or omitting both by assumption.

The resident may separately owe a Notification Concerning an Affiliated Organization ([所属機関に関する届出](/glossary/shozoku-kikan-todokede), **shozoku kikan ni kansuru todokede**) after a covered contract or organization event. The employer's filing does not complete the resident's notification, and either notification does not establish that changed duties fit the status.

## Manage changes before they become compliance failures

Reassess immigration and employment consequences before changing duties, employer entity, worksite, dispatch structure, hours, remuneration, assignment period, or contract. Coordinate renewals early enough to produce current company records, but never promise approval.

For resignation, dismissal, transfer, merger, long absence, or company closure, document the effective facts; give the worker required employment records and belongings; complete employer reports; explain known immigration touchpoints without pretending to act as the worker's lawyer; and preserve an appropriate record of what was filed.

Program-specific regimes can add much more. Specified Skilled Worker, Technical Intern Training and its successor arrangements, regulated professions, dispatched work, and some accepting organizations require their own current compliance workflow. This general guide is the starting layer, not a replacement for those rules.

For workforce programs, identify the legal role of every organization instead of using sponsor as a catch-all. A Specified Skilled Worker employer and registered support organization, a Technical Intern Training implementing and supervising organization, and an Employment for Skill Development implementing and supervising support organization have different approval, plan, support, supervision, protection, reporting, and record duties. Start with the [training and workforce route comparison](/articles/choosing-a-training-or-workforce-development-route-in-japan), then use the selected program's current operating guidance.

> **Operational principle:** The employer provides a truthful role, lawful employment, accurate evidence, understandable onboarding, and required reports. Government authorities decide immigration permission.
