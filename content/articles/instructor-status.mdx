## Start with the school, not the title

Instructor ([教育](/glossary/kyoiku), **kyōiku**) covers language education and other education at specified institutions. These include elementary, junior-high and high schools, compulsory and secondary schools, special-needs schools, professional-training schools, miscellaneous-category schools, and institutions comparable in facilities and curriculum.

Common roles to examine include English or other foreign-language teachers, school subject teachers, vocational-school instructors, and Assistant Language Teachers ([外国語指導助手](/glossary/gaikokugo-shido-joshu), **gaikokugo shidō joshu**), usually called ALTs. These are navigation examples, not automatically approved occupations.

## Compare nearby teaching routes

- University teaching, research, or research guidance normally points first to **Professor**.
- Language instruction for a private company, including some eikaiwa employment, can point to **Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services**.
- Preschool, kindergarten, international-school, cram-school, after-school, tutoring, online, and employer-training roles require an institution-and-duty check. Their English names do not establish an immigration category.
- Freelance lessons, multiple employers, and teaching outside the current status require a separate contract and outside-activity review.

Use [Which residence status fits teaching in Japan?](/articles/choosing-a-residence-status-for-teaching-in-japan) before relying on this route.

## Qualification paths to document

Depending on the institution and activity, the current criteria use evidence such as:

- university graduation or equivalent education;
- a qualifying Japanese professional-training credential related to the education;
- a license needed for the intended education;
- for foreign-language education, at least twelve years of education conducted in that language;
- for another subject, at least five years of experience teaching that subject; and
- remuneration equivalent to that paid to a Japanese worker for comparable work.

An exception modifies part of this analysis for certain schools established to educate children of people holding specified statuses through a foreign-language elementary or secondary curriculum. Do not generalize it to every international school.

## Direct hire, dispatch, and multiple schools

ALT does not identify the employer. Record the legal employer, any dispatch or service company, every worksite, who directs the work, all teaching and non-teaching duties, and each contract. Current application materials contemplate both employment and other contracts and request every contract where several institutions are involved.

## Evidence, period, and renewal

Use the current checklist for the actual application and organization category. Common evidence covers the institution's classification, subjects and timetable, written working conditions or service contracts, education or experience, remuneration, and organization records. Foreign-language documents generally need Japanese translations, and ISA may request additional evidence.

Available periods are **five years, three years, one year, or three months**. The individual period granted is not guaranteed. Extension is possible but discretionary and can examine continuing teaching, contracts, remuneration, notifications, tax and other compliance, and changed circumstances. Moving from a school to a university or private company may require a change of status.

Instructor does not authorize every paid role. Private lessons, freelance work, unrelated employment, or another teaching category may require Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted ([資格外活動許可](/glossary/shikakugai-katsudo-kyoka), **shikakugai katsudō kyoka**) or a different status.
