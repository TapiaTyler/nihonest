> **Resident-focused step:** This process is based on living in Japan and resident registration. A person studying as a Temporary Visitor does not enroll through this ordinary resident procedure.

## Enrollment is separate from payment relief

People living in Japan from age 20 through 59 are generally covered by National Pension ([国民年金](/glossary/kokumin-nenkin), **kokumin nenkin**) regardless of nationality. A student who is not covered through an employer normally completes Category I enrollment through the municipality after resident registration.

The trigger is residence and age, not the informal name of the visa route. Employer coverage, international social-security agreements, and special residence arrangements can affect the correct procedure. Confirm your case with the municipal pension counter or Japan Pension Service.

Students whose prior-year income is below the applicable threshold may apply for the Student Special Payment System ([学生納付特例制度](/glossary/gakusei-nofu-tokurei-seido), **gakusei nōfu tokurei seido**). Approval postpones contributions for the approved period; it is not the same as failing to pay. Applications generally need to be made for each relevant year, and the school must qualify for the program.

Approved periods can count toward eligibility periods, but do not increase the pension amount unless contributions are later paid under the repayment rules. Other exemption or postponement systems may apply when the student system does not.

> **Official requirement:** Do not discard contribution notices or assume student status creates an automatic exemption. Ask the municipal pension counter or Japan Pension Service which application fits your circumstances.

Keep enrollment, application, approval, and payment records. If you start qualifying employment, move, leave Japan, or change schools, confirm whether a new notification is required.
