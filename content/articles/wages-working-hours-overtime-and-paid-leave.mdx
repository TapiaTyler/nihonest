> **Editorial review needed:** Working-time systems, exemptions, wage components, and leave eligibility are fact-specific. Minimum wages and administrative materials change; verify the current prefectural and industry rates.

## Build your own pay-and-time record

Keep the written working conditions, workplace rules, schedules, clock records, computer or access logs, instructions, payslips, bank deposits, leave requests, and employer responses. Record actual start and finish times and work performed before clock-in, after clock-out, at home, during travel, or while supposedly on break.

This record helps distinguish scheduled time, actual Working Hours ([労働時間](/glossary/rodo-jikan), **rōdō jikan**), Overtime ([残業](/glossary/zangyo), **zangyō**), night work, statutory-holiday work, ordinary days off, and leave. Those categories can produce different calculations.

## Check the wage calculation, not only the deposit

Wages generally must be paid in currency, directly to the worker, in full, at least monthly, and on a fixed date, subject to lawful exceptions and deductions. Compare each payslip with:

- base pay and the unit used to calculate it;
- actual hours, overtime, night work, and holiday work;
- allowances and which are included in premium calculations;
- taxes, insurance, housing, meals, or other deductions;
- absence, late-arrival, or leave treatment; and
- the net bank deposit.

A monthly or annual salary does not automatically include unlimited overtime. Where a fixed-overtime arrangement is claimed, the ordinary-wage portion, premium portion, covered hours, and payment for excess time require careful review. A “manager” title likewise does not by itself prove that a statutory exemption applies.

## Minimum wage depends on place and sometimes industry

Minimum Wage ([最低賃金](/glossary/saitei-chingin), **saitei chingin**) is not one permanent national amount. A regional rate is set for each prefecture, and some industries have a specified minimum wage. When both apply, the higher applicable rate controls. For a dispatched worker, the rate at the place of dispatch is relevant.

Check the current official rate and effective date for the actual workplace. Nationality, student status, probation, part-time scheduling, or an employer's financial difficulty does not by itself permit a wage below the applicable minimum. Some narrowly defined exclusions or permissions can require separate confirmation, so do not infer an exception from a job label.

## Start with the statutory-hours framework

The general foundation is no more than eight hours per day and forty hours per week. The framework contains variable schedules, flex systems, exclusions, and a limited forty-four-hour weekly exception for certain small workplaces in specified sectors. Therefore, compare the actual workplace system and filed agreements rather than assuming every hour beyond a personal schedule is legally identical.

For a work period exceeding six hours, the ordinary minimum break is forty-five minutes; when it exceeds eight hours, it is one hour. The general days-off rule is at least one day each week or four days over four weeks. A break must be a real period the worker can use, not time spent answering calls, watching equipment, or remaining under active direction.

## An Article 36 agreement does not make overtime unlimited

An Agreement 36 ([36協定](/glossary/saburoku-kyotei), **saburōku kyōtei**) is the labor-management agreement and filing used for covered overtime or statutory-day-off work. Its existence does not erase upper limits, premium wages, health protections, accurate records, or the need for overtime instructions to have a valid basis in the contract or rules.

The ordinary overtime ceiling is generally forty-five hours per month and 360 hours per year, with a separate exceptional-circumstances framework and sector-specific or transitional treatment. Do not use those numbers as a personal calculation without checking the applicable system.

## Identify each premium separately

Premium Wages ([割増賃金](/glossary/warimashi-chingin), **warimashi chingin**) ordinarily include at least:

- 25 percent for statutory overtime;
- 25 percent for work from 10 p.m. to 5 a.m.;
- 35 percent for work on a statutory day off; and
- 50 percent for monthly overtime exceeding sixty hours.

Categories can overlap, and the correct base wage and exclusions matter. A replacement day off and a substitute holiday are not always treated identically. Even overtime performed without a valid Agreement 36 is not made unpaid merely because the employer failed to follow the required procedure.

## Annual paid leave is not only for permanent employees

Annual Paid Leave ([年次有給休暇](/glossary/nenji-yukyu-kyuka), **nenji yūkyū kyūka**) generally begins after six months of continuous employment when attendance reaches at least eighty percent of working days. A worker scheduled at least five days or thirty hours weekly ordinarily begins with ten days; qualifying workers with fewer scheduled days can receive a proportional entitlement.

Workers ordinarily identify when they wish to take leave. An employer can change the timing when granting the requested dates would interfere with normal operations, but that is not a blanket power to refuse leave. Employers must ensure five days are taken within a year for workers granted at least ten days, while preserving the worker's requested days and other lawful scheduling methods. Leave rights ordinarily expire after two years if unused.

## If the records do not match

1. Ask payroll or HR for the calculation and applicable workplace rule in writing.
2. Preserve the original records before employer-controlled access ends.
3. Identify whether the issue concerns minimum wage, unpaid ordinary wages, overtime premiums, deductions, breaks, days off, or leave.
4. Do not alter records or secretly expose unrelated personal information.
5. Contact the responsible Labour Standards Inspection Office, Labour Bureau consultation service, union, or qualified professional when the issue is not corrected.
