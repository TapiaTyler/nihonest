> **Editorial review needed:** Covered events and statuses must be checked on the current ISA page. This guide does not decide whether a new role fits the current status.

## One job change can create three separate questions

1. **Must the resident notify ISA?** Some activity-based statuses require a notification after specified changes involving an affiliated or contracting organization.
2. **Must the employer report the hire or separation?** The employer may have a separate Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare reporting duty.
3. **Does the new work fit the current status?** Neither notification answers this question.

Complete each applicable task independently. One party's filing does not replace the other's.

## The resident's immigration notification

For covered statuses and events, a Notification Concerning an Affiliated Organization ([所属機関に関する届出](/glossary/shozoku-kikan-todokede), *shozoku kikan ni kansuru todokede*) is generally due within 14 days after the event. Depending on the status, relevant events can include:

- an organization changing its name or location;
- an organization ceasing to exist;
- leaving an organization or ending a contract; and
- joining a new organization or beginning a new contract.

The precise notification type differs among activity organization, contracting organization, and spouse-related events. Check the current ISA list for your status and event. ISA does not accept a future date as though the change already occurred; notify after the event and preserve proof of submission. If late, file promptly and follow current instructions rather than deciding the duty has disappeared.

## The employer's separate report

An employer generally reports specified foreign-worker hiring and separation information through the employment-insurance or other applicable Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) process. The form and deadline depend on whether the worker is covered by employment insurance and on the employer's circumstances.

Three responsibilities must not be confused:

### Your resident notification to ISA

If your status and the organization or contract change are covered, you file your own notification with ISA. This records that the change happened. It does **not** establish that the duties at the new job fit your current status.

### The employer's notification to MHLW

The employer files the applicable foreign-worker hiring or separation report. Ask which process applies and retain any confirmation made available to you. The employer's report does **not** complete your separate ISA notification.

### The work-authorization check

You and the employer should compare the new primary duties, contract, and organization with your current immigration permission. A notification, signed employment contract, or completed employer report does **not** by itself authorize work outside your current status.

## Notification is not work authorization

Submitting the notice records a change. It does not expand the Status of Residence ([在留資格](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku), *zairyū shikaku*), grant Permission to Engage in Activity Outside the Status Scope ([資格外活動許可](/glossary/shikakugai-katsudo-kyoka), *shikakugai katsudō kyoka*), approve a Change of Status of Residence ([在留資格変更許可](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku-henko-kyoka), *zairyū shikaku henkō kyoka*), or extend the current stay.

Before beginning the new role, compare the actual primary duties, employer or contracting organization, work location, remuneration, and relevant qualifications with the current status. If the work falls outside it, determine which permission or status change must be granted first.

## When a Certificate of Authorized Employment may help

A Certificate of Authorized Employment can provide official evidence about the income-producing activity a person is permitted to perform under existing immigration permission. It is not a mandatory document for every hire and does not change the status itself. Its usefulness is fact-specific, particularly when a new role appears similar but the parties want evidence about work scope.

## Keep a complete changeover file

- old and new contracts and detailed duty descriptions;
- organization names, addresses, registration details, and work locations;
- actual end and start dates;
- remuneration and employment conditions;
- proof of the resident notification;
- the employer's filing confirmation where available;
- advice or application records about status fit; and
- the Residence Card ([在留カード](/glossary/zairyu-card), *zairyū kādo*) expiry date.

If the principal authorized activity stops, keep evidence of active job searching, offers, illness, or another relevant circumstance. Do not assume that an informal “three-month grace period” guarantees the status: inactivity, other activity, justifiable reasons, and revocation are assessed under separate rules.
