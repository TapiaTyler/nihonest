## Japan does not have one unrestricted working visa

Most work-authorized Statuses of Residence ([在留資格](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku), *zairyū shikaku*) are defined around activities, organizations, qualifications, or regulated roles. The title printed on a business card rarely answers the immigration question by itself.

A “teacher” might teach at a university, a covered school, or a private language company. A “developer” might primarily design software, manage projects, perform installation work, or do something outside an expected professional route. A “manager” may manage an existing operation or be trying to establish a new business. The actual primary duties and surrounding facts matter.

## Compare routes in this order

### 1. Describe the real work

Write a duty breakdown using ordinary verbs: design, program, analyze, translate, teach, research, manage, perform, manufacture, repair, care, or provide a regulated service. Include approximate time allocation and distinguish the permanent role from short onboarding or training.

### 2. Identify the organization and contract

Record the Japanese contracting or host organization, workplace, contract duration, remuneration, and whether an overseas related company is involved. Some routes depend on the kind of institution, corporate relationship, designated sector, support arrangement, or Japanese office.

### 3. Map every qualification path

The relevant route may accept a related degree, specified professional experience, a recognized examination, or another alternative. Other routes require a particular Japanese professional license, sector test, language level, points result, or relationship. Do not combine alternatives into one false universal checklist.

### 4. Check boundaries and mixed duties

Compare the proposed work with official positive examples and excluded or adjacent activities. If substantial duties fall outside the likely status, ask whether the job should be restructured, another route fits, or separate permission is required. An occupation example is a navigation aid, not a whitelist.

### 5. Choose the application context

A person abroad commonly uses a Certificate of Eligibility ([在留資格認定証明書](/glossary/certificate-of-eligibility), *zairyū shikaku nintei shōmeisho*) and then applies for a visa at the responsible Japanese mission. A person already in Japan may instead need a Change of Status of Residence ([在留資格変更許可](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku-henko-kyoka), *zairyū shikaku henkō kyoka*). These routes have different forms and evidence.

## Coordinate the COE without outsourcing understanding

An employer or other eligible representative in Japan often prepares the COE application, but the applicant should still review:

- the status requested and activity described;
- the detailed duties, work location, remuneration, and contract period;
- the qualification pathway and supporting records;
- the organization's supporting category and documents;
- translations and consistency of names, dates, and history; and
- the intended entry date relative to the COE's ordinary three-month validity.

The COE does not guarantee visa issuance or landing. Keep a complete copy of the submission and disclose material changes rather than letting later forms contradict the COE record.

## Evidence categories to prepare

- identity and travel records;
- contract, offer, assignment, and detailed duty records;
- degree, transcript, experience, examination, or professional-license evidence;
- organization registration, finances, business, or institutional records where required;
- remuneration and employment-condition evidence; and
- route-specific program, sector, relationship, or support records.

Use the current official checklist for the specific status and organization category. A generic list cannot safely predict every requested document.

## Before accepting or starting the role

Confirm what activity is authorized, whether any professional license is independently required, and whether side duties or freelance work need a separate check. If changing employers while already resident, a notification may be due even where the new work remains within the current status. Notification, status fit, employment law, licensing, and tax are separate questions.
