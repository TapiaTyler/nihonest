## Three things that are easy to confuse

**My Number ([個人番号](/glossary/kojin-bango), kojin bangō)** is the 12-digit number assigned after resident registration. It is not your residence-card number.

My Number follows registration in the Basic Resident Register; it is not issued to every visitor merely because a visa was issued. People outside ordinary resident registration should not assume they will receive one.

The **Individual Number Notice** tells you the number. It is generally sent by registered mail after registration and is not itself an identity document.

The **My Number Card ([マイナンバーカード](/glossary/my-number-card))** is a photo identification card you may apply for. Receiving a number does not mean that the photo card has automatically been ordered.

## What to do

Make sure your name is on the mailbox and watch for registered mail. Official guidance says the notice commonly arrives in roughly two to three weeks, but delivery can vary. If it does not arrive, contact your municipality rather than submitting repeated applications.

Keep the notice securely. Provide the number only where a lawful procedure requires it—for example, certain tax, social-security, employment, or international-remittance processes. Do not post or casually send a photo containing the number.

> **Practical advice:** Decide whether the optional photo card will be useful for identification and online or municipal services, then use only the official application method. A lost notice does not change the number; ask the municipality how to confirm it safely.
