## What Training means

Training ([研修](/glossary/kenshu), **kenshū**) covers qualifying acquisition of skills or knowledge while accepted by a public or private organization in Japan. An official example is a trainee hosted by a local government. It is separate from ordinary employment, Student status, and the Technical Intern Training program.

The arrangement’s substance matters more than its English label. A course called “training,” internship, onboarding program, or professional placement does not automatically qualify. The receiving organization should identify the correct status and document the training plan, duration, supervision, expenses, and relationship with any sending organization.

## Official requirements at a glance

- The activity must be qualifying acquisition of skills or knowledge accepted by a public or private organization in Japan.
- It must not instead fall under Student or Technical Intern Training status, and ordinary productive employment cannot be relabeled as Training.
- The application must establish the curriculum, duration, receiving organization, instructors, facilities, treatment and expenses, and any sending organization.
- MOFA’s core visa documents are a passport, application form, photograph, and COE.
- When a dispatching institution is involved, MOFA also identifies the training dispatch contract and the contract between the applicant and sending organization.
- The available periods of stay are **one year, six months, or three months**.

Applicants for certain local-government, public-institution, or publicly funded training who intend to apply without a COE should consult the embassy or consulate in advance. Nationality-specific and local-mission requirements may add documents.

## COE and visa sequence

A proxy in Japan can apply for a COE. Applications without a COE may require extensive supporting material and take substantially longer.

> **Official distinction:** A training allowance, reimbursement, or other payment does not by itself decide the immigration category. The full arrangement must be assessed under the applicable rules.

## Before accepting a program

Obtain written information about the curriculum, daily schedule, supervisor, location, accommodation, insurance, travel and living costs, payments, complaint contact, and what happens if the placement ends early. Verify whether productive work is expected and whether the proposed status permits it.

After arrival, participate in the authorized training and keep the host informed of required changes. Do not change hosts, take side work, or convert the placement into employment without first confirming and completing the appropriate immigration procedure. If the arrangement resembles employment or technical intern training, seek clarification from the Immigration Services Agency or a qualified professional before traveling.
