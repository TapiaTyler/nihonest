## What Training means

Training ([研修](/glossary/kenshu), **kenshū**) covers qualifying acquisition of skills, techniques, or knowledge while accepted by a public or private organization in Japan. It is a non-employment training route and excludes activity assigned to Student or Technical Intern Training.

An English label does not decide the route. A company induction, internship, job shadow, professional exchange, classroom course, technical placement, or overseas-employee visit can have different immigration treatment depending on productive work, payment, host, curriculum, and purpose.

## Training is not ordinary labor

The receiving organization should be able to explain what the participant will learn, why that knowledge cannot reasonably be obtained through the sending arrangement alone, who will teach it, and how the structured program differs from filling an ordinary position.

Non-practical instruction can include classroom teaching, observation, demonstrations, exercises, and other learning that does not involve producing goods or providing services as part of the host's business. Practical training is more restricted. Where the program includes hands-on productive activity, confirm that a current public, non-commercial, or other specified exception actually applies and that every safeguard is met. Do not relabel employment as Training.

An allowance, expense payment, accommodation, or reimbursement does not by itself settle whether the participant is working for remuneration. Examine the reason for payment, relationship, control, productive output, hours, and full arrangement.

## What the plan should document

The current application materials call for evidence such as:

- an invitation explanation covering the skill to be acquired, background, and need for training in Japan;
- a detailed schedule showing subjects, hours, sites, instructors, and practical components;
- a participant-treatment summary covering payments, housing, insurance, supervision, and other conditions;
- the receiving organization's business, facilities, staff, and ability to conduct the program;
- the applicant's employment, qualifications, and relevant prior experience;
- the sending organization and agreements among the applicant, sender, and receiver where applicable; and
- evidence that the applicant will use the acquired skills in relevant work after returning.

Where the current procedure relies on prior non-practical training outside Japan, ISA identifies a specific context in which that training must occur within the six months before planned entry and last at least one month and **160 hours**. Do not apply that line to every program without checking the route and checklist that triggered it.

## Receiving-organization responsibilities

The host should give the participant understandable written terms and deliver the plan actually submitted. It should maintain appropriate instructors, facilities, attendance and progress records, safe conditions, payments and expense records, accommodation arrangements where promised, and a contact for problems.

The organization should also identify changes early. A new site, instructor, curriculum, sending relationship, practical component, payment structure, or early termination can alter the facts used in the immigration decision. Host support does not authorize the status and cannot guarantee a COE, visa, landing, extension, or change.

## Compare nearby routes

- **Student** applies when education at a covered institution is the principal activity.
- **Cultural Activities** can cover qualifying unpaid academic, artistic, or Japanese cultural study.
- **Intra-company Transferee** or another work status may apply when an overseas employee transfers into covered remunerated duties in Japan.
- **Technical Intern Training** uses a regulated employment-and-training program with approved plans and organizations.
- **Employment for Skill Development**, beginning April 1, 2027, is a separate employment-based successor framework leading toward Specified Skilled Worker skill levels.
- A short business visit may cover meetings or observation but not ordinary productive work.

The later comparison guide will place these routes side by side; until then, ask what activity the person performs each day and whether the Japanese organization receives productive work.

## COE, visa, and period

An eligible representative in Japan commonly applies for the COE. MOFA identifies a passport, visa application form, photograph, and COE as core overseas visa materials, with additional mission- or nationality-specific evidence possible. Some public or publicly funded arrangements without a COE should be discussed with the responsible embassy or consulate in advance.

Available periods are **one year, six months, or three months**. The granted period follows the approved facts and is not selected by the applicant.

## Renewal, changes, and the end of training

Renewal can be available while the same qualifying program genuinely continues, but a course should have a defined purpose and completion point. Preserve the plan, attendance, instruction, progress, treatment, funding, and host records.

Do not change hosts, add productive work, begin side work, or convert the arrangement into employment without confirming and completing the necessary procedure first. At completion, follow the documented return and skill-use plan or obtain a different status before beginning a new principal activity. Training does not automatically convert into a work status.
