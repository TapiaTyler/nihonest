## What this status authorizes

Religious Activities ([宗教](/glossary/shukyo), **shūkyō**) covers missionary and other religious activity performed by a religious professional dispatched to Japan by a foreign religious organization. The official example is a missionary ([宣教師](/glossary/senkyoshi), **senkyōshi**), but the analysis turns on the dispatch relationship and actual activity rather than a particular English title or faith tradition.

A minister, priest, pastor, rabbi, imam, monk, nun, bishop, missionary, or other religious worker does not qualify from the title alone. The application must establish the foreign sending organization, the Japanese receiving setting, the person's religious standing and history, and the work planned in Japan.

## The foreign dispatch is central

The statutory activity concerns a religious professional **dispatched by a foreign religious organization**. A Japanese congregation, temple, school, charity, or other body being willing to host or pay a person does not by itself establish that requirement.

Resolve the complete relationship:

- Which foreign religious organization is sending the person?
- What authority does it have within its denomination or religious community?
- What position does the person hold, and why are they being dispatched?
- Which Japanese organization or facility will receive the person?
- Who directs the work, supplies support, and pays remuneration or living expenses?
- How long is the dispatch, and at which locations will the activity occur?

Where there is no genuine foreign-organization dispatch, obtain case-specific advice before assuming this status applies.

## Qualifying activity and mixed duties

Missionary work, worship leadership, pastoral or spiritual care, religious instruction, ceremonies, and other genuine religious duties may fit when performed within the qualifying dispatch. Describe them concretely rather than relying on “religious work.”

Applications also ask about incidental activities, including language teaching. That does not mean any teaching job is automatically authorized. Ordinary school instruction, commercial language lessons, administration, fundraising, childcare, welfare services, retail, media production, or business operations may require a different status or permission when they are separate from the principal religious activity.

Unpaid activity is not automatically Religious Activities either. A person primarily studying Japanese culture or religion without income may need to examine Cultural Activities or another route. A short visit for meetings or ceremonies requires its own entry-purpose analysis.

## Evidence from the worker and both organizations

The current ISA page asks for evidence that makes the dispatch and activity understandable. Depending on the procedure, this includes:

- a dispatch letter stating the period, position, and remuneration;
- material describing the foreign sending organization;
- material describing the Japanese receiving organization or facility;
- the denomination, history, representatives, organization, facilities, and adherents of the relevant bodies;
- records showing the applicant's religious position, education or training, and work history; and
- a concrete schedule and description of religious and incidental duties in Japan.

Foreign-language documents generally require Japanese translations. Names, dates, duties, payment, work locations, and relationships should remain consistent across the dispatch letter, organization records, application forms, and explanations.

The host may help prepare evidence or support a Certificate of Eligibility application through an eligible representative. ISA—not the sending or receiving organization—decides the immigration application.

## Period and renewal

Available periods are **five years, three years, one year, or three months**. The granted period is an individual decision.

Renewal is possible while the qualifying foreign dispatch and religious activity continue. Maintain current evidence of the sending and receiving organizations, dispatch, support or remuneration, duties, locations, and residence compliance. A new denomination or sending organization, locally hired role, substantially different duties, ended dispatch, or move into teaching, charity administration, or business can require a fresh status analysis.

## Side work and changes

Religious Activities is activity-limited. Separate paid employment, freelancing, commercial teaching, or business activity is not automatically covered. Before beginning it, compare the work with the granted status and check whether permission for activity outside the status or a change of status is needed.

Changes to the sending body, Japanese host, worksite, remuneration, or principal duties should be documented promptly. Also confirm any resident or organization notifications that apply to the particular change rather than assuming renewal will resolve it later.

## Questions to resolve before applying

1. Which foreign religious organization is formally dispatching the person?
2. What proves the applicant's religious standing, training, and history?
3. Which organization and facilities will receive the person in Japan?
4. What are the principal religious duties, schedule, duration, and locations?
5. Who provides remuneration, housing, or living support?
6. Which teaching, charity, administrative, or commercial tasks are only incidental—and which are separate work?
7. Which current procedure and evidence checklist apply?
