# Setting up electricity, gas, water, and home internet

Do not order every service as soon as you receive an address. First determine what the lease, dormitory, share house, serviced apartment, or employer housing already includes. The property may bill some services at a fixed rate, require named providers, share internet across the building, or expect the resident to establish every contract separately.

## Confirm responsibility before move-in

Ask the landlord or property management company in writing:

- which services are active, included, individually metered, or separately contracted;
- the provider name, customer or meter reference, and permitted start date;
- whether electricity is all-electric or the home also uses city gas or LP gas;
- whether gas activation requires the resident to attend;
- how water is started and whether the municipality, building, or private operator bills it;
- what internet infrastructure already enters the building and unit;
- whether drilling, exterior work, or landlord approval is required; and
- what must be stopped, transferred, or inspected at move-out.

Keep photographs of initial meter readings when accessible and safe. Do not alter a meter, sealed valve, breaker, cable, or building equipment.

## Start electricity, gas, and water

For [electricity](/glossary/denki) ([電気](/glossary/denki), **denki**), choose a lawful start date and confirm the retailer, plan, base charge, usage rates, fuel or market adjustments, contract period, payment method, and cancellation terms. Electricity retailers can offer different plans, but the network delivering power remains separate from the retail contract. Do not switch merely because a visitor claims to represent the current provider; verify the company and offer independently.

For [gas](/glossary/gasu) ([ガス](/glossary/gasu), **gasu**), identify whether the property uses city gas or LP gas and which company actually supplies it. An opening appointment may be required so staff can inspect the installation, activate service, and explain appliances. Never try to defeat a safety shutoff. Learn where the shutoff is and what the provider says to do after an earthquake, suspected leak, or appliance problem. If you smell gas, avoid flames and electrical switches, ventilate only when safe, leave danger, and use the provider or emergency route appropriate to the situation.

[Water service](/glossary/suido) ([水道](/glossary/suido), **suidō**) is generally arranged through the responsible local water authority or the building's named process. Confirm the exact address, unit, start date, account holder, billing cycle, sewer charge, and emergency contact. Ask management who handles leaks inside the unit, common pipes, and fixtures; do not assume the water authority repairs every part of the property.

## Treat home internet as both a contract and an installation

“Internet included” may mean shared building Wi-Fi, a wired port, a discounted optional plan, or only that a line can be installed. Test what is actually provided and ask about speed limits, congestion, security, equipment, support, and move-out return duties.

For a new fixed connection, confirm whether the unit supports fiber ([光回線](/glossary/hikari-kaisen), **hikari kaisen**), cable, another wired service, or only wireless options. Separate the network operator, internet service provider, sales agent, equipment rental, phone bundle, and optional services on the quotation. Ask about:

- installation feasibility and landlord approval;
- construction and activation lead time;
- installation, activation, equipment, and monthly charges;
- promotional periods and the price afterward;
- minimum term, renewal, transfer, and cancellation charges;
- IPv6, router, Wi-Fi, and device requirements;
- the exact speed description and whether it is a maximum rather than guaranteed; and
- equipment return instructions and non-return charges.

A mobile hotspot or phone connection can bridge a delay, but compare coverage, data limits, throttling, tethering, and total cost before treating it as a permanent substitute.

## Protect yourself when choosing or changing providers

Keep the application, important-matters explanation, plan, campaign conditions, installation record, bills, and cancellation instructions. Verify door-to-door or telephone offers through an independently located official contact. Do not disclose a meter number, account identifier, verification code, or payment information until you understand who is requesting it and why.

If the explanation and contract differ, or an unwanted switch or bundle appears, preserve the evidence and contact the provider promptly. The Electricity and Gas Market Surveillance Commission handles relevant energy-market consultation, while Consumer Hotline 188 can route consumer-contract problems to a local consultation counter. Internet disputes can also require the provider's formal complaint route and a consumer consultation. Do not assume every utility or telecommunications agreement has the same cooling-off or cancellation right.

## Coordinate payment and move-out

Available payment methods can include bank withdrawal, card, invoice, or convenience-store payment, depending on the provider. A newly arrived resident may need an interim method before opening a Japanese bank account. Confirm due dates, paper-bill charges, failed-payment handling, and when automatic payment begins.

Before moving, request [cancellation](/glossary/kaiyaku) ([解約](/glossary/kaiyaku), **kaiyaku**) or transfer for each separately contracted service. Record final dates and readings, arrange any inspection, return rented routers or other equipment using traceable delivery, and preserve the final bill. Avoid closing the only usable bank or card payment route before all final charges and refunds settle.
