> **Editorial review needed:** Income category, necessary expenses, business commencement, blue-return eligibility, municipal filings, and consumption tax depend on the actual activity and current year. Tax registration does not authorize the work.

## Clear permission before classifying tax

Side work can require separate immigration permission, employer consent, professional licensing, platform compliance, and local operating permission. Registering as a sole proprietor ([個人事業主](/glossary/kojin-jigyo-nushi), **kojin jigyōnushi**) or reporting income does not satisfy those systems.

Use the immigration-focused [side-work and freelancing guide](/articles/side-work-and-freelancing-on-a-work-status) before beginning the activity.

## Classify the income from the facts

Japan has defined income categories. A recurring independent activity may need analysis as business income ([事業所得](/glossary/jigyo-shotoku), **jigyō shotoku**) or miscellaneous income ([雑所得](/glossary/zatsu-shotoku), **zatsu shotoku**). Salary, real-estate, capital-gain, and other categories can apply instead.

The person cannot select the most favorable label merely by filing a business notice. Continuity, scale, profit purpose, independence, risk, organization, records, and the nature of the payment can matter. Category affects expense rules, loss treatment, forms, bookkeeping, and access to the blue-return system.

## Record income and necessary expenses

Record every transaction with date, counterparty, purpose, amount, currency, payment status, fee, and supporting document. Separate business and personal use. A necessary expense must have the required relationship to earning the income; a purchase is not fully deductible merely because it is useful or paid from a business account.

Mixed-use housing, utilities, phones, computers, travel, meals, vehicles, depreciation, inventory, foreign currency, platform fees, and payments to family require particular care. Preserve invoices, contracts, receipts, bank and platform statements, mileage or allocation methods, and copies of documents you issue.

## Review commencement and blue-return deadlines

A person beginning an activity expected to produce business, real-estate, or forestry income should check the Individual Business Commencement Notice and other applicable national, prefectural, or municipal filings. Current NTA guidance ties the commencement notice to the filing deadline for the year in which the business began.

The blue return ([青色申告](/glossary/aoiro-shinkoku), **aoiro shinkoku**) is an application-based system for qualifying business, real-estate, or forestry income with defined bookkeeping and timely-filing conditions. For a new business begun January 16 or later, the application deadline is generally two months after commencement; other start dates use the stated annual deadline. Missing the application deadline cannot be repaired merely by keeping good books later.

Blue-return benefits vary with bookkeeping, statements, electronic records or e-Tax, filing timeliness, and the exact activity. Do not promise a deduction before confirming the current conditions.

## Coordinate the annual return and other taxes

An employee's side-income filing analysis includes the limited ¥200,000 rule explained in the [final-return guide](/articles/filing-a-japanese-income-tax-return), but that figure is not a general exemption. Resident-tax reporting can still differ.

Also review consumption tax, qualified invoices, withholding on specified fees, prefectural individual enterprise tax, employees or contractors, social insurance, and business closure. A licensed tax accountant ([税理士](/glossary/zei-rishi), **zeirishi**) is appropriate when classification, prior years, cross-border work, major equipment, losses, employees, or several tax systems interact.
