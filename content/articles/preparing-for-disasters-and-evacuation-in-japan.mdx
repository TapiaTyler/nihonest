> **Live instructions control:** Check JMA, your municipality, and emergency services during an event. The safest action depends on the hazard, exact address, building, timing, people present, and current official instructions—not a universal evacuation rule.

## Start with the hazards at each regular location

Obtain the current municipal Hazard Map ([ハザードマップ](/glossary/hazard-map), **hazādo mappu**) for home, school, work, and other regular locations. Check earthquake effects, tsunami, river flood, inland flooding, landslide, storm surge, fire, volcano, and other hazards actually mapped there. One map or facility may cover only some hazards.

Record safe exits and more than one route. Walk routes in daylight, identify stairs, bridges, underpasses, seawalls, steep land, waterways, locked gates, and places that may become unsafe. Plan alternatives for nighttime, severe weather, power loss, damaged transport, or separated household members.

## Distinguish a place to escape from a place to stay

A Designated Emergency Evacuation Site ([指定緊急避難場所](/glossary/shitei-kinkyu-hinan-basho), **shitei kinkyū hinan basho**) is designated by the municipality for urgent protection from specified hazards. Confirm the hazard pictograms or municipal list: a site suitable for earthquake fire may not be suitable for flood or tsunami.

A Designated Shelter ([指定避難所](/glossary/shitei-hinanjo), **shitei hinanjo**) is intended for people who need somewhere to stay after evacuating or cannot return home. One facility may be designated as both, but the terms are not synonyms. Opening status, capacity, supplies, accessibility, pets, and registration can depend on the event and municipality.

Evacuation can also mean moving to a safe relative, hotel, upper floor, or another location—or remaining in a structurally safe place—when that fits the hazard and official instructions. Do not travel into danger merely because a building is labeled a shelter.

## Build a household plan

- Choose contacts inside and outside the affected area and a method for leaving short status messages.
- Decide meeting points and responsibilities if phones or transport fail.
- Plan for children, older people, disability and communication access, pregnancy, medication, medical equipment, service animals, and pets.
- Learn school, workplace, building, and municipality release or reunification procedures.
- Secure furniture and hazardous objects; know utility shutoff and fire-safety instructions for the property.
- Keep important documents and encrypted copies accessible without exposing identity data.
- Practice the plan and update it after moving, changing work or school, or changing household needs.

## Prepare supplies for actual needs

Maintain water, ready-to-eat food, medication, first aid, masks, hygiene supplies, glasses, lighting, radio or information access, batteries and charged power banks, cash, weather protection, sturdy footwear, gloves, basic tools, chargers, copies of key information, and comfort or access items. Rotate food, water, medication, and batteries.

Separate a portable evacuation bag from supplies used to remain safely at home. A commercial kit is not automatically complete for your household, climate, housing, health, or pets.

## Understand alerts before an event

Use multiple official information paths: JMA weather, earthquake, tsunami, and volcanic information; municipal websites, applications, email, social accounts, loudspeakers, television, radio, and the sources recommended locally. Confirm language and accessibility settings in advance.

An Earthquake Early Warning ([緊急地震速報](/glossary/kinkyu-jishin-sokuho), **kinkyū jishin sokuhō**) may provide only seconds and cannot predict every tremor perfectly. Protect yourself according to the immediate surroundings rather than running blindly. For tsunami or other rapidly developing hazards, follow the current warning and move promptly to the appropriate safer place.

## After immediate danger

Check injuries, fire, gas or electrical hazards, building safety, official information, and household contacts without entering a dangerous area. Call 119 for fire, rescue, or medical emergency and 110 for an urgent police incident. Photograph damage only when safe, preserve official and insurance records, and avoid spreading unverified social-media information.
