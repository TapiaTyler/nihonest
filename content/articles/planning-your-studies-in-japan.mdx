## Start with the course, not the visa

Choose the result you need—language preparation, a degree, graduate research, professional training, exchange credit, non-degree study, or a short experience. Then compare the exact institution and course, its teaching language, intake, qualification, workload, outcomes, and immigration suitability.

Use **Choosing a school and understanding admission requirements** for the detailed comparison. School admission, a Certificate of Eligibility, visa issuance, and landing permission remain separate decisions.

## Decide between a visitor stay and Student status

Some compatible short courses can be attended under Temporary Visitor status ([短期滞在](/glossary/tanki-taizai), **tanki taizai**). Visa exemption depends on nationality, passport, intended activity, and planned stay. Longer programs and qualifying courses designed for Student status ([留学](/glossary/ryugaku), **ryūgaku**) use a different process. A school may call a non-degree program lasting as long as an academic year “short-term,” so the label alone does not choose the route.

Read **Short-term study in Japan as a Temporary Visitor** before using the remaining journey. Later steps are marked by scope: some apply to all students, some only to Student status, and municipal procedures generally require resident registration.

## Build a full budget

Budget for application, examination, and admission fees; tuition and materials; travel and insurance; housing deposits; food, transport, utilities, and phone service; and an emergency reserve. Record which payments are refundable after withdrawal, delayed entry, or an unsuccessful immigration process. Do not make part-time work the foundation of the plan: Student status does not itself authorize work, permitted hours are limited, and employment may take time to find.

## Work backward from enrollment

Create one calendar for school applications, examinations, scholarship deadlines, financial evidence, the Certificate of Eligibility (COE), visa processing, housing, travel, orientation, and enrollment. Add decision points for delayed admission, an expiring document, a changed expense supporter, or a missed intake. Requirements vary by school, application context, diplomatic mission, and nationality.

> **Practical advice:** Keep a private checklist showing who requested each document, its deadline, whether an original or translation is required, and where the original is stored. Do not upload passport or financial-document scans to an informal planning service.

Before paying a non-refundable fee, verify the institution, refund rules, total first-year cost, and whom to contact if the visa timeline changes.
