## Start with the course, not the visa

Choose the kind of education you need—university, graduate school, professional training college, or Japanese-language study—then confirm that each candidate institution and course can support the immigration route you expect to use. Check entry requirements, teaching language, intake dates, application windows, tuition, location, and the qualification awarded. Ask the school directly when its published information is unclear.

## Build a full budget

Budget for application and admission fees, tuition, travel, insurance, initial housing costs, food, transport, utilities, a phone, and an emergency reserve. Do not make part-time work the foundation of the plan: Student status does not itself authorize work, permitted hours are limited, and employment may take time to find.

## Work backward from enrollment

Create one calendar for school applications, scholarship deadlines, financial evidence, the Certificate of Eligibility (COE), the visa application, housing, and travel. Processing and document requirements vary by school, immigration office, embassy or consulate, and nationality.

> **Practical advice:** Keep a private checklist showing who requested each document, its deadline, whether an original or translation is required, and where the original is stored. Do not upload passport or financial-document scans to an informal planning service.

Before paying a non-refundable fee, verify the institution, refund rules, total first-year cost, and whom to contact if the visa timeline changes.
