> **Effective-date warning:** Employment for Skill Development begins on **April 1, 2027**. Preparatory organization and plan applications begin earlier, but that does not make the residence activity available before the effective date.

## What the system is designed to do

Employment for Skill Development ([育成就労](/glossary/ikusei-shuro), **ikusei shūrō**) is an employment-based system intended to develop and retain workers in shortage fields. The ordinary target is to reach the skills and Japanese level required for Specified Skilled Worker ([特定技能](/glossary/tokutei-gino), **tokutei ginō**) category (i) through **three years** of planned employment.

This differs from Technical Intern Training's stated international skills-transfer purpose. It also differs from direct Specified Skilled Worker employment: a person who already satisfies the relevant SSW route may not need a three-year development program.

## Covered fields are current and field-specific

The fields approved in the January 23, 2026 policies are:

1. Nursing care
2. Building cleaning
3. Linen supply
4. Industrial product manufacturing
5. Construction
6. Shipbuilding and ship machinery
7. Automobile repair and maintenance
8. Accommodation
9. Railway
10. Logistics warehousing
11. Agriculture
12. Fishery and aquaculture
13. Food and beverage manufacturing
14. Food service
15. Forestry
16. Wood industry
17. Resource circulation

This list and the work categories inside it are effective-dated. Aviation and automobile transportation appear in the wider Specified Skilled Worker framework but do not currently have Employment for Skill Development fields. Check the current field table and field-specific operating policy before relying on a job title.

## Entry and completion targets

The plan must set the principal skill, work category, training and work sequence, Japanese-language development, assessments, and support. Current national materials use a general entry target around **A1** Japanese, with an approved course alternative in the applicable circumstances, and a completion target around **A2** plus the relevant skills level for Specified Skilled Worker (i). Field policies can add or define specific tests and levels.

The worker and accepting organization should receive a clear explanation of:

- duties and worksite;
- wages, hours, deductions, housing, and employment conditions;
- the three-year development plan and assessment points;
- Japanese lessons and who pays for them;
- required skills and language tests;
- support, consultation, complaint, and transfer routes; and
- the realistic requirements for changing to Specified Skilled Worker afterward.

Not passing the final skills or Japanese examination does not create an unlimited extension. Current policy contemplates a limited continuation of up to one additional year in qualifying cases to attempt the transition requirements.

## Accepting and supervising organizations

An **Employment for Skill Development implementing organization** employs the worker and conducts an approved plan. Under the supervising model, a licensed **supervising support organization** performs defined supervision and support. An individual-enterprise model is also available under its own relationship and plan rules.

Organizations must satisfy national and field-specific criteria, provide appropriate employment and development, protect the worker, maintain records, make required reports, and cooperate with oversight. Field councils, local-distribution measures, caps, additional qualifications, or organization conditions may apply.

Pre-effective applications are staged: ISA states that applications for supervising support organization permission began **April 15, 2026**, and applications for plan certification began **September 1, 2026**. These allow preparation for 2027; they do not authorize early participation.

## Changing the accepting organization

Transfers remain possible for unavoidable circumstances, including serious problems that prevent continuation. The successor system also introduces worker-requested transfer under conditions.

For a worker-requested transfer, the current framework requires all relevant conditions, including:

- completion of the field's transfer-restriction period, generally set between one and two years;
- the required field-specific skills and Japanese level;
- transfer within the same work category;
- an eligible new implementing organization and approved new plan; and
- use of the authorized matching and support process rather than prohibited private placement involvement.

The exact restriction period and tests vary by field. Some field policies permit an implementing organization to adopt a one-year restriction even where the field default is longer. Do not promise that every worker can change after one year or move into a different field.

## Prior Technical Intern Training matters

Time already spent in Technical Intern Training ([技能実習](/glossary/gino-jisshu), **ginō jisshū**) generally counts when determining access to the successor system. Current guidance indicates that a person with two or more prior years usually cannot return for a new three-year Employment for Skill Development cycle in another field. More limited prior participation can still require a new plan and careful time calculation.

A person already in Technical Intern Training around April 1, 2027 may remain under transitional legacy rules rather than automatically becoming an Employment for Skill Development worker. Read the current plan and [Technical Intern Training transition guide](/articles/technical-intern-training-status).

## Transition to Specified Skilled Worker

At completion, the intended route is Specified Skilled Worker (i) in the related field or work category. The worker must have the required skill and Japanese results, a qualifying employment contract, an eligible accepting organization, and the required support arrangement. Employment for Skill Development does not guarantee the later status.

Specified Skilled Worker has its own period limits, family rules, employer-change procedure, field duties, and category (ii) availability. Read the SSW guide rather than treating the development plan as the full application.

## Worker protections and planning cautions

The person is a worker. Wages, overtime, holidays, occupational safety, social and labor insurance, tax, pregnancy and family protections, freedom of movement, identity documents, and access to consultation remain separate legal obligations.

Do not accept recruitment debt, unexplained deductions, passport retention, threats, contract substitution, or work outside the approved category as normal program conditions. Keep copies of the plan, contract, pay records, assessments, lessons, support requests, and immigration documents. Official procedures and support channels should be used when a transfer, complaint, early ending, or status change becomes necessary.
