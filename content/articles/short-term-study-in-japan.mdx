## Choose the immigration route before enrolling

Japan's Temporary Visitor status can cover participation in short courses or lectures when the activity fits a short stay. A national or resident of a country or region covered by Japan's visa-exemption arrangements may be able to travel without applying for a visa in advance. Everyone else must check whether a Temporary Visitor visa is required.

> **Important distinction:** Visa-exempt does not mean immigration-status-exempt. You still request landing as a Temporary Visitor, and the landing permission in your passport controls what you may do and how long you may stay.

Temporary Visitor landing periods can be 15, 30, or 90 days, so 90 days is the maximum under this route—not a universal allowance. Check the current exemption rules for your passport, the requirements of the Japanese embassy or consulate responsible for you, and the period actually granted at the border.

## “Short-term program” does not always mean a visitor stay

Schools may call programs lasting from several weeks to an academic year “short-term” because they do not lead to a Japanese degree. That academic label does not decide immigration status. A course extending beyond your permitted visitor stay, or a course whose structure requires Student status, needs the appropriate long-term route.

Ask the school in writing:

- which immigration status its program is designed for;
- the exact start and end dates, including orientation and examinations;
- whether it sponsors a Certificate of Eligibility for Student status;
- whether attendance, internships, practical training, or other activities change the immigration analysis; and
- which services are available to Temporary Visitor participants.

If you intend long-term study, use the Student-status process before travel. Do not enter as a visitor on the assumption that you can simply change status in Japan. Official student guidance says visa-exempt applicants who enter for an examination must, as a rule, return home after passing to obtain Student status. Temporary Visitor extensions are generally limited to humanitarian or similarly unavoidable special circumstances.

## What changes on the Temporary Visitor route

Temporary Visitors are not mid- to long-term residents. Under the ordinary process, you will not receive a residence card, register an address in the resident register, receive My Number, or enroll in resident-based National Health Insurance or pension procedures. You should not expect to open a normal resident bank account. Arrange suitable travel or private medical insurance and a payment method that works from abroad.

You may still need accommodation, connectivity, school check-in, emergency contacts, and enough money for the stay. Short-stay housing, a travel eSIM or visitor SIM, and foreign cards or travel funds may be more realistic than products designed for registered residents.

Temporary Visitor status does not authorize paid work. This is different from Student status, where separately granted permission may allow limited part-time work. Do not accept paid shifts, income-producing internships, or remote/freelance work without authoritative advice that considers your exact circumstances.

## A practical decision check

Use the Temporary Visitor path only after confirming all of the following:

1. The school confirms the course is compatible with Temporary Visitor status.
2. Your passport is visa-exempt for the planned stay, or you have obtained the required Temporary Visitor visa.
3. The full course and travel plan fit within the landing period you may receive.
4. You do not plan to work or rely on resident-only registrations and services.
5. You have insurance, accommodation, communications, and payment arrangements suited to a visitor.

If any answer is uncertain, ask the school and the responsible Japanese embassy or consulate before paying non-refundable fees or traveling.
