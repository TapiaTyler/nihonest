# Sorting household waste and arranging disposal

Japan does not use one nationwide household collection chart. Municipalities manage ordinary household waste, while your building can add rules about the collection point, containers, access, and the time items may be placed outside. Start with the instructions for your exact address—not a chart from another city or a previous apartment.

## Build an address-specific disposal plan

Ask the municipal waste office or ward/city counter for the current calendar, category guide, designated-bag rules, and multilingual material. Also ask the landlord or management company where the collection point is and when it opens. Record:

- the local categories and required bags;
- each [collection day](/glossary/shushu-bi) ([収集日](/glossary/shushu-bi), **shūshū-bi**);
- the latest permitted set-out time;
- rules for cans, glass, plastic packaging, paper, food waste, and spray cans;
- holiday, storm, and missed-collection changes; and
- the contact for items that ordinary collection will not accept.

Labels such as “burnable,” “non-burnable,” and “recyclable” are translations, not universal specifications. Follow the examples and symbols in your municipality's current guide when performing [waste sorting](/glossary/gomi-bunbetsu) ([ごみ分別](/glossary/gomi-bunbetsu), **gomi bunbetsu**).

## Arrange large-item collection before moving day

[Oversized waste](/glossary/sodai-gomi) ([粗大ごみ](/glossary/sodai-gomi), **sodai gomi**) commonly requires an advance booking and a paid disposal ticket, but size thresholds, prices, waiting periods, and drop-off options vary. Do not leave furniture beside the collection point because a move is imminent. Photograph or record the reservation number, ticket, collection date, and approved location.

Second-hand sale, retailer take-back, donation, and municipal reuse schemes may be alternatives. Confirm who receives the item and any collection charge before handing it over.

## Separate regulated appliances and hazardous batteries

Air conditioners, televisions, refrigerators/freezers, and washing machines/clothes dryers are covered by the [Home Appliance Recycling Law](/glossary/kaden-recycle-ho) ([家電リサイクル法](/glossary/kaden-recycle-ho), **kaden risaikuru-hō**). They do not simply become ordinary oversized waste. The seller replacing the appliance, the original retailer, or a locally explained route may arrange collection; recycling and transport charges can apply. Confirm the current route with the municipality or retailer.

Lithium-ion batteries and products containing them can ignite after damage or compression. Do not hide them in an ordinary bag. Follow the municipality's named battery or small-electronics route, protect exposed terminals when instructed, and tell staff if a battery is swollen, damaged, or cannot be removed. Business waste follows a separate commercial-waste process.

## Avoid illegal dumping and unsafe collectors

[Illegal dumping](/glossary/fuhotoki) ([不法投棄](/glossary/fuhotoki), **fuhō tōki**) is not an acceptable fallback when a collection route is inconvenient. Be cautious when an unsolicited collector offers to remove appliances or furniture without clearly identifying the operator, fee, destination, and receipt. Ask the municipality which authorized route applies before paying or releasing an item.

If waste is refused, leave it where it is only when the municipal or building instructions say to do so. Otherwise bring it back inside, inspect any rejection notice, photograph it, and ask what category, bag, date, or preparation was wrong.

## A practical first-week checklist

1. Save the municipal calendar and waste-office contact.
2. Confirm the building collection point and access hours.
3. Buy only bags or tickets named by the municipality.
4. Create separate containers for the local categories.
5. Plan furniture and regulated-appliance disposal before a move.
6. Keep batteries and hazardous items out of ordinary mixed waste.

Official national guidance explains the overall responsibilities and regulated products. Your current municipality and building rules decide most daily actions.
