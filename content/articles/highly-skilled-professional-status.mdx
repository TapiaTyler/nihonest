## Start with the activity, not the score

Highly Skilled Professional ([高度専門職](/glossary/kodo-senmonshoku), **kōdo senmonshoku**) provides immigration advantages for qualifying advanced activity. It has three category-1 activity classes:

- **Highly Skilled Professional 1(a):** advanced academic research, research guidance, or education;
- **Highly Skilled Professional 1(b):** advanced specialized or technical activity; and
- **Highly Skilled Professional 1(c):** advanced business operation or management.

The person must fit the underlying activity and organization before the points or J-Skip threshold helps. A high salary, degree, or score does not convert unrelated duties into an authorized highly skilled activity.

## Route one: the points system

The ordinary system uses the current table for the relevant activity class. Factors can include education, professional experience, anticipated annual remuneration, age, research achievements, qualifications, Japanese ability, and specified institutional or policy bonuses. The person generally needs **70 points or more**.

Use the current table and keep evidence for every claimed point. Different classes use different items and calculations. Some specialized/technical and business applicants also face an annual-remuneration floor even if the arithmetic reaches 70.

## Route two: J-Skip

J-Skip is an alternative qualification method for special highly skilled professionals. It does not add points and is not a separate status of residence.

For advanced academic research or specialized/technical activity, the current alternatives are:

- a master's-level or higher degree plus anticipated annual remuneration of at least **¥20 million**; or
- at least ten years of relevant experience plus anticipated annual remuneration of at least **¥20 million**.

For advanced business-management activity, the current route requires at least five years of relevant management experience and anticipated annual remuneration of at least **¥40 million**.

The remuneration means the expected annual amount for the proposed highly skilled activity in Japan, including qualifying remuneration from the contracting and foreign organization under the current rule. It is not simply the applicant's highest past salary.

## Category 1 benefits

Highly Skilled Professional 1 receives a **five-year** period of stay. Listed advantages include:

- permission for specified related compound activities;
- preferential processing;
- a relaxed residence-history route toward permanent residence;
- qualifying work by a spouse beyond the ordinary Dependent framework;
- accompaniment by a parent under limited childcare or pregnancy conditions; and
- employment of a domestic worker under defined conditions.

J-Skip adds enhanced treatment, including access to priority lanes at certain large airports. Every family or household benefit has its own conditions. The principal applicant's approval does not automatically authorize a spouse, parent, or domestic worker.

## Category 2 and permanent residence are different

Highly Skilled Professional 2 permits the approved category-1 activity together with almost all employment-status activities and has an **unlimited period of stay**. It still remains a status of residence with continuing obligations; it is not Permanent Resident.

The ordinary points route generally allows a category-2 application after three years of qualifying category-1 activity. A person recognized through J-Skip can use a one-year category-1 period for the corresponding category-2 transition.

Permanent residence is a separate permission. Current accelerated routes can include maintaining 70 qualifying points across a three-year lookback, 80 points across a one-year lookback, or one year under the J-Skip route. The applicant must also satisfy the other applicable permanent-residence requirements. Do not treat today's calculated score as proof of a historical score.

## Evidence to prepare

Build the application in layers:

- the actual activity, duties, contract, organization, worksite, and remuneration;
- the correct 1(a), 1(b), or 1(c) class and its underlying status fit;
- the points worksheet and dated proof for each item, or the J-Skip degree/experience and remuneration pathway;
- degrees, employment certificates, research record, licenses, language results, awards, and institutional evidence actually claimed;
- organization records and projected Japanese remuneration; and
- separate evidence for any spouse, parent, child, domestic worker, category-2, or permanent-residence request.

A COE can support an overseas visa application but does not guarantee the visa or landing decision.

## Changes, renewal, and outside activity

Category 1 is tied to its approved activity class and organization. A new employer, organization, business, or materially different role may require a new change-of-status analysis rather than only an employment notification. Confirm the procedure before starting the changed activity.

The compound-activity benefit is broader than an ordinary work status but not unlimited permission to take any side job. The additional activity must fit the highly skilled framework or have the required separate authorization.

For category 1, preserve the underlying job and organization evidence as well as records supporting the relevant points or J-Skip route. Category 2 has no fixed expiry date, but the authorized activity, notifications, residence, tax, insurance, and other obligations still matter.
