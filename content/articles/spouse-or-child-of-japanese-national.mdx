## Three covered relationships

Spouse or Child of Japanese National ([日本人の配偶者等](/glossary/nihonjin-no-haigusha-to), **nihonjin no haigūsha tō**) can cover a legally recognized spouse, a person born as a child of a Japanese national, or a child specially adopted under the relevant Civil Code procedure. Ordinary adoption, engagement, step-parent status, or financial support does not automatically fit.

## Spouse cases

Immigration examines whether the marriage is valid and genuine. Evidence can include the Japanese spouse's family register, foreign civil records, relationship history, communication, shared residence and finances, and explanations for separation or unusual chronology. No certificate, photograph, marriage duration, or guarantor guarantees approval.

Relationships not recognized under the ordinary spouse route require careful current advice rather than an assumption that this status applies.

## Child and adoption cases

The child path concerns a person born as the child of a Japanese national; it is not limited to a minor dependent. Confirm nationality at birth separately because the person may already be Japanese. The adoption path is limited to the specific form of special adoption named by the status.

## Evidence, work, and period

For entry, a proxy in Japan commonly applies for a COE. Depending on the path, expect Japanese family-register records, foreign marriage, birth, or adoption records with translations, identity and residence records, household and financial information, and explanations for conflicting records.

This is status-based residence without an occupation restriction. Employment, licensing, tax, and business rules still apply. Available periods are **five years, three years, one year, or six months**; the granted period is an individual decision.

## Relationship changes and safety

A holder in the spouse category must notify ISA within **fourteen days** after divorce or bereavement. Long separation or the end of marriage can require an urgent route review, but domestic violence, child-rearing, litigation, and unavoidable separation can matter. Prioritize safety and seek qualified help.

Long-Term Resident may be considered in some cases, but published examples are not an automatic conversion rule.

## Permanent residence

A qualifying spouse or child may use a shorter residence-history exception, but Permanent Residence remains a separate discretionary permission with other requirements.
