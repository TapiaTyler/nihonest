## A pathway, not automatic business permission

Japan’s Start-up Visa pathway lets qualifying foreign entrepreneurs prepare a business with oversight and support from an approved Foreign Entrepreneurship Promotion Organization. It is designed as a bridge toward meeting the requirements for Business Manager ([経営・管理](/glossary/keiei-kanri), **keiei kanri**) status; it is not a promise that the later change of status will be approved.

The entrepreneur first identifies an approved participating organization and submits the business-preparation plan and locally required evidence. The organization evaluates whether the plan has a credible prospect of meeting the Business Manager requirements and, if satisfied, issues confirmation used in the immigration process. Programs differ in eligible industries, location commitments, language, deadlines, interviews, support, and monitoring.

## Official requirements at a glance

For the Start-up pathway itself:

- apply through a municipality or private organization certified as a Foreign Entrepreneurship Promotion Organization;
- submit the business plan and every document required by that program;
- pass the organization’s examination and the Immigration Services Agency’s separate examination;
- obtain a COE before the entrepreneur’s overseas visa application;
- at the visa stage, submit the passport, application form, photograph, and COE, plus any nationality- or mission-specific documents; and
- follow the approved plan, progress checks, support conditions, and immigration renewal schedule after arrival.

The participating organization may restrict the business field or require a connection to its region. Approval by the organization does not bind immigration, and incorporation does not replace either examination.

## The two timelines

MOFA’s overseas Start-up visa page lists a six-month period and requires a COE for the entrepreneur’s visa application. METI states that the supported preparation pathway can permit a stay of up to two years. These describe different layers of the process rather than a guaranteed two-year grant: the period actually authorized appears in the landing and residence documents, and continued stay depends on the applicable reviews and renewals.

## The revised Business Manager target

The criteria for Business Manager were substantially revised on **October 16, 2025**. A founder planning to transition from the Start-up pathway should now prepare for all of the following core requirements:

- a business office in Japan;
- at least **¥30 million** in qualifying paid-in capital, contributions, or qualifying business investment, depending on the business form;
- at least **one qualifying full-time employee**—generally a Japanese national, Special Permanent Resident, or person holding an eligible status based on civil status, rather than a worker holding an activity-based status;
- either a relevant master’s-level or professional degree, or at least three years of business management or administration experience; startup-preparation activity can count where the rules allow;
- Japanese ability around **CEFR B2** in either the applicant or a qualifying full-time employee; and
- professional confirmation of a new business plan, unless an applicable exception applies.

These are not interchangeable alternatives: the former ¥5 million-or-two-employees formulation no longer describes a new Business Manager application. The Immigration Services Agency also examines whether the business is real, viable, properly operated, licensed where necessary, and compliant with tax, labor, social-insurance, and other laws.

> **Official requirement:** The Start-up Visa provides preparation time; it does not waive the Business Manager criteria at the point of transition. Use the current ISA checklist and the participating organization’s milestones, because transitional rules and individual business structures can affect the evidence required.

## Build an auditable plan

Prepare a detailed business model, customer and market evidence, financing, ownership, premises plan, licensing analysis, hiring plan, milestones, and living-expense budget. Keep business and personal funds traceable. Ask qualified tax, corporate, licensing, labor, and immigration professionals about issues outside the support organization’s role.

Meet every progress-reporting and renewal deadline. Well before the preparation period ends, assess whether the business meets the current Business Manager criteria or whether another lawful path is needed. Family applications have separate eligibility and documentation; do not assume they follow automatically from the entrepreneur’s approval.
