> **If danger is happening now:** Move to a safer place when possible. Call Police 110 for an urgent crime or traffic incident, or Fire/Ambulance 119 for fire, rescue, or a medical emergency. General web guidance cannot assess a live emergency.

## Choose the emergency service

Call Police 110 ([110番](/glossary/hyakutoban), **hyakutō-ban**) when police need to respond urgently—for example, violence, a crime in progress, immediate threats, a serious disturbance, or a traffic collision.

Call Fire/Ambulance 119 ([119番](/glossary/hyakujukyuban), **hyakujūkyū-ban**) for fire, rescue, or an urgent medical problem. An Ambulance ([救急車](/glossary/kyukyusha), **kyūkyūsha**) is dispatched through 119. If the situation is serious, the dispatcher may send help while continuing to ask questions.

Do not delay an emergency call while searching for the perfect Japanese sentence. Use short facts, ask a nearby person for help if safe, and follow the dispatcher's questions.

## Give location before a long explanation

Be ready to state:

- police, fire, medical, or rescue emergency;
- exact address, building, floor, room, station exit, intersection, landmark, road direction, or map pin;
- what happened and what danger remains;
- number and condition of injured or threatened people;
- fire, smoke, weapons, traffic, chemicals, water, height, or other hazards;
- your name and callback number; and
- the language you can use.

Open the property's address record and learn how to pronounce the municipality, neighborhood, block, and building. On a road or trail, use signs, route numbers, kilometer markers, shops, stations, bridges, or coordinates. Keep the line available and send someone to guide responders when safe.

FDMA promotes three-way telephone interpretation and multilingual tools, but availability and workflow differ among local fire departments. Police language support also varies. Do not promise that a particular language will be immediate in every location.

## Prepare information before an ambulance is needed

Keep an accessible emergency record with name, birth date, address, allergies, conditions, medication, medical institution, emergency contact, communication or mobility needs, and insurance information. Give responders accurate information; do not delay urgent care because a card or document is missing.

An ambulance call and transport do not guarantee a particular hospital, treatment, insurance payment, or absence of later cost. The emergency team and receiving institutions make clinical and operational decisions.

## Use non-emergency routes appropriately

A neighborhood Police Box ([交番](/glossary/koban), **kōban**) can assist with local police matters, directions, lost property, and some reports, but it may be temporarily unattended and is not a substitute for 110 during immediate danger.

For a non-urgent concern, use the current prefectural police consultation route, municipality, medical-advice service, consumer center, school, employer, insurer, embassy or consulate, or qualified professional matching the issue. Numbers such as police consultation and medical-advice lines can vary in availability, coverage, age group, language, and hours; verify the current service where you live.

## After the immediate response

Record the agency, time, reference or report number, hospital, responsible contact, instructions, and follow-up deadlines. Preserve photographs, messages, witness details, medical documents, receipts, and insurance correspondence where relevant. Emergency dispatch resolves immediate safety; it does not replace later reporting, medical care, insurance, employment, immigration, consumer, or legal procedures.
