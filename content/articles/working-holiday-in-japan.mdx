> **Draft coverage:** This article maps the route and the questions to verify. It is not yet a review-ready eligibility or document checklist.

## What this route covers

Japan's working-holiday programs are bilateral arrangements intended primarily for an extended holiday, with employment allowed as an incidental way to supplement travel funds. Eligible countries and regions, age limits, residence requirements, quotas, repeat participation, funds, insurance, and application locations differ by agreement.

## Who should examine it

- Eligible nationals or residents within the age and other conditions of a current bilateral arrangement
- Travelers whose primary purpose is a holiday rather than ordinary labor migration

## Evidence to plan for

- Passport, age and residence eligibility, itinerary, and purpose statement
- Initial funds, return-travel capacity, and insurance where required
- The checklist from the Japanese mission responsible for the applicant

## Important boundaries

Use the country-specific mission page; a rule for one partner does not apply automatically to another. Confirm prohibited work and whether resident-registration obligations apply to the granted period.

## Before relying on this draft

Open the official sources linked below and use the current application page for the exact procedure you will file: Certificate of Eligibility, overseas visa, change of status, or extension. Requirements can differ by nationality, employer or host category, family circumstances, and the Japanese mission handling the application. Keep the planned activity consistent across contracts, forms, supporting records, and explanations.
