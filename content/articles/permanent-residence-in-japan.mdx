> **Editorial review needed:** Permanent Residence is discretionary and evidence-heavy. Confirm the current guideline and the document list for your present status immediately before applying.

> **Application criteria update:** ISA published revised Permanent Residence guidelines on **October 1, 2026**. The application framework summarized below predates that revision and is not a complete statement of the revised assessment. Check the official guideline's applicability and effective dates before relying on these application sections. The new cancellation rules are explained separately below.

## Permission, not an automatic milestone

Permanent Residence Permission ([永住許可](/glossary/eiju-kyoka), **eijū kyoka**) changes an eligible resident from an existing Status of Residence ([在留資格](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku), **zairyū shikaku**) to Permanent Resident ([永住者](/glossary/eijusha), **eijūsha**). The resulting status has an unlimited period and no activity-based work restriction. It is normally obtained in Japan, not as an ordinary first-entry visa.

## Core requirements

The guideline published on February 24, 2026 organized the assessment around:

- good conduct and compliance with Japanese law;
- assets or skills supporting an independent, stable livelihood; and
- whether permanent residence accords with Japan's interests.

The national-interest assessment includes residence history, the present period of stay, compliance with the current status, and proper fulfillment of public obligations such as taxes, public pension, public medical insurance, and required immigration notifications. Payment after a deadline may still be unfavorable even when the debt is later cleared.

## Ordinary residence path

In principle, the applicant needs **ten continuous years** in Japan, including at least **five continuous years** under qualifying work statuses or status-based residence. Time under Technical Intern Training and Specified Skilled Worker (i) does not count toward that five-year work-or-residence portion under the ordinary rule.

Continuity is not decided by simply adding dates. Long absences, the purpose of travel, residence base, re-entry handling, and other facts require individual review.

## Important exceptions to ten years

Current exceptions include:

- a spouse of a Japanese national, Permanent Resident, or Special Permanent Resident after at least three years of genuine marriage and at least one continuous year in Japan;
- their qualifying biological child after at least one continuous year in Japan;
- five continuous years under Long-Term Resident;
- five years after recognition as a refugee or complementary-protection recipient;
- specified contribution and regional-revitalization routes; and
- accelerated routes for qualifying highly skilled people and J-Skip applicants.

An exception shortens a residence-history component. It does not waive every other requirement or guarantee approval.

## The period-of-stay rule changes in 2027

The guideline requires the applicant to hold the longest period available for their current status. A temporary rule treats **three years** as the longest period through **March 31, 2027**.

From **April 1, 2027**, the actual longest available period is required. ISA also describes limited transitional handling for certain people who hold a three-year period on March 31, 2027 and receive a decision during that period. Check the current notice rather than relying on older advice that three years always qualifies.

## Evidence depends on the route

ISA publishes separate document paths for spouses and children, Long-Term Residents, ordinary work and Dependent applicants, highly skilled applicants, and J-Skip applicants. Common categories include:

- residence, family, and immigration history;
- income, employment, business, or livelihood records;
- national and local tax assessment and timely payment;
- pension and medical-insurance enrollment and timely payment;
- passport and residence card;
- route-specific relationship, points, or contribution evidence; and
- a guarantor, usually a Japanese national, Permanent Resident, or Special Permanent Resident residing in Japan.

Order documents only after identifying the correct current checklist and look-back periods.

## Application fee and the October 2026 change

The fee is charged **when Permanent Residence is granted**, not simply when the application is filed.

- For an application accepted from April 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026, the fee is **JPY 10,000**.
- For an application accepted on or after October 1, 2026, the fee is **JPY 200,000**.

The acceptance date controls the transition. An application accepted by September 30 keeps the JPY 10,000 schedule even if ISA decides it after October 1. ISA has also published reduction or exemption measures from October 1; a qualifying reduced Permanent Residence fee is JPY 20,000. Do not assume eligibility—confirm the current measure and evidence with ISA before filing.

Permanent Residence is not an ordinary online application under the duration-based online table. Follow the payment instructions issued for this procedure and check the current fee immediately before collection.

## Keep the current status valid while waiting

A Permanent Residence application does **not** extend the current period of stay. If it will expire while the application is pending, separately apply for an Extension of Period of Stay ([在留期間更新許可](/glossary/zairyu-kikan-koshin-kyoka), **zairyū kikan kōshin kyoka**) before expiry.

Continue meeting the current status, tax, insurance, pension, address, notification, and travel obligations. The application's acknowledgement form also requires material changes to be reported during examination.

## After permission

Permanent Residents do not renew a period of stay, but must renew the Residence Card ([在留カード](/glossary/zairyu-card), **zairyū kādo**) itself when required and remain subject to residence-card, address, re-entry, tax, and other laws. An unlimited period is not immunity from status revocation or deportation provisions. Special Permanent Residents ([特別永住者](/glossary/tokubetsu-eijusha), **tokubetsu eijūsha**) are a distinct legal category governed by a separate statute; see the [separate guide](/articles/special-permanent-residents-in-japan).

## Cancellation changes from April 1, 2027

Amendments enacted by Act No. 60 of 2024 take effect on **April 1, 2027**. They add cancellation grounds for failing to perform specified statutory immigration duties, deliberately failing to pay public dues despite having the ability to pay, and specified criminal convictions carrying imprisonment. These grounds do not make ordinary inability to pay equivalent to deliberate evasion; the facts and applicable statutory conditions matter.

For the residence-card duty, a single careless failure to carry a card or renew its validity is not by itself envisaged as leading to cancellation under ISA's final guidance. Existing penalties for failing to carry a card or refusing a lawful request to present it remain separate. Repeated noncompliance after guidance, indications that the person will not comply in future, and the surrounding circumstances may lead to an individual cancellation assessment. Keep a required card with you and respond to lawful instructions.

When a cancellation ground applies, the amended process generally provides for an ex officio change to another status if the Minister determines that the person's continued residence is not inappropriate. If continued residence is considered inappropriate, cancellation can proceed. The alternative status depends on the person's circumstances; this is not an automatic conversion or guarantee of Long-Term Resident status. See [Long-Term Resident status](/articles/long-term-resident-status) for its separate eligibility rules and [documents received when entering Japan](/articles/documents-received-when-entering-japan) for card duties and penalties.
