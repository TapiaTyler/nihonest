## Permission, not an automatic milestone

Permanent Residence Permission ([永住許可](/glossary/eiju-kyoka), **eijū kyoka**) changes an eligible resident to Permanent Resident ([永住者](/glossary/eijusha), **eijūsha**). The resulting status has an unlimited period and no activity-based work restriction. It is normally obtained from an existing status, not as an ordinary first-entry visa.

## Core requirements

The February 24, 2026 guideline organizes the assessment around:

- good conduct and compliance with Japanese law;
- assets or skills supporting an independent, stable livelihood; and
- whether permanent residence accords with Japan's interests.

The national-interest assessment includes residence history, the present period of stay, compliance with the current status, and proper fulfillment of public obligations such as taxes, public pension, public medical insurance, and required immigration notifications. Payment after a deadline may still be unfavorable even when the debt is later cleared.

## Ordinary residence path

In principle, the applicant needs **ten continuous years** in Japan, including at least **five continuous years** under qualifying work statuses or status-based residence. Time under Technical Intern Training and Specified Skilled Worker (i) does not count toward that five-year work-or-residence portion under the ordinary rule.

Continuity is not decided by simply adding dates. Long absences, the purpose of travel, residence base, re-entry handling, and other facts require individual review.

## Important exceptions to ten years

Current exceptions include:

- a spouse of a Japanese national, Permanent Resident, or Special Permanent Resident after at least three years of genuine marriage and at least one continuous year in Japan;
- their qualifying biological child after at least one continuous year in Japan;
- five continuous years under Long-Term Resident;
- five years after recognition as a refugee or complementary-protection recipient;
- specified contribution and regional-revitalization routes; and
- accelerated routes for qualifying highly skilled people and J-Skip applicants.

An exception shortens a residence-history component. It does not waive every other requirement or guarantee approval.

## The period-of-stay rule changes in 2027

The guideline requires the applicant to hold the longest period available for their current status. A temporary rule treats **three years** as the longest period through **March 31, 2027**.

From **April 1, 2027**, the actual longest available period is required. ISA also describes limited transitional handling for certain people who hold a three-year period on March 31, 2027 and receive a decision during that period. Check the current notice rather than relying on older advice that three years always qualifies.

## Evidence depends on the route

ISA publishes separate document paths for spouses and children, Long-Term Residents, ordinary work and Dependent applicants, highly skilled applicants, and J-Skip applicants. Common categories include:

- residence, family, and immigration history;
- income, employment, business, or livelihood records;
- national and local tax assessment and timely payment;
- pension and medical-insurance enrollment and timely payment;
- passport and residence card;
- route-specific relationship, points, or contribution evidence; and
- a guarantor, usually a Japanese national, Permanent Resident, or Special Permanent Resident residing in Japan.

Order documents only after identifying the correct current checklist and look-back periods.

## Keep the current status valid while waiting

A Permanent Residence application does **not** extend the current period of stay. If it will expire while the application is pending, separately apply for an Extension of Period of Stay ([在留期間更新許可](/glossary/zairyu-kikan-koshin-kyoka), **zairyū kikan kōshin kyoka**) before expiry.

Continue meeting the current status, tax, insurance, pension, address, notification, and travel obligations. The application's acknowledgement form also requires material changes to be reported during examination.

## After permission

Permanent Residents do not renew a period of stay, but must renew the residence card itself when required and remain subject to residence-card, address, re-entry, tax, and other laws. An unlimited period is not immunity from status revocation or deportation provisions.

## Questions to resolve

1. Which ordinary or exceptional residence path applies?
2. Is residence continuous under the current guideline?
3. Were taxes, pension, and health insurance handled correctly and on time?
4. What is the longest period for the current status on the relevant decision date?
5. Which evidence checklist and look-back periods apply?
6. Will the current status need a separate extension while the application is pending?
