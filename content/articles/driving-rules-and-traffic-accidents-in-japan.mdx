> **Emergency first:** After a collision, stop without creating further danger, assist injured people, call 119 for fire or medical emergency, and report the accident to police at 110. Do not leave because damage looks minor or the other person suggests settling privately.

## Learn the road system before driving

Traffic normally keeps left. The driver sits on the right side of many Japanese vehicles, but imported vehicles differ. Road position, turns, railway crossings, signals, lane arrows, one-way streets, speed limits, parking restrictions, and signs must be learned from current NPA materials rather than inferred from another country.

Drivers must give required priority and protection to pedestrians, including at crossings. Wear seat belts in every seating position and use the required child restraint. Never drive after drinking alcohol or when medication, fatigue, illness, or another condition makes driving unsafe. Do not hold or operate a phone while driving. Follow the posted limit and reduce speed further for visibility, weather, road, people, and traffic.

A foreign license, international permit, or Japanese license does not excuse unfamiliarity. Rental explanations are supplemental; they are not a driving course.

## Keep the vehicle and documents ready

Before departure, check license authority, registration and inspection status, compulsory and voluntary insurance, fuel or charge, tires, lights, mirrors, weather, route, toll method, parking, and emergency equipment. Know any automatic-transmission, vehicle-class, corrective-lens, novice, or other restriction on the license.

Parking temporarily with hazard lights does not legalize a prohibited stop. Confirm signs, meters, private-lot terms, height and width limits, closing times, and what to do if a vehicle is moved or locked in.

## Respond to a Traffic Accident

A Traffic Accident ([交通事故](/glossary/kotsu-jiko), **kōtsū jiko**) can create criminal, administrative, civil, medical, employment, and insurance consequences. At the scene:

1. Stop immediately in a safe way and prevent a secondary collision when possible.
2. Help injured people without putting yourself in further danger. Call Fire/Ambulance 119 ([119番](/glossary/hyakujukyuban), **hyakujūkyū-ban**) for urgent medical or fire response.
3. Call Police 110 ([110番](/glossary/hyakutoban), **hyakutō-ban**) and follow instructions. NPA guidance says to report a traffic accident even when no one appears injured.
4. Exchange and preserve names, contact details, vehicle and plate information, license and insurance details, location, time, photographs, witness information, and police reference details without obstructing care or traffic.
5. Contact the insurer or vehicle owner promptly and follow reporting requirements. If driving for work or in a rental or shared vehicle, notify the responsible organization as required.
6. Seek medical assessment if symptoms appear. Some injuries are not obvious at the scene.

Do not sign an unexplained statement, admit a legal conclusion you do not understand, pay an informal demand, or post identifying allegations online. Describe facts accurately and obtain qualified language or legal support where needed.

## Insurance and police records are separate

Reporting supports the official accident record, but police do not decide every civil payment question at the roadside. Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance ([自賠責保険](/glossary/jibaiseki-hoken), **jibaiseki hoken**) has limited bodily-injury purposes; voluntary policies and other legal responsibility address different losses. Preserve medical, repair, travel, work-absence, communication, and payment records.
