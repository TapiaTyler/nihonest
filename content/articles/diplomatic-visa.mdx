## Who Diplomat status covers

Diplomat ([外交](/glossary/gaiko), **gaikō**) applies to:

- constituent members of diplomatic missions or consular offices of foreign governments hosted by Japan;
- people receiving comparable privileges or immunities under a treaty or international customary practice; and
- qualifying family members belonging to the same household.

The status depends on the recognized role and assignment. A diplomatic passport, government employer, formal title, invitation, or family relationship does not establish the category by itself.

## The period is “during mission”

MOFA describes the Period of Stay as **during mission** rather than a fixed number of months or years. The assignment's continuation, accreditation, and Japan's recognition remain central.

This is not unlimited personal residence. The end of the role, reassignment, loss of recognition, household change, or plan to remain for a private reason requires early coordination.

## Official evidence and application channel

MOFA lists:

- the passport;
- a photograph;
- a note verbale or documents from the foreign government or international organization certifying identity and purpose; and
- a visa application form, although it can be omitted in some cases.

The sending foreign ministry, embassy, consular post, or international organization should coordinate with the responsible Japanese diplomatic channel. An individual should not rewrite the official purpose or improvise an ordinary work-status application without that coordination.

## Diplomatic passport does not always mean Diplomat

MOFA expressly warns that not every diplomatic-passport holder receives a Diplomatic Visa or Diplomat status. If the purpose is a holiday, tourism, transit, or another private visit, the person can instead be admitted as Temporary Visitor ([短期滞在](/glossary/tanki-taizai), **tanki taizai**) for fifteen, thirty, or ninety days.

Visa exemptions differ between diplomatic, official, and ordinary passports and can depend on nationality, official purpose, and duration. An exemption that produces Temporary Visitor status does not create diplomatic privileges or the Diplomat residence category.

## Household family members need their own basis

The family provision covers qualifying members belonging to the same household. Each person's identity, relationship, household position, passport, assignment connection, and official documentation should be confirmed. “Family” should not be expanded informally to every relative or household employee.

If a child is born, a marriage changes, or a family member arrives or departs separately, use the protocol and immigration channel assigned to the mission.

## Residence-card and ordinary onboarding assumptions do not apply

People granted Diplomat status are excluded from the ordinary mid-to-long-term residence-management system and are not issued an ordinary residence card through that system. Do not send them automatically through the standard residence-card, municipal address, My Number, insurance, pension, or employment flows.

Instead, follow the sending organization and Japanese protocol instructions for identity records, residence, vehicles, customs, tax, healthcare, household staff, and other arrangements. A special rule in one area does not imply immunity or exemption in every other area.

## Work, changes, and departure

Diplomat status covers the recognized diplomatic function and qualifying household activity. Private employment, freelancing, unrelated paid activity, or remaining after the mission should never be assumed authorized.

Coordinate role changes, transfer, mission completion, family changes, travel, and departure before they occur. If the person's purpose becomes official-but-nondiplomatic or private, the proper route must be determined rather than simply retaining the Diplomat label.
