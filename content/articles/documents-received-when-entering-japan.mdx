## Landing permission

When entry is approved, your passport receives evidence of landing permission. It records the status of residence and period of stay granted. These—not the label you casually use for your visa—govern your permitted stay after entry.

## Residence card

Mid- to long-term residents receive a residence card (在留カード, **zairyū kādo**). At designated major airports it is normally issued during landing procedures. At other ports, the passport is endorsed to show that the card will be issued later; after you report your address, it is sent to the registered residence.

Check the spelling of your name, status of residence, period of stay, expiration date, and any work-permission notation. Ask immigration or your school promptly if something appears wrong. Do not alter the card.

> **Official requirement:** A residence card is an important government identity and immigration document. Follow current rules on carrying it, presenting it when lawfully required, and reporting changes. Never lend it or share an image casually.

Your COE, visa, landing permission, residence card, My Number notice, and My Number Card are different things. The COE supported the application; the visa allowed travel to seek entry; landing permission records the authorized stay; the residence card proves residence information; My Number belongs to a separate administrative system.
