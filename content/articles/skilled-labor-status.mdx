## What Skilled Labor means

Skilled Labor ([技能](/glossary/gino), **ginō**) covers work under a contract with a Japanese public or private organization that requires a skilled ability in a special industrial field. It is not a general status for every experienced tradesperson, craft worker, technician, cook, or coach.

The proposed work must fit one of the nine categories in the current landing criteria. The applicant must then satisfy that category's experience, achievement, or licensing rule and receive remuneration at least equivalent to a Japanese worker doing comparable work.

## The nine covered categories

### Foreign cuisine or food production

This category covers skilled preparation or production of food developed abroad and considered special in Japan. The general pathway requires at least **ten years of relevant practical experience**; relevant study at a foreign educational institution may count.

The Japan–Thailand agreement creates a separate route for qualifying Thai-cuisine cooks. Current evidence includes at least five years of qualifying Thai-cuisine experience, the specified Thai skills certificate, and proof of appropriate Thai-cook remuneration during the year immediately before applying. Do not generalize this exception to other cuisines or nationalities.

The menu, cuisine, kitchen, business, position, and actual cooking duties must support the claimed foreign specialty. General restaurant service or routine food preparation is not converted into Skilled Labor by using “chef” as a title.

### Foreign-specific architecture or civil engineering

This covers a construction or civil-engineering skill particular to a foreign country. The ordinary threshold is at least **ten years of relevant experience**, including qualifying foreign study. It can be **five years** when the worker will perform the skilled work under the direction and supervision of a foreign worker who has at least ten years of that relevant experience.

Ordinary domestic construction work is not automatically included. Identify what makes the technique foreign-specific and how the project will use it.

### Manufacture or repair of a foreign-specific product

This category requires at least **ten years of relevant experience**, including qualifying foreign study, manufacturing or repairing a product particular to a foreign country. The product and specialized method need to be documented; general factory, maintenance, or repair work does not qualify merely because the worker is experienced.

### Gemstone, precious-metal, or fur processing

The worker needs at least **ten years of relevant experience**, including qualifying foreign study, and must perform work requiring that processing skill. Separate production craft from ordinary retail, sales, appraisal, design, or factory-support duties.

### Animal training

The worker needs at least **ten years of relevant experience**, including qualifying foreign study, and must perform work requiring animal-training skill. General animal care, stable work, kennel work, or veterinary support is not automatically the same activity.

### Specialized drilling or seabed geological work

The worker needs at least **ten years of relevant experience**, including qualifying foreign study, in one of the listed activities: seabed drilling for petroleum exploration, drilling for geothermal development, or seabed geological investigation for mineral exploration.

General drilling, construction, mining, or survey work outside those activities requires another analysis.

### Aircraft piloting

The criterion requires at least **250 hours of flight experience** and work as a pilot aboard an aircraft used for an air-transport business as defined by Japanese aviation law. A pilot license or flight hours alone do not establish that the Japanese operation and duties fit.

### Sports instruction

One pathway requires at least **three years of relevant experience**. Qualifying foreign study in sports instruction and time spent participating in the sport for remuneration may count. The criteria also provide an achievement route for a person who competed as an athlete in the Olympic Games, a world championship, or another qualifying international competition.

This category concerns sports instruction. Professional competition or performance can point to Entertainer, while ordinary school teaching or fitness-facility duties need their own activity analysis.

### Wine appraisal and service

The sommelier route requires at least **five years of relevant experience**, including qualifying foreign study, plus one of the specified achievements: a strong result in an international sommelier competition, participation in a qualifying competition limited to one representative per country, or a certification designated under the current rules.

Restaurant experience or an undesignated wine certificate alone does not satisfy this category.

## Skilled Labor is not Specified Skilled Worker

Specified Skilled Worker ([特定技能](/glossary/tokutei-gino), **tokutei ginō**) is a separate program for employment in designated industrial fields. It uses field-specific duties, skills and Japanese-language routes, employer conditions, category (i) support, and different family and period rules.

Similar words do not merge the categories. For example:

- a foreign-cuisine specialist chef may examine Skilled Labor, while broader restaurant cooking, service, or store-operation work may fall within the Specified Skilled Worker food-service field;
- a foreign-specific construction technique may fit Skilled Labor, while designated construction work may use Specified Skilled Worker; and
- an aircraft pilot can fall within Skilled Labor, while other designated airport or aviation work may use the Specified Skilled Worker aviation field.

Choose by the statutory activity, not by which English label sounds more prestigious or by years of experience alone.

## Evidence and employer responsibilities

Current evidence differs for cooks and other activities. Plan to establish:

- the exact category and detailed duties;
- a traceable chronology of relevant employment and qualifying education;
- category-specific flight, competition, certification, supervision, product, technique, menu, facility, or project records;
- the contract, remuneration, hours, worksite, and any dispatch destination; and
- the Japanese organization's business, financial, and operational capacity to provide the claimed work.

Experience letters should identify the issuing organization, dates, position, and actual skilled duties. Gaps, overlapping periods, generic certificates, or unverifiable organizations can weaken the claimed total.

For category 3 or 4 organizations, ISA's current pages state that applications from April 15, 2026 require the additional declaration concerning the organization's representative. Use the current procedure-specific checklist rather than copying an older document list.

## Period, renewal, and changes

Available periods are **five years, three years, one year, or three months**. The granted period is an individual decision.

Renewal is possible while the covered skilled activity, contract, remuneration, employer, and residence compliance continue. The status does not impose the Specified Skilled Worker (i) five-year aggregate limit, but that does not guarantee renewal or permanent residence.

A move to a different category, ordinary duties, management, a new employer, or materially changed worksite can require a new status analysis and immigration procedure. Additional paid work is not automatically covered because it is practical or skilled.

## Questions to resolve before applying

1. Which of the nine categories exactly matches the principal duties?
2. What category-specific experience, achievement, license, or exception applies?
3. Which study periods can legitimately count toward experience?
4. Can every claimed period and duty be verified?
5. Does the Japanese organization actually provide the specialized work?
6. Is remuneration at least equivalent to comparable Japanese work?
7. Is the role instead within a current Specified Skilled Worker field or another status?
8. Which current procedure and evidence checklist apply?
