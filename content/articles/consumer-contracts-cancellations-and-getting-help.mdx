# Consumer contracts, cancellations, and getting help

A sales pitch, app screen, receipt, confirmation email, and full contract may describe different parts of one transaction. Before assuming a charge is invalid—or that it cannot be challenged—identify what you agreed to, with whom, through which sales channel, and on what date.

## Capture the transaction before contacting anyone

Preserve the advertisement, product page, checkout screens, order confirmation, contract, cancellation terms, messages, call history, invoices, delivery records, and card statement. Record the seller's legal name, address, telephone number, website, payment recipient, and any intermediary platform.

For a rental, phone, utility, lesson, gym, or subscription, separately identify:

- the minimum term and renewal method;
- one-time, recurring, cancellation, return, repair, and restoration charges;
- bundled products or services;
- the cancellation channel and deadline;
- whether the transaction was personal or for a business; and
- what the Japanese contract says when a translation or spoken explanation differs.

A low introductory price can lead into a [subscription purchase](/glossary/teiki-konyu) ([定期購入](/glossary/teiki-konyu), **teiki kōnyū**). Confirm the number of deliveries, total cost, renewal, and cancellation conditions rather than relying on the first payment alone.

## Cooling-off is not a universal return period

[Cooling-off](/glossary/cooling-off) ([クーリング・オフ](/glossary/cooling-off), **kūringu ofu**) is a statutory cancellation mechanism for specified transaction categories and periods. Under the Specified Commercial Transactions Act, covered routes include areas such as door-to-door sales and telemarketing, while ordinary mail-order and online sales do not receive the same general cooling-off provision merely because they occurred remotely.

The sales method, contract type, statutory document, delivery date, misrepresentation, and elapsed time matter. Read the current official instructions and seek help promptly. If an electronic notice is permitted, retain the sent message or screenshot and delivery evidence. Do not let an approaching deadline pass while negotiating informally.

## Use a focused escalation sequence

1. Tell the seller precisely what outcome you seek and why.
2. Use the contract's formal cancellation or complaint route.
3. Preserve every response and avoid returning an item without traceable instructions.
4. Contact the payment provider when a charge is unauthorized or disputed, without assuming a reversal is guaranteed.
5. Ask a public consumer service to classify the problem and next step.

Japan's [Consumer Hotline](/glossary/shohisha-hotline) ([消費者ホットライン](/glossary/shohisha-hotline), **shōhisha hottorain**) at **188** routes callers using their postal code to a nearby municipal or prefectural [Consumer Affairs Center](/glossary/shohi-seikatsu-center) ([消費生活センター](/glossary/shohi-seikatsu-center), **shōhi seikatsu sentā**) or consultation counter. Availability, language support, call charges, and compatible telephone services vary, so use the official directory or local government's published contact if 188 is inaccessible.

For a Japan-based consumer disputing a seller outside Japan, the Cross-border Consumer Center Japan provides a specialist route. It does not guarantee recovery and is different from a local dispute with a Japanese seller. Visitors should use the current official visitor-consumer channel. Immediate danger, threats, or a crime in progress belong with emergency services rather than an ordinary contract consultation.

## Language affects evidence, not whether you deserve help

Ask the business for an understandable explanation, but retain the exact Japanese terms. Bring a trusted interpreter or ask the consultation service what language assistance is available. Never sign a blank form or let someone keep your only copy. If the dispute involves a large loss, litigation, immigration consequences, identity misuse, or business activity rather than personal consumption, ask whether legal, police, immigration, or business advice is also needed.
