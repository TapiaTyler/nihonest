> **Editorial review needed:** Tax residence and taxable scope depend on facts that immigration documents do not decide. Cross-border income, remittances, treaties, remote work, companies, trusts, investments, and departure plans can require a licensed tax professional.

## Start with tax law, not the visa label

Japan's Income Tax ([所得税](/glossary/shotoku-zei), **shotokuzei**) rules classify a person separately from immigration law. A residence card, Permanent Resident status, Temporary Visitor status, nationality, or a foreign tax-residence certificate does not by itself answer the Japanese income-tax question.

For individual income tax, first distinguish:

- a tax resident ([居住者](/glossary/kyojusha), **kyojusha**);
- a non-permanent resident ([非永住者](/glossary/hi-eijusha), **hi-eijūsha**), which is a subcategory of tax resident; and
- a tax non-resident ([非居住者](/glossary/hikyojusha), **hikyojusha**).

These labels control the scope of income that Japan can tax. They do not grant permission to work, determine a residence status, or replace a tax-treaty analysis.

## Resident and non-resident classification

The NTA's current guide generally describes a resident as an individual who has a **domicile in Japan** or who has continuously had a **residence in Japan for one year or more**. Everyone outside that definition is generally treated as a non-resident.

“Domicile” is a factual concept about the center of a person's life. Housing, family, occupation, intended duration, and other circumstances can matter. It is therefore unsafe to assume that tax residence begins only after 365 days or that a short immigration period always means tax non-residence.

A temporary trip abroad does not necessarily end Japanese tax residence. The NTA specifically identifies facts such as family remaining in Japan, retaining a home for the return, and leaving daily-use property in Japan as relevant to a temporary-absence analysis.

## Non-permanent resident does not mean non-resident

A non-permanent resident is a **tax resident** who:

- does not have Japanese nationality; and
- has had a domicile or residence in Japan for an aggregate of five years or less during the previous ten years.

This is not the immigration status called Permanent Resident. A person can hold Japanese Permanent Resident status and still need the separate income-tax timeline checked, while a person on a time-limited immigration status can be a resident other than a non-permanent resident after enough time in Japan.

Preserve entry, departure, domicile, residence, and nationality records across the whole ten-year lookback. Partial years and earlier periods in Japan can affect when the classification changes.

## Identify the possible taxable scope

The broad NTA framework is:

- **Residents other than non-permanent residents:** generally subject to Japanese income tax on worldwide income.
- **Non-permanent residents:** generally taxed on income other than foreign-source income, plus foreign-source income paid in Japan or treated as remitted to Japan.
- **Non-residents:** generally taxed on Japanese domestic-source income under the rules for that income.

The source of income is a legal classification. It is not always the payer's country, the bank-account location, the currency, or where a contract was signed. Work physically performed in Japan, overseas employment, remote work, investments, rent, stock compensation, pensions, and business income can each require different source and timing analysis.

For a non-permanent resident, moving money to Japan is not a simple “only this transfer is taxed” election. The NTA applies ordering and deemed-remittance rules across income paid abroad during the year. Do not label a transfer as savings and assume the taxable result without reconciling income and remittances for that year.

## Build a residence and income timeline

Record:

- every arrival and departure date during at least the relevant ten-year period;
- nationality changes and the date each Japanese home became available or ended;
- where a spouse, children, and household remained;
- employers, clients, work locations, and dates services were performed;
- each income type, earning period, payer, payment date, currency, and receiving account;
- transfers into Japan and the accounts involved; and
- foreign tax charged or withheld.

Do not wait until annual filing to reconstruct the timeline. The classification affects payroll, treaty documents, the Final Tax Return ([確定申告](/glossary/kakutei-shinkoku), **kakutei shinkoku**), and the records a tax professional will need.

## Know when this guide is not enough

Ask the responsible tax office or a licensed tax accountant before relying on a generalized answer when you have competing homes, frequent travel, foreign employment, remote work, equity compensation, cryptocurrency, overseas rent, a business or company, a trust, large foreign assets, a treaty claim, or a mid-year arrival or departure.

Tax treatment remains separate from immigration permission, an employment contract, professional licensing, social insurance, and business registration. Satisfying one system does not satisfy the others.
