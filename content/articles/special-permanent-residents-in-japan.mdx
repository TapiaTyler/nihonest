## A distinct statutory category

Special Permanent Resident ([特別永住者](/glossary/tokubetsu-eijusha), **tokubetsu eijūsha**) is a legal position established under Japan's Special Act on Immigration Control. It is not an ordinary status of residence and is not the same as Permanent Resident ([永住者](/glossary/eijusha), **eijūsha**), which is granted through a separate application and examination.

The special category generally covers people who lost Japanese nationality under the Peace Treaty arrangements and qualifying descendants who meet the Act's conditions. It is not automatically available because someone is Korean, Taiwanese, has Japanese ancestry, or has lived in Japan for a long time. The statutory history and each person's family and residence records matter. If you are unsure which category applies, consult the municipality that holds your resident record or ISA.

## Your certificate and carrying rules

Special Permanent Residents receive a Special Permanent Resident Certificate ([特別永住者証明書](/glossary/tokubetsu-eijusha-shomeisho), **tokubetsu eijūsha shōmeisho**) instead of an ordinary Residence Card ([在留カード](/glossary/zairyu-card), **zairyū kādo**). The municipality normally handles certificate issuance and renewal; issuance following the special permission procedure under Article 5 is handled by ISA. Keep the certificate valid and report required changes through the applicable process.

Special Permanent Residents are generally exempt from the ordinary duty to carry a residence card. This does not mean the certificate can be ignored: an authorized officer may lawfully require you to present it. Follow the specific presentation instructions and keep it available for official procedures. Ordinary residence-card penalties and rules should not be applied to this separate certificate system without checking the applicable law.

## Renewal depends on age and the certificate's issue history

Certificate validity rules changed on **June 14, 2026**. For certificates issued under the new rules:

- For renewal by a holder aged 18 or older, validity generally runs to the tenth birthday after the existing certificate's expiry.
- For renewal by a holder under 18, validity generally runs to the fifth birthday after the existing certificate's expiry. Special treatment applies when the existing expiry falls at age 18 or later; confirm the applicable ten-birthday rule with the municipality.
- Other issuance situations count birthdays from the application or notification date rather than the old certificate's expiry. Certificates issued under the prior system retain their printed expiry date.

Confirm the exact deadline and application window for your age and certificate history rather than relying on older advice about a seven-year cycle.

Check the expiration date printed on your certificate and ask your municipality when to apply. The municipality can confirm the window for your exact certificate, including the transition rules for documents issued before the June 2026 change. Apply early enough to avoid an expired certificate and carry the required documents for your municipal application.

## Leaving Japan and returning

If you plan to return to Japan, confirm the correct re-entry procedure before departure. Under the special re-entry system, an eligible Special Permanent Resident who declares an intention to return can generally re-enter within **two years** using the required valid passport and Special Permanent Resident Certificate. A separate re-entry permit may be available for a longer absence, generally up to **six years**. Check the approved permit's expiry date and plan separately for passport or certificate renewal; do not assume one document's validity automatically changes the other.

These are maximum periods, not a reason to wait until the deadline. If you may be away longer, or your passport or certificate expires during the trip, contact ISA before leaving. See [Re-entry permission and temporary travel](/articles/reentry-permission-and-temporary-travel) for the general process and travel checklist.

## Which guide applies to you?

- If you hold this special statutory position, use this guide and confirm certificate questions with your municipality.
- If you were granted ordinary Permanent Resident status, use the [Permanent Residence guide](/articles/permanent-residence-in-japan), including its application and cancellation sections.
- If you hold Long-Term Resident status, use the [Long-Term Resident guide](/articles/long-term-resident-status); it has a limited period and its own renewal assessment.

Do not assume a change between these categories is automatic. Eligibility, applications, documentation, and consequences differ.
