## “English teacher” is not one immigration category

English teacher can describe work under several Statuses of Residence ([在留資格](/glossary/zairyu-shikaku), **zairyū shikaku**). Ask where and how the person will teach, under which contract, before choosing a route.

## The three main routes

### University or equivalent higher education: Professor

Professor ([教授](/glossary/kyoju), **kyōju**) is the first route to examine for education, research, or research guidance at a university, equivalent institution, or college of technology. Titles include university English lecturer, language instructor, professor, visiting faculty, and some academic research appointments.

[Learn more about Professor status →](/articles/professor-status)

### Covered schools: Instructor

Instructor ([教育](/glossary/kyoiku), **kyōiku**) is the first route to examine for language or other education at the school categories named in the official definition. Common titles include English teacher, school subject teacher, and Assistant Language Teacher ([外国語指導助手](/glossary/gaikokugo-shido-joshu), **gaikokugo shidō joshu**), usually ALT.

[Learn more about Instructor status →](/articles/instructor-status)

### Private-company language teaching: Engineer/Specialist

Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services ([技術・人文知識・国際業務](/glossary/gijutsu-jinbun-kokusai), **gijutsu, jinbun chishiki, kokusai gyōmu**) includes private-company language teachers as an official example. This can include some English-conversation ([英会話](/glossary/eikaiwa), **eikaiwa**) schools and corporate language training.

[Learn more about Engineer/Specialist status →](/articles/engineer-specialist-humanities-international-services-status)

## Compare common settings

### University English lecturer

**Examine first:** Professor.

**Confirm:** The institution, appointment, teaching and research duties, schedule, remuneration, and any other paid work.

### Direct-hire school teacher or ALT

**Examine first:** Instructor.

**Confirm:** The school's category, employer, duties, subject, qualifications, worksite, and contract.

### Dispatched ALT

**Examine first:** Instructor is an important comparison, but ALT alone does not settle the route.

**Confirm:** The actual employer, dispatch or service structure, placements, direction of the work, contracts, and non-teaching duties.

### Private eikaiwa instructor

**Examine first:** Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services.

**Confirm:** The private-company relationship, language-instruction duties, qualification path, remuneration, and any mixed service work.

### International-school teacher

**Examine first:** The institution's formal classification.

**Confirm:** Whether it is legally a listed or equivalent school. The English name “international school” does not establish this.

### Kindergarten or preschool teacher

**Examine first:** An individual institution-and-activity classification.

**Confirm:** The institution type, childcare versus education duties, qualifications, and any adjacent residence-status routes.

### Cram school, tutoring, or after-school program

**Examine first:** The formal institution and private-company relationship.

**Confirm:** The employer, principal duties, worksite, and whether the activity fits private-company language instruction or another route.

### Online teacher or private tutor

**Examine first:** The person's current status and contract structure.

**Confirm:** The Japanese contracting organization, self-employment arrangements, work location, principal activity, and any outside-activity permission.

### Teacher working for several institutions

**Examine first:** The principal residence-status route together with every contract.

**Confirm:** Each institution's category, worksite, hours, income, and whether the activities cross residence-status categories.

## ALT does not settle the answer

ALT is a job description, not a residence status. Record the legal employer, any dispatch or service company, every school, who directs the work, classroom and non-classroom duties, contract duration, remuneration, and the qualification path. Do not rely on a recruiter saying only that it will “sponsor a teaching visa.” Ask which status will be requested and retain the submitted duty description.

## Qualifications differ by route

The three routes do not share one universal bachelor's-degree rule. Instructor uses institution- and subject-related education, license, language-of-education, or experience criteria. Professor focuses on qualifying higher-education activity and institution. Private-company language teaching uses the international-services framework, normally including related experience but with a stated exception for university graduates performing language instruction.

A degree, TEFL certificate, teaching license, experience, or native-language background may be relevant, but no single item decides every teaching route.

## Side teaching and changing jobs

A status for one setting does not automatically cover another. ISA uses a university Professor also teaching for a private company as an example requiring an outside-activity analysis. Before private students, online work, an eikaiwa shift, another appointment, or freelance curriculum work, check Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted ([資格外活動許可](/glossary/shikakugai-katsudo-kyoka), **shikakugai katsudō kyoka**).

Moving among university, covered-school, and private-company teaching can require a status change, not only an employer notification. Immigration permission, notifications, employment law, contracts, tax, and professional qualifications remain separate checks.

## Ask before signing

1. What is the institution's formal Japanese name and legal category?
2. Who is the employer, and is the arrangement direct employment, dispatch, or another contract?
3. Which status will the employer ask ISA to examine?
4. What are the teaching and non-teaching duties and percentages?
5. Where will every duty be performed?
6. Which qualification pathway and evidence apply?
7. What are the remuneration, hours, term, renewal, and termination conditions?
8. Who may submit the COE application, and will the applicant receive a copy?

The employer can support the application, but ISA issues the COE. The diplomatic mission decides the visa application, and immigration decides landing. None should be promised in a job advertisement or contract.
