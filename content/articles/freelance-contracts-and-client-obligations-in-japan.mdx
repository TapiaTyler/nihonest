> **Editorial review needed:** The Freelance Act uses definitions narrower than everyday “freelancer,” and duties depend on the parties and engagement duration. Cross-border contracts and possible employee misclassification need individualized review.

## First ask whether the Act covers this transaction

Japan's Freelance Act ([フリーランス法](/glossary/freelance-ho), **furīransu hō**) took effect November 1, 2024. It addresses fair transactions and the working environment for defined freelancers receiving Business Outsourcing ([業務委託](/glossary/gyomu-itaku), **gyōmu itaku**) from a business.

The statutory freelancer—Specified Entrusted Business Operator ([特定受託事業者](/glossary/tokutei-jutaku-jigyosha), **tokutei jutaku jigyōsha**)—is generally an individual business operator who uses no employees, or a corporation with one representative, no other officers, and no employees. Everyday freelancers who employ staff may therefore fall outside this definition.

The Act also does not simply cover every way a freelancer earns money. A consumer commissioning family photographs, a creator selling self-made products directly, work outside business outsourcing, an overseas relationship, or possible disguised employment can require another analysis.

## Do not let a contractor label remove employee rights

If the client controls working time and place, gives detailed continuous directions, restricts refusal or substitution, supplies the business structure, and pays for labor rather than an independent result, the person may need advice about worker classification. A “business outsourcing” heading or tax registration does not conclusively remove labor-law protection.

[Compare the employment-relationship guide](/articles/understanding-your-employment-contract-and-working-conditions) before relying only on the Freelance Act.

## Require written or electronic transaction terms

When covered outsourcing is made, the commissioning business must promptly state defined Transaction Terms ([取引条件](/glossary/torihiki-joken), **torihiki jōken**) in writing or electronically. Depending on the transaction, these include:

- both parties' names and the commissioning date;
- the work, deliverable, or service;
- delivery or service date and place;
- inspection-completion date when inspection applies;
- Remuneration ([報酬](/glossary/hoshu), **hōshū**) amount and payment date; and
- required details when payment is not cash.

If a legitimate reason prevents an item from being fixed initially, identify the reason and expected decision date, then supply the missing information promptly when fixed. Link every amendment to the original order. A purchase order, email, or platform record can carry terms, but an oral promise alone does not satisfy the statutory disclosure method.

## Payment normally has a sixty-day outer boundary

For covered commissioning by a business using employees, the payment date generally must be set as soon as possible within sixty days after receiving the deliverable or service, and payment must be made by that date. A defined re-outsourcing exception can use a date within thirty days after the original client’s payment date when the required upstream details were disclosed.

Do not assume the deadline moves merely because the client did not inspect promptly or the freelancer did not send an invoice. Preserve delivery, acceptance, correction, invoice, and payment records. Tax invoice requirements and the commercial payment obligation are separate.

## Longer engagements activate additional protections

For outsourcing lasting at least one month, a covered commissioning business using employees is prohibited from defined conduct, including unjustified refusal to receive, reduction of remuneration, return, substantially low pricing, compelled purchases, demands for unjust economic benefit, and uncompensated changes or rework.

Recruitment information must not be false or misleading and must be kept accurate and current. Harassment-response systems apply under the defined framework. For engagements lasting at least six months, the commissioning business must address requested compatibility with childcare or family care under the statutory standard and ordinarily give at least thirty days' notice before cancellation or nonrenewal, with reasons supplied when properly requested during the specified period. Exceptions and duration calculations require current guidance.

These thresholds do not mean conduct below them is automatically lawful. Contract law, competition law, other statutes, and possible worker status can still matter.

## Immigration and tax remain separate

A client contract, Freelance Act protection, sole-proprietor notice, tax return, or qualified invoice number does not authorize paid activity under immigration law. [Resolve the immigration question first](/articles/side-work-and-freelancing-on-a-work-status).

Then keep income and expense records and review [side-work and sole-proprietor tax](/articles/tax-for-side-work-and-sole-proprietors) and [consumption tax and invoice registration](/articles/consumption-tax-and-the-invoice-system) separately.

## If a transaction goes wrong

1. Preserve the order, terms, amendments, delivery, acceptance, invoice, communications, and payment history.
2. Identify whether the issue is missing disclosure, late payment, prohibited conduct, misleading recruitment, harassment, termination, contract interpretation, or possible employment.
3. Request the precise correction and deadline in writing.
4. Avoid surrendering unrelated confidential information or publicly accusing individuals before obtaining advice.
5. Use the current JFTC, Small and Medium Enterprise Agency, MHLW, labor, legal, or court route that matches the issue.
