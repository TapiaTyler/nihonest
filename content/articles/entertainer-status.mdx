## Entertainer is broader than performing on stage

Entertainer ([興行](/glossary/kogyo), **kōgyō**) covers theater, entertainment, music, dance, sports performances, and other entertainment activity outside Business Manager. Actors, singers, dancers, models, and professional athletes are common examples.

The current official procedure also expressly addresses advertising activity, broadcast or film production, commercial photography, and commercial recording or filming.

This wider scope is important for directors, producers, camera and production personnel, commercial photographers, and recording professionals. Their title still does not decide the route: identify the production, role, contract, organization, location, and actual duties.

## First identify the official activity category

Entertainer criteria and evidence vary substantially by activity. Some performance routes examine the performer's education or experience, the contract chain, remuneration, venue, audience capacity, food or drink service, management of the facility, and the record of the organizations involved. Other routes cover professional sports, entertainment activity outside the principal performance categories, or commercial production.

The rules were revised effective August 1, 2023. Do not use an older promoter checklist or assume a prior engagement's category still controls. Start from the current ISA page for the exact COE, change, or extension procedure.

## Map every organization and contract

Write down:

1. who employs or contracts with the applicant;
2. who invites or receives the applicant in Japan;
3. any promoter, production company, agent, dispatching body, venue operator, team, or client;
4. every performance, production, practice, shooting, recording, or work location;
5. dates, role, duties, hours, and remuneration; and
6. who directs the work and who is responsible for payment.

If contracts pass through several organizations, the complete chain matters. A short-form appearance letter may not explain the legal relationship, performance obligations, payment, deductions, or work locations sufficiently.

## Evidence varies with the work

Depending on the category, evidence can include the applicant's résumé and professional history; education or performance experience; contracts and remuneration; schedules and itineraries; promotional material; venue plans and photographs; organization registrations and financial records; staffing; prior foreign-entertainer handling; or records of the production, team, or event.

For film, advertising, photography, broadcast, or recording work, state the production and the applicant's function precisely. “Creator,” “talent,” “production staff,” or “camera work” is too broad to show which official activity is involved.

## Compare nearby routes

### Artist

Artist ([芸術](/glossary/geijutsu), **geijutsu**) covers income-producing artistic activity outside Entertainer. Independent composition, fine art, writing, or another artistic practice may require that comparison, while commercial production or performance can point back to Entertainer.

### Journalist

Reporting, news photography, or other journalism under contract with a foreign news organization can point to Journalist. A documentary style or press-like output does not establish that foreign-news relationship.

### Employed specialist work

In-house design, product, advertising, or media duties can require comparison with Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services. The organization's industry alone does not make every employee an Entertainer.

### Business management

Operating or managing a production company, agency, team, or venue is a different activity analysis. A performer who owns a company should separate the performance and management duties rather than assuming one status covers both.

Use [Which residence status fits creative and media work?](/articles/choosing-a-residence-status-for-creative-and-media-work) for the full route map.

## Period and renewal

Available periods are **three years, one year, six months, three months, or thirty days**. This differs from many other work statuses. The granted period can reflect the individual engagement and review; it is not selected by the applicant.

Extension evidence should show continuing qualifying activity, contracts, schedule, remuneration, organization facts, residence record, and compliance. A later production, venue, role, or organization may fall under a different evidence category even if the public-facing title is unchanged.

## Changes and side work

Do not treat one Entertainer permission as approval for every entertainment-related project. A new contract, additional production, artistic commission, teaching job, brand campaign, private client, or online activity can change the facts or cross a status boundary. Check the authorized activity, notification obligations, and whether outside-activity permission or a change of status is needed before the work starts.

## Questions for the applicant and receiving organization

1. Which current Entertainer activity category describes the work?
2. Is the person performing, competing, modeling, producing, filming, photographing, recording, directing, or doing another activity?
3. What is the complete contract and payment chain?
4. Which venues and work locations are involved?
5. Which applicant, organization, facility, or production criteria apply?
6. What changed since any earlier application or engagement?
7. Does any work belong to Artist, Journalist, Engineer/Specialist, or Business Manager instead?
