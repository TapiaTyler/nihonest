## What Long-Term Resident means

Long-Term Resident ([定住者](/glossary/teijusha), **teijūsha**) is status-based residence granted for special circumstances recognized by the Minister of Justice. It differs from Permanent Resident ([永住者](/glossary/eijusha), **eijūsha**): Long-Term Resident has a limited period that must be renewed.

Current official examples include third-country-resettled refugees, specified people of Japanese descent, and people connected to Japanese nationals left in China. Published notification paths also address certain spouses, supported minor unmarried biological children, and adopted children under six.

## Find the exact legal basis

The facts and evidence depend on the notification category or individual circumstances. Japanese ancestry cases can require a documented chain of birth, marriage, nationality, and family records. Family cases can depend on age, marriage, support, custody, residence history, and the status of relatives in Japan.

For the supported minor unmarried-child category, the current threshold follows Japan's age of majority. A covered new entrant must arrive before turning eighteen; a COE does not override that age-at-entry condition.

## Divorce, bereavement, and marital breakdown

Some former spouse-status holders ask to change to Long-Term Resident after divorce, bereavement, or breakdown of the relationship. ISA publishes examples of permissions and refusals involving marriage length, residence history, livelihood, care of a Japanese child, contact and support, domestic violence, and other facts.

Those examples show individual evaluation. They do not create a minimum marriage formula, guarantee status to a parent, or make divorce an application category by itself. Seek advice before the existing period expires, especially where safety, custody, or court proceedings are involved.

## Work, period, and renewal

Long-Term Resident has no activity-based occupation restriction. Available periods are **five years, three years, one year, six months, or another individually designated period not exceeding five years**. The granted period is an individual decision.

Renewal depends on the continuing basis, livelihood, family and residence circumstances, and compliance. Unrestricted work does not remove tax, insurance, licensing, or notification duties.

## Permanent Residence

The current Permanent Residence guideline has a five-year continuous-residence exception for a person residing continuously under Long-Term Resident status. This is only a residence-history exception; it does not guarantee permission or remove the other requirements.
