> **Applies to both routes:** Temporary Visitor students usually need short-stay accommodation, not a registrable long-term residence. Student-status and other mid- to long-term residents should also check the address-registration sequence below.

## Choose the arrangement, then read the contract

Start with your school’s international office. Dormitories can simplify guarantor, furniture, and utility arrangements, but may have limited spaces and house rules. Private rentals offer more choice but often involve deposits, key money, agency fees, guarantor or guarantee-company fees, insurance, and advance rent.

New employees, dependents, and other residents can ask their accepting organization, relocation contact, municipality, or real-estate agent about newcomer-friendly housing. The practical problem is the same: a standard private lease may expect a Japanese phone number, local emergency contact, guarantor or guarantee company, and a Japanese account for later rent payments before a newcomer has established all of them.

Compare the total move-in cost, commute, contract term, renewal and cancellation terms, included furniture, internet, bicycle parking, and disaster risk—not rent alone. Never pay until you know who the counterparty is and have received understandable contract terms.

## Break the address–phone–bank loop

For people who must register as residents, a workable order is often:

1. Arrange a school dormitory, employer housing, serviced apartment, share house, or other initial residence that accepts overseas applicants.
2. Confirm before booking that it can be used as your actual registered residence and can receive government and bank mail. A hotel or very short booking may not be suitable.
3. Move in and complete address registration when the resident-registration rules apply to you.
4. Use the updated residence card or other accepted address evidence to arrange a phone number and bank account.
5. If desired, apply for a conventional long-term rental after those records exist.

If you must sign a long-term lease first, ask the agent in writing whether initial costs can be paid by international transfer, card, cash at the counter, or another documented method, and whether automatic bank withdrawal can begin later. Ask whether a guarantee company, school or employer support, or an emergency contact can replace a personal guarantor. These are contractual options, not rights; the landlord, agent, payment provider, and guarantee company set their own screening rules.

> **Practical warning:** Do not register an address where you do not actually live, open an account using someone else’s address, or let another person control your account to solve the loop.

## On move-in day

Inspect every room and fixture. Photograph existing scratches, stains, mold, and damage with timestamps and submit the required condition report promptly. Confirm keys, emergency exits, locks, appliance instructions, and the property’s contact process.

Arrange electricity, gas, water, and internet as the property requires. Gas activation may require an appointment. Learn the municipality’s garbage categories, collection days, and designated bags; building rules may add requirements.

> **Practical advice:** Keep the lease, condition report, payment records, management-company contact, utility account details, and move-out notice deadline together.

Once the address is settled, complete municipal registration within the required period. Do not treat a postal forwarding request or notice to the landlord as a substitute.
