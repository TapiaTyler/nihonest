> **Editorial review needed:** Departure duties depend on whether Japanese residence, employment, and accounts actually end. Confirm national and municipal procedures for your dates and circumstances.

## First decide whether this is temporary or permanent

If you expect to return under the same residence permission, stop and use the temporary-travel guide. Re-entry Permission ([再入国許可](/glossary/sainyukoku-kyoka), *sainyūkoku kyoka*) and Special Re-entry Permission ([みなし再入国許可](/glossary/minashi-sainyukoku-kyoka), *minashi sainyūkoku kyoka*) have their own eligibility, declaration, and deadline rules.

Use this checklist when you are ending Japanese residence or when a temporary departure changed into a decision not to return.

## Build one departure timeline

Record the last day of work or study, municipal move-out date, lease handover, service closures, final salary, tax deadlines, and flight. Some obligations arise before departure; bills, tax notices, refunds, deposits, and payroll records can arrive afterward.

### Immigration and organization records

- Check whether a covered employer, school, or contracting-organization change requires a resident notification.
- Tell the employer, school, or sponsor and collect separation or completion records.
- At departure, state the permanent intention accurately and follow immigration instructions for the Residence Card ([在留カード](/glossary/zairyu-card), *zairyū kādo*).
- Keep copies of the card, passport pages, notifications, and departure evidence where lawful and secure.

If you left with re-entry permission but later decide not to return, determine when it or the status ceased to be usable. ISA states that a mid- to long-term resident who does not return within the re-entry validity generally must return the card within 14 days after that circumstance arises. Follow the current ISA return instructions rather than keeping an apparently active card indefinitely.

### Municipality, insurance, and pension

- Ask the municipality when to file the Moving-out Notification ([転出届](/glossary/tenshutsu-todoke), *tenshutsu todoke*).
- Confirm the end or continuation of municipal health-insurance and pension records.
- Return cards or certificates when instructed and provide a reliable contact address.
- Ask how later resident-tax or premium bills will be delivered and paid.

Municipal timing and documents vary. Do not assume the airport procedure closes local records.

### Tax

Confirm whether a final income-tax return or payment is due before departure. If national tax procedures will remain after leaving, determine whether to appoint a Tax Agent ([納税管理人](/glossary/nozei-kanrinin), *nōzei kanrinin*). National income tax and local resident tax are separate, and later bills may relate to income earned before departure.

### Housing and services

- give contractually required lease notice and schedule the inspection;
- document condition, keys, meter readings, and deposit deductions;
- close or transfer utilities, internet, mobile service, subscriptions, and insurance;
- arrange lawful mail handling and a reachable contact method; and
- resolve school, employer, credit, or guarantee-company obligations.

### Banking and payments

Ask each bank, card issuer, and mobile provider whether the service can remain open after nonresidence and what information must be updated. Do not misstate a Japanese address or residence to keep an account.

Avoid closing the only usable payment route too early. Final rent, utilities, card charges, tax, salary, refunds, or deposit settlement may post later. Agree on a lawful method and timing for every remaining transaction.

## Keep a departure archive

Retain notices, receipts, final statements, tax and pension records, employment or school documents, lease settlement, account closures, and the contact details of anyone handling later matters. These records may be needed for refunds, future Japanese applications, or correcting a bill after departure.
