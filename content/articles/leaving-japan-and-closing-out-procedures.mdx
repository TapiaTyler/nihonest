> **Draft departure checklist:** The correct sequence depends on whether departure is temporary or permanent, whether residence and employment end, and whether later bills or refunds remain.

## Decide what is ending

Record the last day of work or study, lease end, move-out date, and departure date. Confirm whether you are ending Japanese residence or using a valid re-entry route; do not treat a holiday and a permanent departure as the same process.

## Build the sequence around deadlines

- notify a covered school, employer, or contracting-organization change when required;
- file the municipal Moving-out Notification ([転出届](/glossary/tenshutsu-todoke), **tenshutsu todoke**) and ask how insurance, pension, and resident-tax accounts close;
- complete income-tax filing and payment or appoint a Tax Agent ([納税管理人](/glossary/nozei-kanrinin), **nōzei kanrinin**) when the applicable procedure requires it;
- arrange lease inspection, utilities, forwarding, refunds, and a reachable contact method;
- ask banks and mobile providers whether accounts may remain open after nonresidence and update details honestly; and
- settle school, employer, credit, subscription, and local-service obligations.

At the airport, follow the immigration instructions for your Residence Card ([在留カード](/glossary/zairyu-card), **zairyū kādo**) and re-entry intention. Keep departure filings, final statements, and Japanese contact details accessible after leaving because tax, pension, deposit, and payroll documents can arrive later.

## Do not close your only payment route too early

Final salary, rental settlement, card charges, tax, utilities, or refunds may post after physical departure. Coordinate timing with each provider and maintain a lawful way to receive records and settle balances; do not misstate a Japanese address or residency to keep a service.
