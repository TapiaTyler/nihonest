## Start with the relationship—not “family visa”

Japan does not have one category for every relative. The starting point depends on the principal person's nationality or exact status, the applicant's legally recognized relationship, dependency, age, place of birth, and continued residence. An invitation or financial support cannot make an uncovered relative eligible.

## Dependent

Dependent ([家族滞在](/glossary/kazoku-taizai), **kazoku taizai**) is for the supported spouse or child of a foreign resident holding an eligible principal status. It is an activity-based status for ordinary family life, not unrestricted work. The current sponsor list includes Specified Skilled Worker (ii), but not Specified Skilled Worker (i). Paid work requires separate permission; full-time work generally calls for the person's own work status.

[Learn more about Dependent status →](/articles/dependent-family-stay-visa)

## Spouse or Child of Japanese National

Spouse or Child of Japanese National ([日本人の配偶者等](/glossary/nihonjin-no-haigusha-to), **nihonjin no haigūsha tō**) covers a qualifying spouse, a person born as a child of a Japanese national, or a qualifying special adoptee. It is status-based residence without an occupation restriction, although a spouse must establish a genuine continuing marriage.

[Learn more about Spouse or Child of Japanese National →](/articles/spouse-or-child-of-japanese-national)

## Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident

Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident ([永住者の配偶者等](/glossary/eijusha-no-haigusha-to), **eijūsha no haigūsha tō**) covers a qualifying spouse of a Permanent Resident or Special Permanent Resident. Its child route is narrower: a biological child born in Japan who continued residing in Japan. A child born abroad may need another route.

[Learn more about Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident →](/articles/spouse-or-child-of-permanent-resident)

## Long-Term Resident

Long-Term Resident ([定住者](/glossary/teijusha), **teijūsha**) applies under a published notification category or an individual decision based on special circumstances. Current examples include specified people of Japanese descent, refugee-resettlement cases, and defined family situations. It is not an automatic fallback after divorce or long residence.

[Learn more about Long-Term Resident status →](/articles/long-term-resident-status)

## When circumstances change

A covered spouse holder must notify ISA within fourteen days after divorce or bereavement. That notice does not itself grant a replacement status or instantly decide the person's future. Seek qualified help early where a relationship ends, a child is involved, or domestic violence makes ordinary evidence unsafe. Personal safety comes first.

## Questions to resolve

1. What nationality or exact status does the principal person hold?
2. Is the applicant a spouse, biological child, special adoptee, or another relative?
3. Where was a child born, and have they continued residing in Japan?
4. Does the route require actual financial dependency?
5. Does the applicant plan part-time or full-time work?
6. Has divorce, bereavement, separation, or violence created an urgent issue?
