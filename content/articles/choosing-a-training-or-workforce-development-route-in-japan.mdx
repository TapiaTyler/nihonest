> **Route-finding guide:** This comparison identifies the route to examine. It does not classify an individual program or guarantee eligibility.

## Begin with what happens each day

Words such as training, internship, study, trainee, and skills program are not immigration categories by themselves. Write down:

- what the person does each day;
- whether the Japanese organization receives productive work;
- whether and why the person is paid;
- the school, instructor, employer, host, and sending organization;
- the curriculum or development plan;
- the field, work category, qualification, and tests; and
- the intended outcome after the program.

Then compare the routes below.

## Unpaid cultural or academic activity

Examine **Cultural Activities** ([文化活動](/glossary/bunka-katsudo), **bunka katsudō**) for qualifying unpaid academic or artistic activity, specialized research into culture or skills particular to Japan, or learning those skills under expert guidance.

This is not permission for ordinary paid work. Formal education at a covered school can instead point to Student, while income-producing art or professional activity can point to a work status.

[Learn more about Cultural Activities →](/articles/cultural-activities-visa)

## Education at a covered institution

Examine **Student** ([留学](/glossary/ryugaku), **ryūgaku**) when education at a qualifying Japanese school is the principal activity. The school's admission decision and immigration's Student-status decision are separate.

Practical lessons inside a genuine curriculum do not automatically turn a student into a worker. Conversely, calling productive labor a class does not settle the route. Optional part-time work requires separate outside-activity permission and remains secondary to study.

[Learn more about Student status and the COE process →](/articles/student-visa-and-certificate-of-eligibility)

## Non-employment organizational training

Examine **Training** ([研修](/glossary/kenshu), **kenshū**) for qualifying skills or knowledge acquisition through a Japanese public or private receiving organization outside Student and Technical Intern Training.

Training is not an ordinary job. Practical productive components are restricted and must fit a current exception and safeguards. The host, sending relationship, curriculum, instructors, participant treatment, prior preparation, and expected use after return are central.

[Learn more about Training status →](/articles/training-visa)

## The legacy employment-and-training program

Examine **Technical Intern Training** ([技能実習](/glossary/gino-jisshu), **ginō jisshū**) for a participant entering or continuing the regulated legacy program under an approved plan. It combines employment and skills transfer through specified stages and organizational models.

The program remains active under transitional rules even after its successor begins on April 1, 2027. Plan dates and current stage decide whether a person can continue; the new law does not automatically rename every participant.

[Learn more about Technical Intern Training and transition planning →](/articles/technical-intern-training-status)

## Employment designed to reach SSW skill level

Examine **Employment for Skill Development** ([育成就労](/glossary/ikusei-shuro), **ikusei shūrō**) for the successor program beginning April 1, 2027. It ordinarily uses three years of planned employment to develop a worker toward Specified Skilled Worker (i) skill and Japanese levels in a covered field.

This program has approved plans, field-specific rules, accepting and supervising support organizations, assessments, protections, and conditional transfers. Preparatory applications in 2026 do not permit early participation.

[Learn more about Employment for Skill Development →](/articles/employment-for-skill-development-system)

## Direct field-based employment

Examine **Specified Skilled Worker** ([特定技能](/glossary/tokutei-gino), **tokutei ginō**) when the person can satisfy the skills, Japanese, field, employment, employer, and support requirements for direct work in a designated industrial field.

SSW is not a course. A qualifying Technical Intern Training or Employment for Skill Development history can support the transition or examination treatment, but neither guarantees the new status. Some SSW fields do not have a corresponding successor-program field.

[Learn more about Specified Skilled Worker →](/articles/specified-skilled-worker-status)

## Ordinary professional or other employment

A job can involve onboarding or skill development without belonging to a special training program. Examine the appropriate professional, skilled, business, care, or other work status when the principal activity is ordinary remunerated work fitting that status.

For example, a transferred corporate specialist can require Intra-company Transferee; a software developer can require Engineer/Specialist; and a foreign-cuisine specialist can require Skilled Labor. Use [Choosing a work status and COE](/articles/choosing-a-work-status-and-coe) for the duty-first comparison.

[Learn more about choosing an activity-based work status →](/articles/choosing-a-work-status-and-coe)

## Quick comparison

- **Unpaid Japanese cultural study or research:** Cultural Activities may be the first route to examine.
- **Education at a qualifying school:** Student may be the first route to examine.
- **Structured non-employment training through a host:** Training may be the first route to examine.
- **A current approved legacy technical-intern plan:** Technical Intern Training controls, including transition provisions.
- **Three-year employment development beginning from April 2027:** Employment for Skill Development may apply in a covered field.
- **Direct work after meeting field skill and language requirements:** Specified Skilled Worker may apply.
- **Ordinary specialized or other work:** compare the activity-based employment statuses.

## Questions for the school, host, or employer

Ask the organization to identify the exact Japanese status or program, plan, field and work category, daily duties, payment basis, period, progression conditions, tests, support, transfer rules, and end-of-program outcome. Request official links and copies of the submitted terms.

An organization can support truthful documents but cannot guarantee a COE, visa, landing permission, extension, change, or later transition. Immigration approval also does not replace labor law, school regulation, professional licensing, tax, insurance, or sector rules.
