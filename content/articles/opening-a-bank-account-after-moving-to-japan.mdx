> **Resident-focused step:** This article is for people establishing residence in Japan. A Temporary Visitor should not plan around opening a standard resident bank account; arrange cards, cash access, tuition payment, and emergency funds that work from abroad.

## Pick an account that fits your actual needs

Ask your school, employer, or accepting organization whether it recommends a bank for tuition, scholarships, payroll, or local payments. Compare branch and ATM access, fees, online banking languages, international transfers, cash-card delivery, and whether the bank can support your name as shown on official identity records.

Banks perform identity and purpose checks and set their own account-opening procedures. Commonly requested records can include a residence card with current address, contact details, student or enrollment evidence, and information about the expected use of the account. A Japanese phone number or particular payment/address history may be required by some institutions, not all. Check the chosen bank’s current official list rather than treating any checklist as universal.

## If housing asks for a bank account first

Do not assume either side will waive its checks. Explain to the agent that you are newly arrived and ask whether you can:

- pay move-in costs and the first rent by an accepted international transfer, card, cash, or payment slip;
- provide the Japanese account after address registration;
- use school, employer, relocation, or guarantee-company support; or
- begin with housing designed for new arrivals and move after the account is open.

The reverse sequence is common because the bank may need a residence card showing the current Japanese address, while a mobile plan or rental application may also ask for Japanese payment details. A realistic setup plan therefore includes temporary payment access from abroad and enough funds for deposits and living costs before local banking works.

> **Practical guidance:** Complete resident registration as soon as required, put your name on the mailbox, and use the same official name format consistently. Bank cards and verification letters may be sent to the registered address and may not be forwarded.

Some institutions restrict available services based on how long you have lived in Japan, employment or enrollment evidence, remaining period of stay, or their assessment under banking rules. Ask what account or remittance services are available now and what can change later; do not state that every newcomer must simply wait six months.

## Protect the account

Use the account only for legitimate personal transactions. Never sell, rent, lend, or give another person the account, cash card, PIN, passbook, login, or one-time codes. Be cautious of messages asking you to receive or forward money for someone else.

> **Official guidance:** Tell the financial institution when your address, residence period, status, school, or other requested customer information changes. Respond to legitimate review requests so transactions are not restricted.

Keep opening documents and fee information. Before permanently leaving Japan, settle payments and follow the bank’s closure procedure instead of abandoning the account.
