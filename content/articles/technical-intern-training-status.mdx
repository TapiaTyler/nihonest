## A current program with a scheduled successor

Technical Intern Training ([技能実習](/glossary/gino-jisshu), **ginō jisshū**) remains a current status and regulated program while Japan prepares to begin Employment for Skill Development ([育成就労](/glossary/ikusei-shuro), **ikusei shūrō**) on **April 1, 2027**.

Do not describe Technical Intern Training as ordinary employment, an internship chosen freely from any employer, or a program that has already disappeared. A participant's approved plan, route type, occupation and work, organizations, stage, tests, and dates determine what can continue and which transition rules apply.

## The plan and organizations control the route

The activity combines instruction with work that develops the approved skill. It generally uses one of two organizational models:

- **Individual-enterprise type:** a Japanese organization accepts a participant through a qualifying overseas business relationship and implements the approved plan.
- **Supervising-organization type:** an authorized supervising organization receives participants and supervises implementing organizations that conduct the plan.

The implementing organization is the workplace. The supervising organization, when used, has separate supervision and support duties. Neither can substitute a different job, worksite, pay arrangement, or plan merely because all parties agree informally.

## Stages and maximum program shape

The program has Technical Intern Training 1, 2, and 3, with individual-enterprise and supervising-organization subtypes. Stage 1 ordinarily covers the first year. A qualifying move to stage 2 can cover the next two years, and stage 3 can cover a further two years for eligible participants and approved organizations. This creates a possible **five-year** program shape, not an automatic five-year permission.

Each stage and move requires the applicable plan, examination, status procedure, organization conditions, and timing. The period of stay is individually designated within the applicable stage limit. A participant cannot simply renew one stage indefinitely.

## Documents and conditions to check before entry

Ask for understandable copies of:

- the employment contract and working conditions, including wages, deductions, hours, overtime, holidays, and payment date;
- the approved training plan, occupation, work category, daily duties, stage, worksite, and instructors;
- the implementing and supervising organizations and reliable contact details;
- housing, utilities, food, insurance, travel, and every expected deduction or fee;
- the sending-organization arrangement and any payment made before departure;
- required courses, examinations, stage-change conditions, and return periods; and
- complaint, emergency, transfer, and early-ending procedures.

The actual work must match the approved plan. A recruiter or intermediary should not promise an unrelated job, unrestricted employer changes, guaranteed stage progression, or automatic transfer to another status.

## You are a worker with labor protections

Technical interns are covered by Japanese labor protections during employment. Warning signs include unpaid wages or overtime, work different from the contract or plan, violence or threats, unsafe conditions, excessive or unexplained deductions, confiscation of a passport or residence card, restrictions on leaving accommodation, or pressure to return home against the person's will.

The Organization for Technical Intern Training provides multilingual information, consultation, an SOS channel, reporting, and support concerning training-site changes. A participant can seek independent help; the employer or supervising organization is not the only source of information.

Marriage, pregnancy, childbirth, illness, injury, or a complaint does not by itself authorize forced resignation or return. Obtain current advice promptly because residence, wages, accommodation, medical care, and transfer timing can interact.

## Changing the training site

Technical Intern Training is plan-specific, so changing workplaces is not the same as changing an ordinary job. However, a transfer can be available when unavoidable circumstances make continued training difficult. Current examples and support address abuse, serious labor violations, plan mismatch, or other circumstances recognized by the program.

Tell OTIT or another independent official contact if the responsible organization does not act. ISA also has measures for eligible participants maintaining residence during a transfer procedure. Do not disappear from the worksite or begin work elsewhere without advice and the required plan and immigration steps.

A separate opportunity can exist when moving from stage 2 to stage 3 through the approved framework. That is not a general right to select any employer or occupation.

## Transition to Specified Skilled Worker

Successful completion of Technical Intern Training 2 can support transition to Specified Skilled Worker ([特定技能](/glossary/tokutei-gino), **tokutei ginō**) category (i) and exemptions from some skills or Japanese tests when the completed skill and proposed field or work category have the required relationship. A completion label alone does not establish that relationship.

The new employer, employment contract, field, support plan, status application, and any remaining tests must independently satisfy Specified Skilled Worker rules. Read [Specified Skilled Worker status](/articles/specified-skilled-worker-status) before assuming a direct progression.

## What happens around April 1, 2027

The new Employment for Skill Development system replaces Technical Intern Training prospectively, but transition provisions preserve some legacy participation:

- a person already conducting Technical Intern Training on April 1, 2027 can continue under the applicable approved plan;
- certain Technical Intern Training plan applications made by March 31, 2027 can support a program start through June 30, 2027, subject to the official transition rules;
- movement from stage 2 to stage 3 has additional effective-date and prior-participation conditions; and
- qualifying completion of Technical Intern Training 2 can continue to support a Specified Skilled Worker transition during the transition period.

Do not choose a route from the calendar alone. Plan approval date, actual start, current stage, time already completed, occupation, and target status all matter. Use [Employment for Skill Development](/articles/employment-for-skill-development-system) for the successor rules.

## Renewal and ending participation

Continuation is limited by the approved stage and total program structure. Preserve wage statements, attendance, training and test records, plan changes, support requests, and communications. The implementing and supervising organizations have their own records and reports, but the participant should retain accessible copies.

If training ends, confirm the immigration, final-pay, housing, insurance, tax, departure, transfer, or new-status steps before the last day. Neither a promised transfer nor an unresolved complaint automatically extends the authorized period of stay.
