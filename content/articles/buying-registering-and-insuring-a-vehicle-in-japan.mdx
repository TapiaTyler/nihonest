> **Budget for ownership, not only purchase:** The seller can assist with paperwork, but the buyer should independently confirm the registered owner and user, parking, inspection, taxes, insurance, financing, and transfer deadline before paying.

## Confirm driving and parking first

Decide who will own, register, insure, and drive the vehicle. A valid residence status, address, purchase contract, or insurance quote does not create driving authority; start with the [license route](/articles/driving-in-japan-with-a-foreign-or-international-license).

Many ordinary-vehicle procedures require evidence that an appropriate parking space is available, commonly called a Garage Certificate ([車庫証明](/glossary/shako-shomei), **shako shōmei**). Applicability and police procedure depend on the vehicle and location. A lease saying “parking available” is not necessarily the certificate or owner consent required for registration. Confirm dimensions, access, monthly cost, distance rules, and paperwork with the local police and property manager.

## Map every party and record

Before purchase, record:

- seller and current registered owner;
- buyer, intended registered owner, and principal user;
- vehicle identification, plate, inspection status, liens or finance, and disclosed condition;
- transfer documents, signatures or seals, and who files each step;
- parking-space procedure;
- registration, tax, recycling, insurance, and agent charges; and
- delivery only after lawful registration and coverage are confirmed.

Ordinary vehicles and light vehicles do not use identical organizations or procedures. A private sale, dealer sale, inherited vehicle, corporate vehicle, financed vehicle, motorcycle, address change, and move between jurisdictions can also differ. Use the current MLIT inspection and registration portal, the relevant light-vehicle authority, prefectural tax information, and police instructions.

## Keep inspection current

The periodic motor-vehicle inspection is commonly called Shaken ([車検](/glossary/shaken), **shaken**). Inspection confirms compliance at the inspection point; it does not replace routine maintenance or guarantee that every later fault is covered.

Check the electronic or paper inspection record and its expiry before purchase. Budget for inspection fees, required maintenance or repairs, vehicle weight tax, compulsory insurance, and administrative service. Do not drive after inspection or insurance expiry.

## Understand the two insurance layers

Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance ([自賠責保険](/glossary/jibaiseki-hoken), **jibaiseki hoken**) is required for covered motor vehicles and provides a minimum framework for injury or death to other people. It ordinarily does not cover property damage, repairs to your vehicle, or every loss for which you could be liable.

Voluntary Automobile Insurance ([任意保険](/glossary/nini-hoken), **nin'i hoken**) can address selected liability, property, collision, injury, legal-support, roadside, driver, and vehicle risks according to the policy. It does not replace compulsory insurance. Compare named drivers, age and use restrictions, commuting or business use, household coverage, deductibles, limits, exclusions, replacement transport, and what happens when another person drives.

## Maintain records through disposal

Keep the registration and inspection record, compulsory-insurance certificate, voluntary policy, tax and parking records, maintenance history, purchase and finance documents, accident records, and every address or ownership change.

When selling, scrapping, exporting, or giving away the vehicle, complete the correct transfer or deregistration process and confirm taxes, plates, insurance cancellation, recycling, and finance. Handing over the keys alone may leave records and liabilities in the old owner's name.
