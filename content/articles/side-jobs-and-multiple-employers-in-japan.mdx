## Separate employed side work from freelancing

Side Jobs and Concurrent Employment ([副業・兼業](/glossary/fukugyo-kengyo), **fukugyō・kengyō**) can include a second employment contract, an independent client contract, business operation, an officer role, or another activity. This guide focuses on **two or more employed roles**. Freelance commissioning uses a different contract and protection analysis.

Before continuing, write down for each role:

- the legal employer, worksite, and any dispatch company;
- employee or contractor classification;
- duties, schedule, actual hours, and travel between workplaces;
- pay, benefits, insurance, equipment, clients, and confidential information; and
- immigration treatment and any permission or notification.

## A model rule is not a universal entitlement

MHLW's model Rules of Employment ([就業規則](/glossary/shugyo-kisoku), **shūgyō kisoku**) permit workers to engage in other work outside working hours, while allowing restrictions for reasons such as interference with the main job, disclosure of secrets, competition, damage to trust, or legal and health concerns.

That model is guidance, not the text of every employer's policy. Read the rules and contract that actually apply. Determine whether advance notice, consultation, or approval is required and what information must be supplied. An employer restriction is not automatically valid merely because it is written, but a worker also should not promise secrecy about facts needed to manage working time or health.

## Immigration comes before starting the second activity

Two jobs that look similar do not automatically have the same immigration treatment. Compare the second role's actual duties and organization with the current status. If it is outside the authorized activity, advance Permission to Engage in Activity Outside the Status Scope ([資格外活動許可](/glossary/shikakugai-katsudo-kyoka), **shikakugai katsudō kyoka**) may be required; if it becomes the principal activity, a status change may be the relevant route.

[Use the immigration-focused side-work guide](/articles/side-work-and-freelancing-on-a-work-status) before accepting or beginning the second role. Employer consent, when required by a workplace rule or an immigration criterion, does not itself grant immigration permission.

## Working time can cross employer boundaries

Defined Working Hours ([労働時間](/glossary/rodo-jikan), **rōdō jikan**) under separate employment contracts can require aggregation for Labor Standards Act purposes. The order in which contracts were formed and the order in which work occurs can affect which employer bears particular premium-pay obligations under the ordinary method. MHLW also describes a voluntary management model intended to reduce administrative burden.

Do not calculate this from total weekly hours alone. Each employer needs a lawful method to learn the information reasonably necessary to manage hours and health. Keep your own daily record across both jobs, including travel, overnight work, schedule changes, and days off. An Agreement 36 ([36協定](/glossary/saburoku-kyotei), **saburōku kyōtei**) at one workplace does not authorize another employer's overtime or eliminate aggregate health concerns.

## Manage health and conflicts deliberately

Before combining schedules, test whether you retain genuine breaks, statutory days off, sleep, commuting time, and recovery. Report fatigue, medical restrictions, or unsafe scheduling through an appropriate route. Neither employer should assume the other job is risk-free simply because it occurs outside its clock.

Protect confidential information, personal data, employer equipment, intellectual property, client relationships, and competitive boundaries. Keep devices and repositories separate. Ask how inventions or work products created outside scheduled hours are treated before beginning a related project.

## Insurance and tax require their own review

Employee health and pension, employment insurance, and workers’ compensation do not all combine multiple workplaces in the same way. A work or commuting injury— including qualifying travel between workplaces—needs a workers’ compensation assessment. [Review the employment-insurance and work-injury guide](/articles/employment-insurance-social-insurance-and-work-injury).

Each employer may withhold tax, but one payroll year-end adjustment may not reconcile both salaries or other income. [Use the tax filing guide](/articles/filing-a-japanese-income-tax-return) and preserve both employers' withholding slips.

## Before starting the second job

1. Classify both relationships and obtain written terms.
2. Check both workplace policies and disclose what is lawfully necessary.
3. Resolve immigration permission before work begins.
4. Agree how schedules and actual hours will be reported.
5. Check conflicts, confidentiality, health, and equipment boundaries.
6. Confirm insurance and tax treatment with the responsible organizations.
7. Reassess when either job's duties, hours, employer, or priority changes.
