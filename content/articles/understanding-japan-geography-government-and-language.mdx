# Understanding Japan: geography, local government, and language

Japan is an archipelago with national rules, local governments, and communities that can differ substantially by place. A region name helps you orient yourself, but most practical tasks require an exact address, responsible office, and current local instructions.

## Start with the physical map

Japan's four largest islands are Hokkaido, Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu, alongside many smaller islands. The country extends far enough north to south that climate ranges broadly from subarctic to subtropical conditions. Seasons, transport, ecosystems, and exposure to snow, typhoons, flooding, tsunami, volcanoes, and other hazards cannot be generalized from Tokyo.

For a move, check the exact community rather than relying on a national description. Compare climate and transport at the intended address, and use current Japan Meteorological Agency information plus the municipality's hazard maps for live or location-specific decisions.

## Regions are useful labels, not one level of government

A [region](/glossary/chiho) ([地方](/glossary/chiho), **chihō**) is a useful way to organize geography, weather, culture, transport, or statistics. Common groupings include Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto, Chubu, Kansai or Kinki, Chugoku, Shikoku, Kyushu, and Okinawa.

These boundaries are not universal. An agency, industry, or publication may group a prefecture differently for a particular purpose. A region name therefore does not identify one regional government or one office that controls every procedure.

## Prefectures and municipalities do different work

[Prefectures](/glossary/todofuken) ([都道府県](/glossary/todofuken), **todōfuken**) are Japan's 47 prefectural-level governments. The English word “prefecture” is a convenient umbrella for Tokyo-to, Hokkaido-do, Kyoto-fu and Osaka-fu, and the other forty-three ken.

[Municipalities](/glossary/shichoson) ([市町村](/glossary/shichoson), **shichōson**) include cities, towns, and villages. They commonly handle address registration, resident records, local insurance procedures, waste collection, and other address-specific services. Prefectural governments have separate responsibilities, while national agencies control national systems. A school, employer, landlord, or private provider can add another distinct layer.

Tokyo's 23 [special wards](/glossary/tokubetsu-ku) ([特別区](/glossary/tokubetsu-ku), **tokubetsu-ku**) have municipal status for many purposes. A ward inside a government-designated city such as Nagoya is instead part of that city and may operate local service counters. Do not choose an office from the English word “ward” alone.

For any procedure, identify:

1. the exact address or event;
2. whether the rule is national, prefectural, municipal, or private;
3. the named authority or organization;
4. the current form, deadline, and office; and
5. whether moving or changing circumstances changes responsibility.

## Japanese varies by place and situation

[Common Japanese](/glossary/kyotsugo) ([共通語](/glossary/kyotsugo), **kyōtsūgo**) is broadly useful across Japan and is an appropriate starting point for most learners. Local conversations may also include a regional [dialect](/glossary/hogen) ([方言](/glossary/hogen), **hōgen**) with different pronunciation, vocabulary, or grammar.

You do not need to imitate a local dialect to participate in community life. Listen, ask what an unfamiliar expression means, and learn useful local forms gradually. Speech does not divide neatly into one dialect per prefecture: it can cross borders and vary by generation, setting, and speaker.

Ainu and Ryukyuan varieties require particular care. Official and scholarly classifications do not always draw the language-versus-dialect boundary in the same way, and several varieties are endangered. Treat them as living languages and community heritage—not novelty examples or stereotypes.

## Use the exact place and a live authority

Start with current official information, then narrow it to your situation:

- use the [Foreign Residents Support Portal](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/support/portal/) and its multilingual living guide for national orientation;
- use the exact prefecture, municipality, or international association for local procedures and language support;
- use [JMA's multilingual information](https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/kokusai/m_multi.html) for live weather, earthquake, tsunami, and volcano information;
- use the [Statistics Bureau's current handbook](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/handbook/index.html) for national facts and maps; and
- follow focused Nihonest guidance for immigration, work, taxes, healthcare, municipal procedures, housing, emergencies, and daily life.

A national overview can tell you what to investigate. It cannot identify the correct counter, current deadline, hazard response, or local service without your exact place and circumstances.
