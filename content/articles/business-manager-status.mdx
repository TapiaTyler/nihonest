## What Business Manager authorizes

Business Manager ([経営・管理](/glossary/keiei-kanri), **keiei kanri**) covers actually operating a qualifying business in Japan or managing that business or a qualifying business division. Common settings include a founder directing a Japanese company, an executive responsible for a Japanese operation, or a senior manager with substantive authority.

A title, shareholding, or investment does not establish the activity by itself. Immigration examines what the applicant will decide and manage, the organization beneath that role, the business premises and resources, and whether the operation is real and sustainable. Hands-on professional or production work may need a separate status analysis even when the person owns the company.

## The requirements changed in October 2025

For applications under the criteria effective **October 16, 2025**, the business must satisfy a combined set of requirements:

- maintain an actual place of business in Japan appropriate to the proposed operation;
- employ at least one qualifying full-time employee;
- have at least **¥30 million** in qualifying business assets, capital, contributions, or investment, calculated under the rule for the business form;
- have Japanese-language capacity around JF Standard B2 through the applicant or a qualifying full-time employee;
- show that the applicant has a qualifying graduate-level degree connected to management or the business field, or at least three years of relevant business-management experience;
- submit a concrete business plan evaluated by an appropriate professional; and
- operate consistently with applicable licensing, tax, labor, social-insurance, and other legal obligations.

These requirements work together. The former shorthand of ¥5 million or two employees is outdated for a new application.

### What the numbers do—and do not—mean

The ¥30 million requirement is not simply a personal bank-balance test. Current rules examine qualifying property used for the business, with the calculation depending on whether the operation is a corporation or an individual business. Prepare a traceable record of contributions or investment, ownership, transfers, capitalization, and how the funds or assets support the operation.

Not every employee counts toward the staffing requirement. The official rule focuses on a qualifying full-time employee, generally a Japanese national, Special Permanent Resident, or foreign resident with an eligible status based on personal position. A contractor, dispatched worker, nominal hire, or employee holding an activity-based status should not be assumed to satisfy it.

The Japanese-capacity requirement is not necessarily applicant-only. Either the manager or a qualifying full-time employee can provide the capacity, but the evidence and the person's real continuing role must be clear.

## Evidence should tell one business story

Prepare evidence in connected categories rather than treating the application as a pile of forms:

- **Activity and authority:** proposed position, decision-making power, organizational chart, responsibilities, and time spent managing rather than performing unrelated work.
- **Business substance:** products or services, customers, contracts or credible demand, suppliers, pricing, operating plan, projections, and required licenses.
- **Scale and funding:** incorporation and ownership records, qualifying assets or capital, source and movement of funds, financial statements where available, and hiring budget.
- **Premises:** lease, permission to use the site for the business, layout, equipment, and separation from a home or shared facility where that becomes relevant.
- **People:** qualifying full-time employee, employment terms, payroll and enrollment records, Japanese-language evidence, and a plausible staffing plan.
- **Applicant background:** degree or management history, prior responsibilities, and evidence connecting that experience to the proposed role.
- **Plan review and compliance:** the required professional evaluation plus tax, labor, insurance, corporate, and licensing records appropriate to the stage of the business.

Use the current checklist for the exact procedure—COE, change, or extension—and organization category. ISA can request additional material.

## Start-up preparation is a different route

The supported Start-up program can give an approved entrepreneur time to prepare before applying to change to Business Manager. It uses a designated-activities framework, local or approved-organization review, support, and monitoring. It neither waives the current Business Manager criteria nor promises the later result.

Use [Japan's Start-up Visa pathway](/articles/startup-visa) when the business cannot yet document the established-operation requirements. Incorporating a company or receiving local plan confirmation is not the same as receiving Business Manager permission.

## Periods, renewal, and business changes

Available periods are **five years, three years, one year, six months, four months, or three months**. Immigration decides the period; meeting the route definition does not guarantee a particular duration.

Renewal examines the continuing business and management activity, finances, premises, staffing, remuneration or livelihood, required filings, taxes, labor and social-insurance compliance, and the accuracy of prior applications. A company can exist on paper while the authorized activity has weakened, so retain operating records throughout the period.

Current transition guidance applies to people already holding Business Manager when the revised criteria took effect. Through **October 16, 2028**, ISA states that renewals are assessed using the business situation and prospects of meeting the new criteria rather than treating one unmet new item as an automatic refusal. Later applications are expected to satisfy the revised framework, subject to the current official guidance.

A new company, additional company, major role change, closure, long interruption, or shift from management into hands-on work can alter the immigration analysis. Confirm the required status or notification procedure before making the change.

## Before relying on the route

Separate the legal layers. Immigration permission does not replace company registration, professional licensing, tax, employment, social-insurance, lease, or sector-specific compliance. A support organization, accountant, or business consultant can help within their field but cannot guarantee an immigration decision. Use current ISA instructions and qualified advice for the actual structure and filing date.
