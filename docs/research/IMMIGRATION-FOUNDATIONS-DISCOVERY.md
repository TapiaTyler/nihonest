# Immigration Foundations Discovery

## Executive finding

The six existing immigration-foundation guides should be revised as one connected lifecycle, not as independent introductions. The clearest user model is:

1. identify the intended activity or basis of residence;
2. identify the matching status of residence and evidence path;
3. obtain a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) where that route uses one;
4. apply for a visa abroad when required;
5. request landing permission at entry;
6. maintain the granted status and period of stay; and
7. use the correct notification, extension, change, re-entry, or departure procedure when circumstances change.

Each item is a separate decision or record. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs explains that a visa is one condition for applying to land and does not guarantee entry. It also states that a COE does not guarantee visa issuance. Since March 2023, an emailed COE or printed copy can be presented for the visa and landing processes.^1 ^2

Temporary travel is the main missing foundation. It should become a dedicated, reusable article rather than an appendix to permanent departure. The guide must treat re-entry permission and the underlying period of stay as separate clocks. Special re-entry ordinarily expires at the earlier of one year after departure and the current period-of-stay expiry; it also has route and enforcement exclusions.^3

This document proposes the scope for Iteration 3B. It is not publishable legal guidance, and every resulting article should remain `needs-review` until explicitly approved.

## The entry and residence decision chain

### Visa and landing permission

A Japanese visa is issued by an embassy or consulate outside Japan. It confirms passport validity and indicates that the holder may present themselves for landing examination, but it is not landing permission and does not guarantee entry.^1 This distinction should lead the overview article because informal phrases such as “work visa” otherwise collapse several different decisions.

For a long-term stay, MOFA generally recommends beginning with a COE obtained through a proxy in Japan. The COE supports the visa and landing processes by certifying that the proposed activity fits a landing-condition category, but MOFA expressly states that it does not guarantee visa issuance. A valid visa likewise does not guarantee landing.^2

The current ordinary COE validity is three months. Earlier pandemic extensions are historical exceptions and should not be repeated as current general guidance.^4 A changed employer, school, activity, passport, or other material fact after issuance should trigger an official check rather than an assumption that the old record remains usable.

### Status of residence and period of stay

Landing permission establishes the status of residence and period of stay under which the person may remain. These should be rendered as separate concepts: a status controls the permitted activity or position; a period controls the current time limit. The residence card records important granted facts for mid-to-long-term residents, but it is not a substitute for understanding the permission itself.

The existing structured-status pilot should supply route definitions, available periods, work boundaries, qualifications, organizations, and transitions. The foundation article should explain how to use those records without duplicating them.

### Long-term arrival

Arrival guidance should verify passport, visa where required, COE presentation, supporting itinerary and contact details, and consistency of the intended activity. After landing, mid-to-long-term residents must follow the residence-card and address-registration process. ISA instructs a new mid-to-long-term resident to notify the municipality within 14 days after establishing a place of residence.^5

That is the correct handoff point. My Number, insurance, pension, banking, housing, and phone setup are cross-route arrival guides rather than immigration-foundation sections.

## Choosing and maintaining an activity-based status

### Actual work, not the job title

The work-status guide should ask, in order:

- What will the person actually do most of the time?
- Which Japanese organization, contract, workplace, or institution supports the activity?
- Does the route require a degree, experience, examination, Japanese professional license, sector test, or other qualification?
- Does remuneration or employment structure meet route-specific rules?
- Are meaningful duties excluded or better matched to an adjacent status?
- Is a COE, in-country change, or another application context appropriate?

This is deliberately not an eligibility calculator. A title such as “teacher,” “developer,” “consultant,” or “manager” cannot decide the result without duties and organizational facts.

### Extension versus change

An extension continues the existing basis of residence; a change requests permission for a different qualifying activity or position. ISA accepts an ordinary extension application before expiry and, for a person with a period of six months or more, generally from three months before expiry. A change application may be filed after the reason for change arises and before the current period expires.^6 ^7

Both remain discretionary decisions. Filing a change should not be described as permission to begin an activity prohibited by the current status. Route-specific pages should say what official page must be checked for the current documents instead of copying a universal checklist.

### The special pending-application period

When a qualifying extension or change application is filed by the old expiry date but remains undecided, the person may remain under the old status until the decision or two months after the old expiry date, whichever comes first.^8 This is not a guaranteed two-month extension and should be named consistently as the special period (`特例期間`, *tokurei kikan*).

Travel during a pending application requires a prominent shared warning. ISA says travel is possible with ordinary or special re-entry arrangements, but the applicant should remain reachable. Under special re-entry, the return deadline becomes the earliest applicable deadline; after return, the applicant must receive the decision before the special period ends.^9

### Stopping work or study

The current status does not simply disappear when a job or school relationship ends, but inactivity is not consequence-free. ISA describes revocation exposure where a person with an Appended Table I status does not carry out the status activity continuously for three months without a justifiable reason; Highly Skilled Professional (ii) uses six months. It also identifies cases involving another activity undertaken or intended while the person is not performing the authorized one.^10

The eventual copy must avoid a false “90-day grace period.” Revocation is an individualized procedure, justifiable reasons matter, and a person should promptly preserve job-search or other evidence and seek qualified advice where the facts are uncertain.

## Contract and organization changes

For covered statuses, the resident must notify ISA within 14 days after a qualifying affiliation or contracting-organization event such as leaving or joining an employer. ISA's FAQ also makes clear that a future-dated change cannot be notified before it occurs.^11

This resident duty is distinct from an employer's foreign-worker reporting to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. Employer deadlines and forms vary with employment-insurance coverage and other circumstances.^12 Neither filing proves that a new role falls within the resident's current status.

The revised guide should include a decision sequence:

1. identify whether the resident's status and event trigger a notification;
2. file the resident notification within the applicable deadline;
3. ask the employer about its separate reporting duty;
4. compare the new primary duties with the current status;
5. decide whether a change application or outside-activity permission is needed before performing different activity; and
6. consider a Certificate of Authorized Employment when useful as evidence of permitted employment activity.

The Certificate of Authorized Employment is optional evidence about lawful work activity under the existing permissions. It must not be presented as a status change, a universal hiring prerequisite, or a guarantee about facts not examined.^13

## Temporary travel and re-entry

### Special re-entry permission

An eligible status holder with a valid passport—and, for a mid-to-long-term resident, a residence card—can ordinarily use special re-entry permission when returning within one year. At departure, the traveler must indicate the intention to return using the re-entry departure record and tell the immigration inspector.^3

The effective deadline is not always one year. If the current period of stay expires sooner, the traveler must return by that earlier date. Special re-entry cannot be extended abroad. Temporary Visitor and a person granted three months or less are excluded, along with several listed enforcement and public-interest cases.^3

### Ordinary re-entry permission

A resident planning to return after more than one year should assess ordinary re-entry permission before departure. ISA offers single-use and multiple-use permission, with a maximum ordinarily up to five years but never beyond the current period of stay. Special Permanent Residents follow a distinct maximum.^9 ^14

Ordinary re-entry permission does not renew or extend the underlying status. ISA states that a period-of-stay extension cannot be filed through a Japanese embassy abroad; the person generally needs to return before expiry and apply in Japan.^9 Limited overseas extension of an ordinary re-entry permit is a separate consular possibility and cannot extend the status itself.

### Pending applications

The article needs a dedicated “do not rely on the one-year headline” panel for applicants who depart while an extension or change remains pending. The governing return date can instead be the earlier deadline tied to the old expiry and special period. The person must also remain reachable and complete the decision process after returning.^9

### Documents lost abroad

ISA states that losing a residence card abroad does not itself eliminate the ability to re-enter under special re-entry. If a carrier needs evidence, a representative in Japan can request certification of the re-entry-permission deadline. After return, the resident should promptly use the card-reissuance procedure; the ordinary loss deadline is 14 days from learning of the loss, or from re-entry when the loss was learned abroad.^15

Passport loss still requires the nationality country's replacement or emergency-document process and may introduce consular and carrier requirements. The guide should direct the user to the relevant embassy, airline, and ISA rather than promise boarding.

## Temporary versus permanent departure

The present departure guide should begin with a branch:

- **Returning under the same residence permission:** use the dedicated re-entry guide and do not follow a permanent close-out checklist blindly.
- **Not returning under the same permission:** follow permanent departure, residence-card, municipal, tax, insurance, pension, housing, utilities, banking, and account close-out steps.
- **Plans changed after departure:** determine when re-entry permission or status ceased to be usable and return the residence card as required.

ISA says a mid-to-long-term resident who left with re-entry permission but did not return within its validity must return the residence card within 14 days after that circumstance arises.^15 This edge case belongs in both the re-entry and permanent-departure articles through one source-backed cross-reference.

## User-situation and exception matrix

| Situation | Essential guidance | Common failure to prevent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COE received, visa not yet issued | Check COE expiry, consular jurisdiction, current documents, and changed facts | Treating COE as guaranteed visa or entry |
| Visa-exempt short visit with intended paid work | Verify the correct work/residence route before travel | Treating visa exemption as work authorization |
| New employer, similar title | Notify if covered and separately verify actual-duty fit | Assuming the notification approves the new job |
| New employer, materially different duties | Assess status change or other permission before outside-status activity | Starting first because an application was filed |
| Renewal filed before expiry | Track the decision-or-two-month special-period deadline | Calling it an automatic two-month renewal |
| Travel while renewal/change is pending | Use valid re-entry arrangements, remain reachable, and return before the earliest deadline | Relying on a one-year special re-entry headline |
| Short trip, status expires in six months | Return before status expiry, not one year | Confusing two legal clocks |
| Planned absence over one year | Apply for ordinary re-entry before departure and compare it with status expiry | Assuming ordinary re-entry extends residence |
| Residence card lost abroad | Obtain carrier-facing proof if needed and replace promptly after return | Assuming the card loss automatically cancels status—or that an airline will board without evidence |
| Left temporarily, later will not return | Identify card-return and permanent close-out duties | Leaving an active-looking residence record unattended |

## Proposed Iteration 3B scope

Iteration 3B should:

1. revise the six assigned foundation articles according to the responsibilities above;
2. create `reentry-permission-and-temporary-travel` as shared national guidance;
3. add glossary entries for ordinary re-entry permission, special re-entry permission, and the special pending-application period;
4. add one re-entry FAQ and refine the existing renewal/change and employer-change FAQs;
5. add the temporary-travel guide to the ongoing-resident journey as a focused optional branch and link it contextually from other eligible long-term journeys;
6. add current source records and article-level assertion provenance using the existing Phase 12 workflow; and
7. keep all changed content at `needs-review` pending explicit human approval.

It should not rewrite the full work-status catalog, student/school guidance, or detailed tax and municipal departure rules. Those remain assigned to later bounded research workstreams.

## Decisions requested before Iteration 3B

1. Approve the dedicated shared re-entry article and the separation of temporary travel from permanent departure.
2. Approve a prominent pending-application travel warning in both the renewal/change and re-entry articles.
3. Approve a compact resident-versus-employer notification distinction in the contract-change article, while deferring a full status matrix until more structured routes are populated.
4. Keep the Certificate of Authorized Employment as a focused section in the contract-change guide for now; reassess a standalone reference page after search-term and FAQ expansion.
5. Keep permanent close-out as one article until later tax and municipal research reveals a user-facing reason to split it.
