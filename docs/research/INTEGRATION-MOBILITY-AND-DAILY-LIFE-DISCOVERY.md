# Integration, mobility, and daily-life discovery

Prepared September 12, 2026 for Phase 13 Iteration 11A.

## Proposed direction

Create two public content groups supported by focused cross-links:

- **Transport, safety, and emergencies** covers driving authority, licence conversion, owning a vehicle, traffic rules and accidents, bicycles, emergency calls, and disasters.
- **Daily life and local community** covers household waste, consumer contracts, shared-space living, Japanese-language learning, and local support.
- Driving, vehicle ownership, emergency preparation, and local integration remain optional guide sequences rather than account-level journeys. This prevents a supporting topic from replacing a user's selected relocation pathway.

The central editorial rule is that “the rule in Japan” is usually too imprecise for this workstream. Every claim must identify whether it comes from national law, prefectural administration, a municipal rule or live instruction, a private contract, an official recommendation, a common practice, or an individual's preference.

## Proposed implementation split

### Iteration 11B-1 — Mobility, emergency response, and disaster readiness

- Driving in Japan with a foreign licence or qualifying international driving permit.
- Converting a foreign driver's licence in Japan.
- Buying, registering, inspecting, and insuring a vehicle.
- Driving rules and traffic-accident response.
- Bicycle rules, registration, insurance, accidents, and the April 2026 traffic-violation system.
- Calling police, fire, and ambulance services.
- Preparing for disasters and evacuation.
- Transport/safety group plus direct prerequisite, next-step, and related-guide links.

This is the higher-risk pass. It should use current National Police Agency, MLIT, Fire and Disaster Management Agency, Cabinet Office, JMA, and relevant prefectural instructions. Exact fines and exhaustive violation lists should be avoided unless they add clear user value and can be reliably maintained.

### Iteration 11B-2 — Local responsibilities, consumer protection, and integration

- Sorting household waste and arranging unusual or moving-related disposal.
- Consumer contracts, cancellation questions, common scams, evidence, and help.
- Living in a neighborhood and using shared spaces.
- Finding Japanese-language learning, plain-Japanese information, local international associations, and multilingual consultation.
- Daily-life/local-community group and direct links from the relevant primary journeys and guides.
- FAQs, Japanese terminology, ordinary-language search terms, and contextual links from housing, arrival, work, school, and ongoing-resident guidance.

The consumer guide belongs in this pass because housing, utilities, phones, subscriptions, moving services, and unauthorized waste collection are recurring integration problems. It must make clear that cooling-off and cancellation rights depend on the transaction rather than promising a universal cancellation period.

## Findings that must control drafting

### Driving authority and licence conversion are separate decisions

Japan recognizes a Japanese licence, a qualifying international driving permit under the 1949 Geneva Convention, or a narrow set of foreign licences accompanied by an authorized Japanese translation. A document marketed as an “international licence” is not enough. The allowed driving period depends on the original document, its validity, landing history, and—where the driver is registered as a resident—the rule that a return after less than three months abroad generally does not restart the period.

Conversion is a separate application to the prefectural police authority for the person's Japanese address. It requires proof that the person stayed in the issuing country or region for a total of at least three months after obtaining the licence. Translation issuers, appointments, evidence, knowledge or skills checks, exemptions, categories, and fees need a national foundation plus current prefectural instructions.

### Vehicle ownership is more than registration

The ownership guide must connect the transaction to the registered owner and user, parking-space evidence where applicable, inspection, taxes, compulsory automobile liability insurance, voluntary insurance, maintenance, and later transfer or disposal. Compulsory insurance primarily provides minimum bodily-injury protection; it does not ordinarily cover property damage, and a voluntary policy does not replace it.

### Current bicycle guidance needs an effective date

Bicycles are vehicles under the Road Traffic Act, even though defined sidewalk exceptions exist. Since April 1, 2026, covered violations by riders aged 16 or older can use the traffic-violation notice or “blue ticket” system. Warnings, covered blue-ticket handling, and criminal procedures for serious conduct are different. Helmet guidance, phone and alcohol rules, lights, intersections, accidents, child passengers, parking, anti-theft registration, prefectural insurance duties, pedal-assist bicycles, nonconforming powered cycles, and specified small motorized bicycles cannot be collapsed into a single slogan.

### Emergency numbers need a decision, not a directory

Use 110 for police emergencies and 119 for fire or ambulance emergencies. Readers should learn to give the type of emergency, exact location or landmark, injuries, hazards, and a callback number. Multilingual measures exist, but implementation and availability are not identical everywhere. The guide must distinguish emergencies from non-emergency police consultation, local medical-advice services, consumer help, immigration advice, and later insurance or legal procedures.

### Evacuation site and shelter are not synonyms

A municipality designates an emergency evacuation site for immediate protection from specified hazards and a designated shelter for people who need a place to remain. One facility can have both designations, but a nearby public building is not automatically suitable for a flood, tsunami, landslide, fire, earthquake, or volcanic event. Readers need their exact local hazard map and current municipal/JMA information. Evacuation can also mean another safe location or remaining safely in place, depending on the hazard and official instructions.

### Waste guidance must teach local verification

Municipalities are responsible for municipal waste, so categories, names, bags, schedules, collection points, reservations, fees, oversized-item thresholds, appliance routes, and battery handling cannot be copied from one city into national guidance. The useful national article teaches a resident how to find the correct municipal calendar and distinguish ordinary collection, oversized waste, appliance recycling, hazardous items, moving disposal, and authorized collectors.

### Culture must never disguise preference as law

Neighborhood noise, smoking, pets, balconies, parking, deliveries, common areas, greetings, associations, and communication style may involve a mixture of law, ordinance, lease, building rule, recommendation, custom, and personal preference. The guide should help readers ask what rule applies, obtain important requirements in understandable writing, communicate without assumptions, and seek mediation or help where appropriate. It must not present an imagined uniform Japanese personality or turn optional etiquette into a duty.

## Proposed public structure

1. Let the reader choose whether they need temporary driving authority, licence conversion, vehicle ownership, or bicycle guidance.
2. Keep road rules and collision response available to drivers, cyclists, passengers, and pedestrians.
3. Teach emergency calls separately from household disaster planning.
4. Make every disaster route depend on the hazard, exact place, current alert, and municipal instruction.
5. Route local-life readers through waste, contracts, shared spaces, language learning, and consultation without making optional customs mandatory.
6. Link arrival, housing, school, employment, and ongoing-resident journeys into these focused branches only when relevant.

## Approved implementation decisions

1. Approve separate temporary-driving and foreign-licence-conversion guides. Combining them would hide the different evidence, authority, and timing decisions.
2. Approve consumer contracts and scams for 11B-2. This is a stronger fit than deferral because the issue cuts across housing, phones, utilities, subscriptions, moving, and waste collection.
3. Approve the two-pass sequence above. It isolates the most safety-critical and time-sensitive content in 11B-1 and keeps the local/cultural pass reviewable.
