# Student, School, and Arrival Discovery

Prepared: September 11, 2026  
State: Scope approved September 11, 2026; implemented locally in Iteration 4B

## Executive finding

The current student journey has the right broad milestones, but its next research pass should stop treating “choosing a school,” “getting a student visa,” and “arriving as a resident” as one process. Four separate decisions are involved:

1. a school decides whether to admit the student;
2. the Immigration Services Agency (ISA) decides whether to issue a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) or grant an in-country residence application;
3. a Japanese diplomatic mission decides a visa application made abroad; and
4. an immigration inspector decides landing permission at the border.

A school commonly prepares institutional evidence and may act as the applicant's representative for a COE application, but it does not issue the COE and cannot guarantee the result.^1 ^2 This distinction should appear in both the student guide and a new guide for education providers.

The second major finding is that “short-term study” is a program description, not an immigration status. Japan's official study portal uses the term for non-degree programs lasting from a few weeks to a full academic year. Depending on nationality, passport, duration, class hours, remuneration, and the actual activity, a participant might use Temporary Visitor, Student, Cultural Activities, or another existing status.^3 ^4

Iteration 4B should therefore create two public guides—one for school selection and one for education providers—while revising the existing student articles as a route-aware sequence. Shared arrival articles should remain shared across visa types and change only where an applicability or dependency problem is found.

This discovery record is not publishable immigration guidance. Every implemented article remains `needs-review` until it receives explicit human approval.

The provider guide should have its own focused journey. Mixing institutional duties into the student's journey would make both audiences work through irrelevant steps.

## School selection and admission

### Choose the institution and course, not only the school name

The comparison guide should begin with the student's intended outcome: language preparation, a degree, graduate research, professional training, exchange credit, non-degree study, or a short experience. It should then verify both the institution and the particular course.

For Japanese-language study, the official Study in Japan portal recommends checking recognition, course fit, class levels and size, preparation for later examinations, graduate outcomes, counseling, housing, and refund policy.^5 The regulatory label also matters. MEXT now operates a certification system for Japanese-language institutions, while qualifying institutions under the preceding ISA notification framework have a transition period through March 31, 2029.^6 ^7 Marketing phrases such as “visa school” are not an adequate check.

University, graduate-school, and professional-training-college admission must remain separate. Twelve or sixteen years of prior education are common paths, but preparatory education, equivalency, age, credential type, and individual eligibility assessment can create alternatives. Requirements also vary by institution and program.^8 ^9 ^10 A Japanese-language benchmark stated for one route must not become a universal claim. English-taught programs can remove a Japanese-language admission requirement while leaving academic, financial, and immigration evidence intact.

### Compare total commitment and failure paths

Students need more than headline tuition. The guide should route them to application and examination fees, admission charges, tuition, materials, insurance, housing deposits, daily living, travel, and emergency reserves. It should also make users read refund and withdrawal terms before paying. JASSO provides national cost ranges, but the school remains authoritative for its fees and refund contract.^11

Useful decision questions include:

- Is the institution and exact course recognized for the intended credential and immigration route?
- Does the curriculum serve the student's next step, and what do graduates actually do?
- What language evidence is required for admission and separately for immigration?
- Which documents must be original, authenticated, translated, or recently issued?
- Who may pay the expenses, and how will the source and continuity of funds be shown?
- What happens to deposits or tuition after refusal, withdrawal, delayed entry, transfer, or school cancellation?
- What housing, disability, mental-health, academic, career, and emergency support is genuinely available?

The article should not rank named schools. It should teach a repeatable verification method using official institution lists, the school's current rules, and independent evidence.

## Short-term study is not one immigration route

JASSO describes five common short-program forms: exchange, non-exchange, research, audit, and credit-earning study. Credits and tuition arrangements vary, particularly where a home-university agreement controls them.^3

MOFA describes Short-Term Stay as up to ninety days for purposes such as tourism, business, and visiting people, without remunerative activity. Visa exemption itself depends on nationality and passport conditions; a small number of bilateral arrangements have different maximums and their own extension rules.^4 ^12 Consequently, the revised guide should never say simply that a person can study for ninety days “without a visa.” It should say that some travelers may attend a compatible short course using visa-exempt short-stay entry, after checking nationality, passport, duration, intended activity, and current official rules.

Program length alone is not decisive. JASSO states that a research, audit, or credit-earning student seeking Student status generally must attend at least ten hours of classes per week, while a research student conducting unpaid research without auditing classes may require Cultural Activities.^3 Paid internships, work, or remuneration require a separate status and permission analysis.

The journey should branch early:

- **Compatible short stay:** no resident onboarding sequence; link insurance and temporary-living information appropriate to visitors.
- **Student status:** continue through COE, visa where required, landing, residence registration, school enrollment, and conditional resident administration.
- **Another existing status:** explain that the person may be able to study without changing to Student, but must check work, scholarship, and school consequences.
- **Research, internship, or mixed activity:** direct the user to the actual-activity comparison and official confirmation.

## Student COE, visa, and financial evidence

JASSO states that the student or a proxy in Japan applies for a COE and that, in practice, many schools act as proxy. The applicant then uses the COE for the visa process abroad and later presents the relevant records for landing.^1 ISA remains the issuing authority.

The revised article should divide evidence into roles:

- **Applicant:** identity, education, study purpose, history, and consistent facts.
- **School:** admission and course facts, institutional status, and required institutional confirmation.
- **Expense supporter:** savings, income, relationship, source of funds, and continued ability to pay where requested.
- **Representative:** submits or communicates within authorized boundaries; does not become the decision-maker or financial guarantor merely by filing.

There is no safe universal savings figure. JASSO notes that an applicant may be asked for a savings-balance certificate, recent income records, and evidence explaining how the funds were formed.^1 The eventual guide should teach users to reconcile tuition, living costs, duration, and the source of funds, then use the current school and ISA checklist.

Changed circumstances need a stop-and-check panel. A deferred intake, different school or course, changed expense supporter, new passport, inconsistent dates, or expiring COE should not be quietly carried into the visa or landing process.

## Education-provider responsibilities

### Before accepting the student

The provider guide should help a school establish a defensible workflow:

1. verify that the institution and course may receive the intended student under the proposed route;
2. publish accurate admission, tuition, refund, attendance, housing, and support conditions;
3. assess academic preparation, study purpose, language ability, and financial plan using current criteria;
4. identify inconsistencies or unexplained evidence rather than coaching a false narrative;
5. explain who is the applicant, expense supporter, school contact, and authorized representative; and
6. use secure collection, translation, retention, and return practices for sensitive documents.

The guide should avoid suggesting that every staff member can file every application. ISA's online system has specific conditions for accepting-organization staff and approved application intermediaries, and paid representation can engage legal restrictions.^13 Schools should route uncertain cases to ISA or an appropriately qualified professional.

### Current Japanese-language-school change

For affected Japanese-language institution cases, ISA announced that a prior record of 150 hours of Japanese study alone will no longer establish the required ability. For COE applications involving October 2026 or later entrants, and for change or extension applications filed from July 1, 2026, the institution must use the specified examination certificate or interview-confirmation route and prepare the required institutional confirmation. The notice states limited exceptions, including identified applicants from listed countries or regions who submit qualifying foreign higher-education graduation evidence.^14

This needs an effective-date callout rather than a timeless requirement. The article should link to the current notice and forms because the operational details may change.

### After acceptance and arrival

The provider workflow should include:

- a named pre-arrival contact and escalation route;
- document and granted-status checks without retaining originals unnecessarily;
- enrollment and orientation records;
- clear attendance, absence, tuition, refund, academic-progress, and emergency rules;
- explanation that Student status does not itself permit paid work;
- guidance on outside-activity limits and when the school cannot answer an individualized permission question;
- support for address registration and resident administration without claiming to control municipal decisions;
- procedures for transfer, leave, withdrawal, expulsion, graduation, disappearance, or loss of contact; and
- separate tracking of the student's own notification duties and the institution's reporting.

ISA asks education providers accepting Student-status mid-to-long-term residents to notify the start or end of acceptance within fourteen days and to report the acceptance position as of May 1 and November 1 within fourteen days.^15 Japanese-language institutions also have more detailed attendance and outcome reporting under their applicable framework.^7 The eventual guide must preserve the precise legal character of each notification rather than calling all of them criminally enforceable duties.

This education-provider content is the first half of a broader organizational track. Iteration 5 should research an employer counterpart covering lawful recruitment, actual-duty and status fit, contracts, organization evidence, COE support, onboarding, work-authorization checks, employment reporting, and changes. The two guides should share terminology and the “organization supports; ISA decides” rule, but not pretend that school and employer obligations are identical.

## Part-time work and study maintenance

Student status is based on education, not employment. The revised work guide should explain that outside-activity permission is a separate permission. ISA's ordinary comprehensive student permission is generally capped at twenty-eight hours per week, with up to eight hours per day during a long vacation set by the institution, and excludes adult-entertainment-related business.^16 Multiple jobs count toward the shared limit. An internship or activity that does not fit the comprehensive permission may require individual review.

The provider guide should explain expectations and escalation without turning the school into the immigration decision-maker. Attendance, study progress, and continued enrollment matter independently of whether a work shift stayed under the hourly cap. Transfer, withdrawal, expulsion, graduation, or a long unexplained absence can affect both the resident's next actions and institution reporting.

## Arrival and the shared resident sequence

The arrival material should preserve the solution to the address-bank-housing loop:

1. use school-arranged housing, temporary accommodation, a guarantor service, or another viable first address/payment path;
2. establish the actual residence and complete address notification when required;
3. obtain or update resident records needed by later providers;
4. establish workable connectivity; and
5. approach banks and long-term housing providers with the documents they require, recognizing that private screening varies.

This is sequencing help, not a claim that every bank or landlord uses the same requirements.

At designated airports, a new mid-to-long-term resident receives the residence card with landing permission. At other ports, the passport records that the card will be issued later; it is generally mailed after the resident reports an address.^17 The documents article should ask users to check names, status, work restrictions, and expiry information promptly and preserve any designation attached to the passport.

Temporary Visitor students should not be sent through address registration, My Number, National Health Insurance, or National Pension as if those were universal study steps. Conversely, those articles should remain shared guidance because they apply to many mid-to-long-term residents, not only students.

Temporary travel during school vacation should link to the shared re-entry article. A student must compare the return plan with the current period of stay and re-entry rules; the fact that a school break is authorized does not extend immigration permission.

## User-situation matrix

| Situation | Essential next check | Failure to prevent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Six-week language course | Nationality/passport, actual activity, permitted stay, and school rules | Promising every student visa-free ninety-day entry |
| Semester exchange | Home-school agreement, credits, tuition, host admission, and immigration basis | Treating “exchange” as an immigration status |
| University admission received | Separate COE, financial, visa, and landing steps | Treating admission as government approval |
| Graduate applicant with atypical education | School's current eligibility assessment and official alternative pathways | Presenting sixteen years as an exceptionless rule |
| Vocational applicant | Recognized school/course, prior education, accepted language path, and outcome | Copying one language threshold to every program |
| Unpaid research with few classes | Student ten-hour rule and Cultural Activities comparison | Selecting Student from the word “student” alone |
| School staff preparing a COE case | Representative authority, current form, truthful school evidence, and secure handling | Saying the school issues or guarantees the COE |
| Student wants a part-time job | Outside-activity permission, combined hours, business exclusion, and study priority | Treating enrollment as work permission |
| Student changes or leaves school | Resident and institution actions, status maintenance, records, and next route | Assuming one notification automatically changes status |
| Residence card not issued at arrival | Port used, passport notation, address notification, and delivery | Claiming every airport issues the card immediately |
| New arrival lacks bank account and permanent lease | Temporary housing/payment path followed by address, phone, bank, and long-term housing | Circular instructions with no workable first step |
| Student travels home for vacation | Re-entry eligibility and the earliest status/return deadline | Applying the permanent-departure checklist or assuming school dates control |

## Proposed Iteration 4B scope

Iteration 4B should:

1. create `choosing-a-school-and-understanding-admission-requirements`;
2. create `accepting-international-students-and-supporting-coe-applications` and a focused education-provider journey;
3. revise the planning, short-term, Student COE, pre-entry, school-arrival, and part-time-work guides according to the responsibilities above;
4. audit the eight shared arrival guides and edit only confirmed applicability, sequencing, or source gaps;
5. branch the international-student journey between compatible short stay and Student status, keeping shared resident steps conditional;
6. add the Japanese terminology, FAQ language, search terms, source records, and assertion provenance needed by the accepted articles;
7. introduce an `education-provider` audience value if approved, while reserving the parallel `employer` audience and guide for Iteration 5B; and
8. keep all new or materially revised content at `needs-review` pending explicit human approval.

It should not implement the employer guide, rank individual schools, reproduce exhaustive institution-specific checklists, or rewrite shared arrival content merely to make the diff larger.

## Decisions requested before Iteration 4B

1. Approve the two new guides and the separate education-provider journey.
2. Approve `education-provider` as a new Audience filter value in 4B; add `employer` with its first substantive guide in 5B rather than expose an empty filter now.
3. Keep school selection as one comparative guide with school-type sections; split it later only if the resulting page proves too dense.
4. Include a prominent conditional section for minors and secondary-school students, but defer a dedicated safeguarding guide until its narrower source review is scheduled.
5. Cover transfer, withdrawal, expulsion, and graduation at orientation level in 4B, then deepen their immigration transitions in the later status batch where necessary.
6. Make the education-provider journey visible through its article, Audience filter, and contextual routes initially; revisit a default Explore group after employer coverage makes the organizational catalog substantial.
7. Preserve the existing editorial-review state of shared arrival guides unless a substantive change is necessary; source-checking alone should not churn reviewed copy.
