# Family, Designated, Visitor, and Official Routes Discovery

Prepared and scope-approved September 12, 2026. This discovery record defines the approved Iteration 8B scope; researched public guidance remains subject to article-level editorial review.

## Why this batch needs route-first navigation

This batch contains several labels that look broader than they are. “Family visa” can refer to materially different statuses. “Designated Activities” is an umbrella over programs with incompatible work, family, period, and renewal rules. “Medical Stay” can describe a visa process connected to either a short visit or a longer Designated Activities route. A diplomatic or official passport does not by itself determine the status granted.

Iteration 8B should therefore teach readers to identify the exact relationship, designation, purpose, or assignment before presenting requirements. It should not turn these routes into a universal checklist.

## Family routes are not interchangeable

Dependent (`家族滞在`) covers ordinary daily activities of a supported spouse or child of a person holding one of the listed principal statuses. The current Japanese status table includes Specified Skilled Worker (ii), not Specified Skilled Worker (i). Parents, siblings, unmarried partners, and other relatives do not enter this route merely because someone can support them.[^1][^2]

Dependent does not itself authorize ordinary work. A person may seek blanket outside-activity permission for work up to twenty-eight hours per week under the applicable restrictions, but a full-time career generally requires an independent work status or a separately defined special provision.[^3]

The two spouse-or-child statuses have different child definitions:

- Spouse or Child of Japanese National covers a qualifying spouse, a person born as a child of a Japanese national, or a qualifying special adoptee.[^4]
- Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident covers a qualifying spouse of a permanent or special permanent resident, or their biological child who was born in Japan and continued residing in Japan.[^5]

Both are status-based residence routes without an activity-based work restriction, but spouse applications and renewals still depend on the genuine underlying marital relationship. Covered spouse holders must notify ISA within fourteen days after divorce or bereavement.[^6]

The implementation must handle separation and domestic violence safely. Published ISA material recognizes circumstances such as domestic violence, child-rearing, divorce proceedings, and unavoidable separation when discussing spouse-activity and revocation issues. Nihonest must not advise a person to remain in danger merely to preserve immigration evidence.[^7]

## Long-Term Resident is not a generic fallback

Long-Term Resident (`定住者`) is granted in consideration of special circumstances under a listed notification category or an individual decision. Current ISA examples include third-country-resettled refugees, third-generation people of Japanese descent, and people connected to Japanese nationals left in China. Published application paths also cover specified spouses, supported minor unmarried biological children, and adopted children under six.[^8]

The current minor-child notification rule changed with Japan's age of majority: a covered new entrant must enter before turning eighteen. ISA also publishes granted and refused examples for some people seeking to change from a spouse status after divorce, bereavement, or marital breakdown. Those examples reveal relevant facts, but they are not a formula or entitlement.[^8][^9]

A concise family-route comparison would add value beyond the existing journey selector because it can explain relationship definitions, work authorization, sponsor status, evidence, and what happens when circumstances change. The proposed article is `choosing-a-family-or-personal-status-in-japan`.

## Permanent Residence has a dated 2027 change

The Permanent Residence guideline was revised on February 24, 2026. Its ordinary route requires, in principle, ten years of continuous residence, including five continuous years under a qualifying work or status-based residence category. It also addresses good conduct, stable livelihood, public obligations such as taxes and social insurance, the applicant's present status, and national-interest considerations. Separate exceptions apply to qualifying spouses and children, Long-Term Residents, recognized refugees or complementary-protection recipients, highly skilled people, and other defined cases.[^10]

The current maximum-period treatment needs an explicit date boundary. Through March 31, 2027, a three-year period can still be treated as the longest period for the guideline. From April 1, 2027, the published notice returns to requiring the actual longest available period, with limited transitional handling for certain people holding three years on March 31, 2027.[^10][^11]

A Permanent Residence application is separate from extension of the current status. If that status will expire while the permanent-residence application is pending, the applicant must still apply separately for an extension. The guide must also route readers to the evidence list corresponding to their current position rather than presenting one document checklist.[^11]

## Designated Activities is a family of programs

Designated Activities (`特定活動`) means the Minister of Justice has designated a particular activity for the person. The umbrella currently includes many unrelated programs, including medical stay, internships, domestic workers, long-stay sightseeing, spouses and parents of certain highly skilled residents, continued job hunting, J-Find, Start-up, Working Holiday, and Digital Nomad.[^12]

The exact designation controls:

- whether work is permitted and what work;
- whether a spouse, child, or parent may accompany;
- the period and any cumulative maximum;
- whether renewal is possible;
- the evidence and organization involved; and
- what later transition can be requested.

The existing Designated Activities article should become a routing hub with contextual links inside each program section. The generic residence-status record can explain the umbrella, but should not display one rule as though it governs every designation.

## Graduate job hunting and J-Find solve different problems

Continued job hunting after Japanese study applies to defined graduates who began job hunting before graduation and continue it afterward. The covered institution, course and credential, relationship between study and prospective work in some vocational cases, school recommendation, continuing search evidence, and support funds matter.[^13]

The implementation should distinguish three situations:

1. ordinary continued job hunting after a covered Japanese program;
2. extended participation under a qualifying local-government employment-support program; and
3. waiting to begin a job after receiving an offer.

The third situation has its own requirements. Current guidance generally requires employment to begin within one year after the offer and within eighteen months after graduation, anticipated fit with a work status, a compliant residence record, and an undertaking from the employer to stay in contact and report cancellation. Part-time work still requires outside-activity permission.[^14]

J-Find instead concerns a recent graduate of a currently qualifying overseas university or graduate school. Current material requires a qualifying institution under the published ranking method, graduation within five years, a bachelor's-level or higher degree, age eighteen or older, and at least 200,000 yen for initial living expenses. It permits job hunting or business preparation for up to two years and can include a spouse or child.[^15]

That two-year maximum is not always a fresh clock. ISA's outline identifies time under certain similar continued-job-hunting and entrepreneurial-preparation programs as relevant to the cumulative maximum.[^16]

## Digital Nomad and long-stay sightseeing are not relocation routes

Digital Nomad Designated Activities permits specified foreign-facing remote work for six months. Current requirements include nationality or citizenship of a listed country or region, annual income of at least 10 million yen, and private medical insurance with at least 10 million yen of treatment coverage. The period cannot be extended, a residence card is not issued, and a person must wait six months after leaving before using the route again.[^17]

It does not permit employment or a comparable contract with a Japanese public or private organization. A spouse or child can have a separate accompanying designation under its conditions, but outside activity is generally unavailable. Tax treatment depends on the applicable tax convention and facts; the immigration label is not a tax exemption.[^17]

Long-stay sightseeing is a separate no-work route for nationals or citizens of visa-waiver countries or regions. The principal applicant must be at least eighteen and generally show savings exceeding 30 million yen, plus qualifying medical insurance. The ordinary grant is six months, with a possible extension producing at most one year. An accompanying spouse can qualify under specific travel and household conditions, but children cannot accompany through this scheme. If spouses stay separately under the scheme, combined savings exceeding 60 million yen are required.[^18]

## Working Holiday rules are bilateral and changing

Working Holiday is holiday-first: employment is incidental and supports travel funds. As of April 1, 2026, Japan listed thirty-two partner countries or regions. The general outline includes nationality or regional-passport rules, residence in the relevant country for many applicants, age, funds, health, and no accompanying dependants or children, but exact requirements belong to the applicable Japanese embassy or consulate.[^19]

Recent changes allow a second participation for specified nationalities or Taiwan, with different consecutive or non-consecutive structures. Those changes took effect on different dates from December 2024 through February 2026. The guide should carry a checked date and avoid copying one country's age, quota, funds, insurance, or work restriction to every applicant.[^19]

## Temporary Visitor is not the same as visa-free travel

Temporary Visitor covers short activities such as tourism, recreation, visiting relatives, observation, lectures or meetings, and business contact. Current periods are normally 15, 30, or 90 days, or another individually designated period not exceeding 90 days.[^1][^20]

The status granted at landing must remain separate from:

- whether the traveler requires a visa;
- whether a nationality and passport qualify for visa exemption;
- whether an eVISA application channel is available;
- how long an issued visa remains valid for presentation at the border; and
- how long the person is actually permitted to stay after landing.

Short stay does not authorize remunerative activity. Extensions are exceptional rather than a normal way to remain in Japan, and Temporary Visitors do not receive a residence card or enter ordinary mid-to-long-term resident onboarding.[^20][^21]

## Medical Stay crosses the visa and status boundary

MOFA's Medical Stay visa supports treatment, examinations, dental care, and other planned medical services, with an accompanying person where necessary. A registered medical coordinator or travel agency helps arrange the institution and guarantee documents. A multiple-entry visa may be available when each visit is ninety days or less and a treatment plan supports it.[^22]

The immigration route changes with the plan. For treatment of ninety days or less, a short-stay framework may apply. Hospitalization expected to exceed ninety days requires a COE and the Designated Activities medical-stay analysis. ISA's longer-stay route requires hospital, treatment, accommodation, and financing evidence and has a corresponding attendant designation. The attendant is present for care and cannot use that role for remunerative activity.[^22][^23][^24]

## Diplomat and Official depend on the assignment

Diplomat covers members of recognized diplomatic missions or consular posts, people receiving equivalent privileges or immunities under treaty or international practice, and qualifying household family. Its period is the duration of the mission.[^25]

Official covers official business of foreign governments or recognized international organizations outside the Diplomat category, along with qualifying household family. Available periods are five years, three years, one year, three months, thirty days, or fifteen days.[^26]

Neither status follows automatically from holding a diplomatic or official passport. A holder traveling privately for tourism or transit may instead receive Temporary Visitor status. The articles should direct applicants to the sending organization and Japanese diplomatic mission for coordinated, assignment-specific instructions and should not send these users through ordinary residence-card steps.[^25][^26][^27]

## Proposed Iteration 8B sequence

To keep implementation and review bounded:

1. **8B-1 — Family and durable residence:** create the family-route comparison; rewrite Dependent, both spouse-or-child guides, Long-Term Resident, and Permanent Residence; update the joining-family and permanent-residence journeys, structured records, FAQs, terminology, search, and links.
2. **8B-2 — Designated, visitor, medical, and official routes:** rewrite the Designated Activities hub and its five assigned program guides; revise Temporary Visitor, Medical Stay, Diplomat, and Official; then update structured records, route-specific journeys, groups, FAQs, terminology, search, and links.

No materially revised guide should move beyond `needs-review` without explicit human approval.

## Approved scope decisions

1. Create the standalone family-route comparison in addition to the joining-family journey.
2. Keep Designated Activities as a contextual routing hub using the existing generic structured record rather than adding a program-variant schema in this phase.
3. Keep Medical Stay primarily in Short stay and medical visits, with a contextual link from Designated Activities, to avoid a duplicate default group card.
4. Implement 8B in two passes: family and durable residence first, then Designated Activities, visitors, medical, Diplomat, and Official.
