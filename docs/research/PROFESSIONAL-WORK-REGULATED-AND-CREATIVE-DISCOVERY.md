# Professional Work: Regulated and Creative Discovery

Prepared September 11, 2026. This is an Iteration 6A research and scope proposal. It does not change published guidance.

## Why this batch needs comparison-first guidance

The eight statuses in this batch cannot be explained safely as a list of job titles. Creative work can move among several statuses based on the activity, income model, contract, and organization. Legal, accounting, medical, and care work can turn on a Japanese qualification. Skilled Labor is a defined set of special industrial skills, not a general category for experienced workers.

The implementation should therefore answer two questions separately:

1. What facts distinguish the plausible routes?
2. What evidence supports the route after those facts are known?

Search aliases such as photographer, nurse, caregiver, lawyer, chef, or coach should help a user reach that comparison. They must never appear as an automated eligibility result.

## Creative and media routes

The already approved creative-and-media discovery amendment remains the basis for this part of Iteration 6.[^1]

- **Artist** covers income-producing music, fine art, literature, and other artistic activity outside Entertainer. Official materials distinguish contracted work from activity without a contract and request concrete activity, duration, expected income, and professional-achievement evidence.[^2]
- **Entertainer** is broader than stage performance. Current materials include advertising activity, broadcast or film production, commercial photography, and commercial audio or video recording, with evidence that varies by activity category.[^3]
- **Journalist** requires journalistic activity under contract with a foreign news organization. The current procedure recognizes dispatch, employment, and qualifying non-employment contracts, including freelance arrangements.[^4]
- **Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services** remains a comparison for employed specialist design, digital-product, advertising, and related work.
- **Cultural Activities** is a nearby route for unpaid artistic activity or specialized cultural study, not paid creative employment.[^5]
- **Business Manager** may matter when the principal activity is genuinely operating or managing a business. Owning a studio does not make all production work management activity.

Iteration 6B should create the approved central comparison guide and then rewrite Artist, Entertainer, and Journalist around these boundaries. Photographer is the clearest illustration: fine-art, foreign-news, commercial-production, and in-house photography can present different questions.

## Qualification-controlled professional work

### Legal/Accounting Services

The current landing criteria name eleven professions: attorney, judicial scrivener, land and house investigator, registered foreign lawyer, certified public accountant, foreign certified public accountant, certified tax accountant, labor and social security attorney, patent attorney, maritime procedure agent, and administrative scrivener.[^6]

This is not a general legal-office or accounting-office status. The guide must distinguish the reserved practice of a listed qualified professional from work such as paralegal support, compliance, bookkeeping, financial analysis, or consulting. Those adjacent roles may require a different comparison based on their real duties. An overseas degree or professional title alone must not be described as the Japanese qualification.

### Medical Services

Medical Services covers licensed medical work. Current criteria name physician, dentist, pharmacist, public health nurse, midwife, nurse, assistant nurse, dental hygienist, radiologic technologist, physical therapist, occupational therapist, orthoptist, clinical engineer, and prosthetist/orthotist. The work must receive remuneration at least equivalent to that of a Japanese worker performing it.[^7]

Two less obvious rules require prominent treatment:

- an assistant nurse works under a training limitation within four years after obtaining the Japanese license;
- pharmacists and several allied-health professions must be invited by a Japanese medical institution or pharmacy.

The implementation must use current criteria rather than repeat outdated profession restrictions from older summaries. It must also separate licensed care from medical research, hospital administration, translation, and health-technology work.

### Nursing Care

Nursing Care requires a contract with a Japanese public or private organization, Certified Care Worker qualification, and care work or care instruction. Since April 1, 2020, the route is available regardless of how that qualification was acquired.[^8]

The English words nurse, care worker, and caregiver are not interchangeable route labels. The comparison must distinguish:

- a nurse or another licensed medical professional under Medical Services;
- a registered Certified Care Worker under Nursing Care;
- nursing-care field employment under Specified Skilled Worker;
- EPA nurse or care-worker candidates under Designated Activities;
- a student who passed the care-worker examination but is waiting for registration and may use the specific temporary Designated Activities bridge.[^9]

Because users commonly begin with these ambiguous job names, Iteration 6B should add `choosing-a-status-for-medical-and-care-work-in-japan` as a concise route comparison rather than force them to infer the distinctions across several long articles.

## Religious Activities

Religious Activities covers religious work by religious personnel dispatched to Japan by a foreign religious organization. Current evidence includes the dispatch period, position and remuneration; details of the dispatching and receiving organizations; and the applicant's religious standing and work history.[^10]

The revised guide should cover missionaries and other religious personnel while making these limits visible:

- a Japanese congregation's invitation alone does not prove the foreign-organization dispatch relationship;
- ordinary language teaching, charity administration, commercial activity, and other non-religious work require their own analysis;
- the application form anticipates incidental activities such as language teaching, but this should not be presented as blanket permission for unrelated paid work.

## Skilled Labor is category-bound

Skilled Labor covers work requiring skilled ability in special industrial fields under contract with a Japanese public or private organization. ISA gives foreign-cuisine cooks, sports instructors, aircraft pilots, and precious-metal craftspeople as examples and lists five-year, three-year, one-year, and three-month periods.[^11]

The full guide should map the official categories rather than call any experienced occupation skilled labor. Current materials cover foreign-cuisine cooking or food production, foreign-specific architecture or civil engineering, manufacture or repair of foreign-specific products, animal training, seabed drilling or related exploration work, gemstone/precious-metal/fur processing, aircraft piloting, sports instruction, and wine appraisal or service by a sommelier.[^12]

Important category rules include:

- foreign-cuisine work generally requires ten years of relevant experience, including qualifying foreign education;
- the Thailand-Japan agreement supports a distinct five-year evidence route for qualifying Thai cuisine cooks;
- foreign-specific architecture generally uses ten years, or five years when working under a person with at least ten years of the relevant experience;
- the relevant pilot route requires evidence of at least 250 flight hours;
- sports instruction uses a three-year experience route, which may count specified education and paid athletic work, or an international-competition route;
- the sommelier route uses five years of relevant experience plus specified competition, sole-country-representation, or designated-certification evidence.[^12][^13]

Skilled Labor must be compared clearly with Specified Skilled Worker. The latter is a sector program with its own skill and Japanese-language routes, accepting-organization duties, support model, family rules, and period structure. Similar words such as cook, manufacturing, or agriculture do not make the statuses interchangeable.

## Proposed Iteration 6B implementation

1. Create the already approved `choosing-a-residence-status-for-creative-and-media-work` guide.
2. Create `choosing-a-status-for-medical-and-care-work-in-japan` if the editor approves this proposal.
3. Rewrite Artist, Entertainer, Journalist, Legal/Accounting Services, Medical Services, Nursing Care, Religious Activities, and Skilled Labor.
4. Populate structured status guidance for all eight routes.
5. Add ordinary-language profession aliases, FAQs, and Japanese terms without presenting those terms as route decisions.
6. Review and cross-link Engineer/Specialist, Cultural Activities, Business Manager, Researcher, Specified Skilled Worker, Designated Activities, side-work, work-change, and employer guidance.
7. Keep Skilled Labor versus Specified Skilled Worker inside the relevant status guides unless the implementation becomes too dense.

## Scope decisions requested

Approve the medical-and-care comparison guide and the eight-status implementation scope above. The creative-and-media comparison was approved previously. The recommended default is to avoid another standalone Skilled Labor comparison unless the researched articles demonstrate a usability need.
