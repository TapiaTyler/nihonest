# Business and Workforce Programs Discovery

Prepared September 12, 2026. The editor approved this Iteration 7A scope on September 12, 2026, with implementation combined into two passes.

## Why this batch needs two separate comparisons

This batch contains two different decision problems. Founders and highly skilled professionals compare ways to conduct an already defined business or professional activity. Culture, training, and workforce-program users instead need to distinguish unpaid study, non-employment training, regulated employment-and-development programs, and direct sector work.

Trying to solve both in one article would hide the most important questions. Iteration 7B should keep the existing founder and highly skilled journey, while adding a focused training and workforce-development comparison.

## Business Manager after the 2025 reform

The Business Manager criteria changed on October 16, 2025. Current guidance must explain the requirements together rather than reduce the route to a capital headline:

- a real place of business in Japan;
- at least one qualifying full-time employee;
- at least 30 million yen in qualifying business assets, capital, contribution, or investment, calculated according to the business form;
- Japanese capacity at approximately JF Standard B2 through the manager or a qualifying full-time employee;
- a qualifying graduate-level degree for the business or management activity, or at least three years of management experience;
- a concrete business plan evaluated by an appropriate professional; and
- compliance with applicable licensing, tax, labor, and social-insurance obligations.[^1]

The article must explain the definition and evidence behind each item. For example, the capital rule is not safely paraphrased as money in the applicant's personal bank account, and not every worker counts as the required full-time employee. Existing status holders also receive transitional examination through October 16, 2028; the guide must not claim that one unmet new criterion automatically decides every renewal during that period.[^1]

## Start-up is preparation, not Business Manager approval

Japan's nationally authorized Start-up program was unified in January 2025 and can provide up to two years to prepare a business. The applicant first asks an approved municipality or private organization to review the entrepreneurial plan. If the organization confirms the plan, the applicant separately applies to ISA for the applicable Designated Activities permission. The organization then provides support and monitors progress.[^2]

The implementation should use the legal identity—Designated Activities No. 44—alongside the familiar Start-up label. Local industries, areas, application materials, support, and intake conditions vary. Neither a local confirmation nor a preparation period guarantees a later Business Manager decision.

## Highly Skilled Professional has two qualification routes

The ordinary Highly Skilled Professional system uses three activity classes and a current threshold of 70 points. It does not replace the underlying requirement that the planned academic, specialized/technical, or management activity fit its category.[^3]

J-Skip is a separate threshold method introduced in April 2023, not another residence status and not a points bonus. Current thresholds are:

- advanced academic research or specialized/technical activity: a master's-level or higher degree and anticipated annual remuneration of at least 20 million yen, or at least ten years of relevant experience and that remuneration;
- advanced business-management activity: at least five years of relevant management experience and anticipated annual remuneration of at least 40 million yen.[^4]

Both routes can lead to Highly Skilled Professional category 1, with a five-year period and listed benefits. Category 2 has an unlimited period but is not permanent residence. The ordinary route generally uses three years of qualifying category-1 activity before category 2, while J-Skip can use one year. J-Skip also has a one-year accelerated permanent-residence route; those concepts must remain separate.[^3][^4]

## Culture and Training are not casual work routes

Cultural Activities covers unpaid academic or artistic activity or specialized study of Japanese culture or skills. Official examples include fields such as tea ceremony, flower arrangement, judo, architecture, painting, dance, cuisine, and traditional music. Evidence differs between independent research or practice and study under an expert. The guide should make funding and the prohibition on ordinary paid work prominent.[^5]

Training concerns skills acquisition through a receiving institution, not an ordinary employment relationship. Current materials ask for the invitation reason, training schedule, participant treatment, relevant prior overseas training, and evidence that the acquired skill will be used after return. In the stated application context, qualifying prior overseas non-practical training must have occurred within six months before planned entry and lasted at least one month and 160 hours.[^6] That condition must be presented in context rather than as a universal slogan.

## Technical Intern Training is a live legacy route

Technical Intern Training should be rewritten as a current but transitioning regulated program. The guide needs its approved-plan and organization structure, stages, work and protection context, transfer situations, periods, and receiving-organization duties. It must also tell users that the program cannot be understood without their plan and dates.[^7]

The successor takes effect on April 1, 2027, but transitional rules preserve some legacy participants and plans. Current official material indicates that people already participating on the effective date may continue under their approved plan. Certain legacy plan applications made by March 31, 2027 can support a start through June 30, 2027, subject to the transition rules.[^8]

## Employment for Skill Development needs a standalone guide

Employment for Skill Development (育成就労) is designed to develop workers through three years of employment to the Specified Skilled Worker (i) skill level in covered shortage fields. It introduces its own plans, accepting organizations, supervising support organizations, support and transfer rules. It is therefore not merely a renamed Technical Intern Training stage.[^9]

The route should receive a standalone guide now for three reasons:

1. it begins April 1, 2027, within the lifetime of the guidance being prepared;
2. pre-effective applications for organization permits and plans begin in 2026; and
3. date-specific rules affect people already in Technical Intern Training and those planning a later Specified Skilled Worker transition.[^9][^10]

Prior Technical Intern Training time generally matters. Current Q&A indicates that a person with at least two previous years in Technical Intern Training generally cannot begin a new Employment for Skill Development cycle in a different field. Successful completion of Technical Intern Training stage 2 can continue to support transition to Specified Skilled Worker (i) under transitional handling when the occupational relationship fits.[^8][^9]

The already researched Specified Skilled Worker article should remain the authoritative field, test, support, family, period, and employer-change guide. Iteration 7 should add transition context and links rather than duplicate that catalog.

## Approved Iteration 7B sequence

To keep implementation and review bounded without unnecessary fragmentation:

1. **7B-1 — Founder, high-skill, culture, and training:** Business Manager, Start-up, Highly Skilled Professional/J-Skip, Cultural Activities, and Training.
2. **7B-2 — Workforce transition and integration:** rewrite Technical Intern Training; create Employment for Skill Development and the training/workforce comparison; add a distinct workforce-development journey and core successor terminology; update structured records, groups, search, FAQs, and cross-links; then run focused integrity checks.

No materially revised guide should move beyond `needs-review` without explicit human approval.

## Scope decisions

The editor approved both new guides, immediate core successor terminology, and a distinct workforce-development journey so direct workers are not mixed with people entering a training or development program. The editor also requested the two-pass implementation sequence above.
