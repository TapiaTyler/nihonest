# Professional Work: Academic and Technical Discovery

## Executive finding

Iteration 5B should teach users to move from an ordinary job title to the legal facts behind it: actual principal duties, institution or workplace, contracting organization, qualifications, remuneration, and application context. It should not publish an occupation-to-visa lookup table that implies a title decides eligibility.

The most important example is **English teacher**. Japan's official residence-status list places university education within Professor, language education at specified schools within Instructor, and language teaching by a private company within Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services.^1 A useful guide must therefore ask *where and how will you teach?* before suggesting a route.

This is a discovery record, not publishable immigration guidance. Candidate occupations and edge cases remain subject to route-by-route source review in Iteration 5B.

## Common job titles must be visible

Every researched work-status article should contain three distinct lists:

1. **Representative titles people search for** — ordinary employment language and legitimate aliases.
2. **Why the work may fit** — the qualifying duties and organization facts behind those titles.
3. **Nearby roles requiring another check** — similar titles whose duties, institution, licensing, or employment structure can point elsewhere.

The title inventory should also populate reviewed article `searchTerms` and FAQs. It must not become a hidden eligibility engine or an SEO keyword dump.

### Proposed title coverage

| Route | Representative titles to explain | Boundaries to show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services | Software developer, software engineer, programmer, systems engineer, IT engineer, network or cloud engineer, security engineer, data analyst, technical consultant, mechanical or electrical engineer, product or industrial designer, interpreter, translator, private-company or eikaiwa language instructor, overseas-sales or trade specialist, marketing specialist, planner, accountant, business consultant, and qualifying HR roles | Actual specialized duties; degree, experience, or IT-exam path; field relationship; international-services rules; manual or routine work; regulated activities; Researcher and Intra-company exclusions |
| Instructor | English teacher, foreign-language teacher, ALT or assistant language teacher, elementary-school teacher, junior-high teacher, high-school teacher, special-needs teacher, vocational-school instructor, and teacher at another qualifying school | Exact institution category; direct hire versus dispatch; private-company language school; university teaching; preschool, international-school, tutoring, after-school, online, and administrative duties |
| Professor | Professor, associate professor, assistant professor, lecturer, university English instructor, visiting faculty, academic researcher, research supervisor, and teacher at a college of technology or equivalent institution | Institution and activity, not rank; part-time and multiple appointments; private-company teaching; corporate or government research; separately paid consulting or lectures |
| Researcher | Corporate researcher, government-laboratory researcher, research-institute scientist, R&D researcher, laboratory researcher, and research project investigator | Professor-covered university research; engineering or product-development work; medical or other licensed activity; designated programs; research plus management or consulting |
| Intra-company Transferee | Expatriate assignee, overseas-office transferee, software or systems engineer, technical consultant, analyst, marketer, designer, translator, corporate language specialist, and other qualifying professional roles transferred within a group | Same professional-duty boundary as Engineer/Specialist plus corporate relationship, qualifying offices, prior overseas service, limited transfer, worksite, and unrelated-employer changes |

Some proposed titles—particularly cloud engineer, data analyst, HR professional, product manager, and research-adjacent development roles—are useful search language but are not explicit official approval examples. Iteration 5B must describe them as roles that *may* contain qualifying duties and pair them with concrete duty questions.

## English teaching needs its own decision path

The article set should make the following comparison prominent:

| Work setting | Route to examine first | Why it is not automatic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| University, equivalent higher-education institution, or college of technology | Professor | The activity and institution matter; lecturer or teacher rank alone does not decide it. |
| Elementary, junior-high, high school, special-needs school, professional-training school, miscellaneous-category school, or qualifying equivalent | Instructor | The school's actual Japanese legal category and the teaching activity must be checked. |
| Private eikaiwa or another private company providing language instruction | Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services | ISA lists private-company language teachers as an example, but qualification and contract criteria still apply. |
| Direct-hire or dispatched ALT | Instructor is an important comparison | The employing organization, contracting arrangement, worksite, and actual duties need source-specific treatment. `ALT` is not itself a status. |
| University teacher also teaching for a private company | Professor plus an outside-activity check | ISA uses this as an example of activity potentially requiring separate permission.^2 |
| International school, kindergarten, preschool, cram school, after-school program, tutoring service, or online platform | Institution and duty classification required | The English business name does not prove that the organization fits a listed school category. |
| Freelance or private lessons alongside another status | Current status and outside-activity review | Self-employment, multiple organizations, and hours that are difficult to verify can require individual permission or another route analysis.^2 |

This comparison should appear in or immediately beside the Instructor guide, with reciprocal links from Professor and Engineer/Specialist. A standalone “Which status for teaching?” article is plausible, but its value should be evaluated against duplication during scope approval.

## Engineer/Specialist requires alternatives, not one degree rule

ISA's official examples include mechanical engineers, interpreters, designers, private-company language teachers, and marketing workers.^1 Separate official materials provide systems engineer, programmer, and software engineer examples and describe accounting, sales, planning, consulting, system design, and program development as representative professional work.^3

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- relevant university education;
- qualifying Japanese professional-training education with the required relationship to the work;
- the ordinary relevant-experience route;
- the different experience rule for specified international-services activity;
- the university-graduate exception for specified translation, interpretation, or language-instruction work;
- recognized information-processing examinations or qualifications; and
- Japanese-worker remuneration parity and a qualifying contract.

It should explain that a qualification route and an activity route are both needed: having an IT examination does not turn routine work into qualifying engineering, and performing technical work does not erase the need to establish an accepted qualification path.

Official case material now explicitly names systems engineer, programmer, and software engineer work.^3 Those titles should appear in visible prose, not only metadata. Common adjacent search terms such as web developer, frontend developer, backend developer, DevOps engineer, cloud engineer, cybersecurity engineer, and data analyst can be included as duty-dependent examples after their relationship to the official technical definition is reviewed.

## Mixed duties and misleading titles

The articles should give readers a method for describing the role:

- list each substantial duty using verbs;
- estimate the time allocation;
- identify which duties require specialized knowledge or foreign-cultural sensitivity;
- distinguish temporary onboarding from the permanent assignment;
- identify customer-facing, manual, production, installation, administrative, management, and regulated work; and
- compare the principal activity with nearby statuses.

The current ISA clarification package includes separate treatment of permissible practical training and case examples.^4 Iteration 5B should use that material to explain why limited connected onboarding is different from an indefinite non-qualifying assignment.

Titles such as engineer, coordinator, manager, consultant, researcher, international staff, and teacher can all conceal routine or differently regulated work. The UI should therefore label occupations as “common roles to examine,” not “jobs allowed by this visa.”

## Academic teaching and research boundaries

Professor covers research, research guidance, or education at a university, equivalent institution, or college of technology. Researcher covers contracted research at a Japanese public or private organization while excluding Professor activity. Instructor is defined by education at its own listed school categories.^1

Iteration 5B should test these situations:

- a university English lecturer or language instructor;
- a visiting or part-time university faculty member;
- a university researcher who also teaches or supervises students;
- a government or private research-institute scientist;
- an industrial R&D employee whose work resembles engineering or product development;
- a postdoctoral or fellowship arrangement whose host, contract, or designated program is atypical;
- a research role involving clinical or other professionally licensed activity; and
- multiple appointments or separately paid lectures.

The evidence should focus on activity, institution, contract or appointment, period, position, remuneration, academic background, and research history. Professor renewal materials distinguish full-time and part-time cases, reinforcing that “Professor” does not mean only a permanent full professor.^5

## Intra-company Transferee is not a general multinational-company route

Intra-company Transferee uses the professional activity field of Engineer/Specialist but adds transfer-specific conditions. Official material identifies a defined transfer period and work at the relevant Japanese establishment as distinguishing features.^6 Current criteria and evidence also address qualifying prior overseas service, remuneration, the relationship between overseas and Japanese entities, the transfer order, and the existence of the offices.^7

The article should cover common assignee titles because the job can still be technical, humanities, or international-services work. It must also explain why the following need another route or closer review:

- a new external hire by a multinational company;
- a person without sufficient qualifying prior overseas service;
- a transfer between entities without the required relationship;
- indefinite assignment without a defined transfer period;
- work mainly at an unrelated client or location; and
- changing to an unrelated Japanese employer after arrival.

## Employer-facing counterpart

Iteration 5B should create `accepting-foreign-workers-and-supporting-coe-applications`, paralleling the school guide without pretending schools and businesses have identical duties.

The employer workflow should cover:

1. define the real role and identify plausible status boundaries before recruitment promises are made;
2. check the person's current work authority where relevant, without treating the optional Certificate of Authorized Employment as mandatory when authorization is already clear;
3. provide lawful written employment conditions and avoid discriminatory recruitment or employment practices;
4. collect only necessary applicant evidence and prepare truthful organization-side records;
5. use an authorized employee or qualified professional for application intermediation where applicable;
6. review the complete COE submission with the applicant and preserve copies;
7. state plainly that ISA issues the COE and that it does not guarantee a visa or landing;
8. prepare arrival, payroll, tax, social-insurance, workplace, language, and emergency orientation handoffs;
9. complete applicable foreign-worker employment reporting and any distinct organization-side ISA reporting; and
10. coordinate resignation, termination, transfer, role change, renewal, and resident notification without implying that one filing resolves all others.

ISA's online filing guidance limits who may act through an accepting organization and warns about unauthorized paid representation.^8 MHLW requires employer reporting when covered foreign workers are hired or leave, while ISA describes separate organization-notification rules and exceptions.^9 These responsibilities must remain separate in both prose and visual presentation.

## Period, renewal, and ongoing work

The current official list gives Professor, Researcher, Instructor, Engineer/Specialist, and Intra-company Transferee the same available periods: five years, three years, one year, or three months.^1 The implementation should distinguish those available values from the period an individual is actually granted.

Renewal must not be described as automatic. Each article should address:

- continuation of the authorized principal activity;
- current institution or organization and contract evidence;
- remuneration and ability to maintain residence;
- tax, notification, and other compliance considered in residence review;
- changes in duties, worksite, organization, or employment structure;
- whether the resident instead needs a change of status; and
- timing, temporary travel, and pending-application considerations through shared guidance.

Outside work should link to the existing focused guide. ISA identifies a university Professor teaching at a private company and individual self-employed or hard-to-measure work as examples requiring an outside-activity analysis.^2

## Proposed Iteration 5B scope

Iteration 5B should:

1. rewrite the five academic and technical status guides with periods, renewal, qualification paths, work boundaries, evidence, and visible representative occupations;
2. include a prominent English-teaching comparison and reciprocal route links;
3. create the employer-facing COE and lawful-acceptance guide, `employer` audience, and focused employer journey;
4. populate the existing structured status records with source-backed facts for all five routes;
5. expand reviewed search terms, FAQs, and Japanese terminology for common occupations and procedures;
6. connect employer, side-work, notification, route-selection, and status guides without duplicating cross-cutting rules; and
7. keep all new or materially revised guidance at `needs-review` pending explicit human approval.

It should not rewrite regulated or creative professions, business and workforce programs, employment law generally, or the later tax and social-insurance curriculum.

## Approved direction for Iteration 5B

The editor approved the proposed scope on September 11, 2026, with the explicit requirement that the English-teaching decision path receive a concise standalone comparison guide as well as reciprocal treatment in the three relevant status guides.

1. Use the visible occupation-map format: representative titles, why they may fit, and nearby roles requiring another check.
2. Publish the concise standalone teaching comparison and link it reciprocally with Professor, Instructor, and Engineer/Specialist.
3. Create the proposed employer guide, `employer` Audience value, and employer journey.
4. Keep dispatched ALT and ambiguously classified schools as explicit conditional cases rather than asserting one universal route.
5. Use duty-dependent everyday titles in search metadata while explaining visibly that titles never guarantee status fit.
6. Keep detailed employment-law and social-insurance instruction in their later batches while giving employers a clear onboarding and reporting handoff now.
