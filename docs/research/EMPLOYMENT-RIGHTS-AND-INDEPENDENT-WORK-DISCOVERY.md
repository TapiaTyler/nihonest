# Employment rights and independent work discovery

Prepared September 12, 2026 for Phase 13 Iteration 10A.

## Proposed direction

Create a distinct **Working in Japan** content group. The current Professional work group helps a reader choose and maintain immigration permission; it should not also carry the entire employment-law curriculum. The new group should begin with the employment relationship and documents, then cover pay and time, employment-linked coverage, workplace problems, side jobs, freelance commissioning, and endings.

The central boundary is:

> Immigration permission answers whether an activity is authorized. It does not decide whether someone is an employee, whether a contract term is lawful, whether wages are correct, whether insurance applies, or whether a client followed the Freelance Act.

Likewise, paying tax or receiving a business registration does not authorize work.

## Proposed implementation split

### Iteration 10B-1 — Employee foundation and employment endings

- Understanding your employment contract and working conditions.
- Wages, working hours, overtime, and paid leave.
- Resigning, dismissal, and fixed-term contract endings.
- A new employment-lifecycle journey and the first Working in Japan group placement.

This pass gives every employed reader a coherent baseline before introducing optional or problem-dependent branches.

### Iteration 10B-2 — Coverage, problems, side jobs, and independent contracts

- Employment insurance, employee social insurance, and work-related injury orientation.
- Workplace harassment, safety, evidence, and multilingual help routes.
- Side jobs and multiple employers under employment rules.
- Freelance contracts and client obligations under the Freelance Act.
- Refocus the existing side-work guide on immigration and connect it to employment, freelance, insurance, and tax layers.
- Complete FAQ, glossary, search, group, and journey integration.

## Findings that must control drafting

### A label does not settle the relationship

Calling someone a contractor, manager, trainee, or freelancer does not itself decide which protections apply. Drafting must ask about actual direction, control, independence, equipment, substitution, economic relationship, and other facts, then route uncertain classification to qualified help. The Freelance Act must never be presented as a replacement for labor protections when the person may legally be a worker.

### Side work is not a simple yes-or-no rule

The current MHLW materials encourage workable side-job arrangements and include a model rule permitting outside work, while recognizing restrictions connected to work performance, secrets, competition, reputation, legal working-time limits, and health. A model rule is not a universal statutory entitlement. Multiple employed jobs can raise aggregate working-time questions; independent contracts do not simply inherit the same analysis. Immigration, employer rules, tax, insurance, and licensing remain separate checks.

### Ending work has several legally different forms

Voluntary resignation, agreed separation, dismissal, and fixed-term nonrenewal must not share one generic “quit” checklist. Dismissal notice or allowance is a procedural protection and does not itself establish substantive validity. Conversely, the popular two-week resignation statement is too broad for every fixed term, workplace rule, collective arrangement, or dispute. The guide should help readers identify the event, preserve documents, request the correct records, and find help.

### Freelance Act coverage is narrower than everyday language

The Act took effect November 1, 2024 and uses defined commissioning-party and freelancer concepts. Written or electronic transaction terms apply broadly to covered outsourcing, while payment, prohibited-conduct, care, harassment, and termination duties vary with the ordering party and engagement duration. Consumer commissions, direct sales, freelancers with employees, overseas relationships, and possible disguised employment require separate treatment.

### Coverage systems must not be merged

Employee health insurance and pension, employment insurance, workers' accident compensation, municipal National Health Insurance and National Pension, private policies, and immigration have different tests and purposes. The implementation should provide a decision map and handoffs rather than a single threshold. A workplace injury guide must also warn readers not to substitute ordinary health-insurance handling for a possible work or commuting injury claim.

## Proposed public structure

1. Check the employment relationship and written terms.
2. Understand pay, hours, leave, and records.
3. Confirm employment-linked insurance and work-injury routes.
4. Use a conditional workplace-problem and help branch.
5. Use conditional side-job or freelance branches.
6. Identify the type of employment ending and complete separate immigration, insurance, tax, and municipal handoffs.

The new journey should not imply that harassment, side work, injury, or dismissal happens to everyone. Those are clearly labeled conditional branches.
