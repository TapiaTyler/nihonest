# Creative and media work discovery

Prepared September 11, 2026. This is a scope proposal, not published legal guidance.

## Finding

Creative professions need to be searchable, but they cannot be mapped safely from job title to one residence status. The strongest design is one cross-status comparison backed by deeper status guides.

The official Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services page names “designer” as an example, and the current clarification package includes design-related education and employment cases.[^1] That supports discovery for graphic, visual, brand, web, UI, UX, digital-product, motion, multimedia, and some game or advertising design roles. It does not make those titles an approval list. The contract, actual duties, qualification relationship, remuneration, and neighboring-status exclusions remain material.

Photography illustrates why title-only routing fails. Income-producing artistic photography may need comparison with Artist; photography for a foreign news organization may point to Journalist; and ISA expressly includes commercial photography in an Entertainer activity category.[^2] The same person may need a different analysis when moving between a gallery practice, news assignment, advertising shoot, and in-house design job.

Film and digital media have the same problem. ISA expressly identifies broadcast-program or film production, advertising activity, and commercial recording or filming within an Entertainer category.[^2] A performer, production worker, employed corporate-media specialist, foreign-news camera operator, and independent artist therefore should not receive the same answer merely because each uses “film” or “video” in a title.

Published guidance must explain the facts that determine whether the work is specialized employment, artistic creation, entertainment production, journalism, business management, or another activity.

## Route map to implement

### Employed specialist design

Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services is a route to examine for specialized design, digital-product, advertising, or related work performed under a contract with a Japanese organization. The official “designer” example and design cases support this comparison.[^1] Iteration 5B-2 should add the reviewed design titles to the existing article and search metadata, while keeping the role-description and qualification tests prominent.

### Independent artistic creation

Artist covers income-producing artistic activity and excludes activity assigned to Entertainer.[^3] A fine artist, illustrator, writer, fine-art photographer, or another independent creator may need this comparison. A portfolio or creative identity alone does not establish the activity, viable income, or boundary from employed specialist work.

### Commercial production and entertainment

Entertainer materials expressly cover advertising activity, broadcast or film production, commercial photography, and commercial audio or video recording in addition to performance categories.[^2] Iteration 6 must explain which activity category is being discussed and the corresponding organization, contract, schedule, position, remuneration, and evidence questions.

### Foreign-news reporting

Journalist requires journalistic activity under contract with a foreign news organization. ISA names reporters and camera personnel as examples.[^4] Photojournalist and news photographer should therefore be searchable, but the guide must not route domestic publishing or generic documentary work to Journalist from title alone.

### Unpaid artistic study

Cultural Activities covers unpaid artistic activity and specified study of Japanese culture or arts.[^5] It should appear as a nearby comparison, never as a substitute for paid creative employment.

### Operating a creative business

Business Manager may become relevant when a person genuinely operates or manages a creative agency, production company, or studio. “Freelancer,” “founder,” and “creative director” do not by themselves establish management activity. Production duties, client work, side activity, and business operation should remain separate questions.

## High-priority edge cases

- A graphic designer employed by an agency versus an independent illustrator with several clients.
- A UI or UX title combining user research, product planning, visual design, and implementation.
- A game role combining visual art, narrative, programming, production, and marketing.
- An actor or model versus the people directing, shooting, editing, or producing the work.
- A fine-art photographer, foreign-news photojournalist, commercial advertising photographer, wedding photographer, and in-house studio photographer.
- A corporate video producer versus a worker contracted for a film or broadcast production.
- A YouTuber, influencer, streamer, or content creator with platform, sponsorship, advertising, foreign-client, or Japanese-company income.
- A work-status holder accepting commissions, royalties, exhibitions, or short projects alongside the principal job.
- A creative-agency owner who also performs the creative production.
- A remote creator serving foreign clients while physically residing in Japan.

The wedding, event, influencer, and remote-work examples require particularly careful treatment. The current sources establish useful boundaries but do not justify one universal status result for those models.

## Proposed content changes

1. During Iteration 5B-2, add common design and digital-media titles to the visible occupation map and search metadata of `engineer-specialist-humanities-international-services-status`, with clear duty-dependent language.
2. During Iteration 6, create `choosing-a-residence-status-for-creative-and-media-work` as the central comparison guide.
3. Rewrite the Artist, Entertainer, and Journalist guides around their distinct activity tests, evidence, periods, renewal, and transitions.
4. Review and link Cultural Activities, Business Manager, side-work, and unrestricted-status guidance where relevant.
5. Add FAQs for graphic design, film and video production, photography, content creation, and mixed or freelance creative work.
6. Do not create a separate article for every profession yet. Split film-production or independent-creator guidance only if the researched comparison becomes too dense to scan safely.

## Scope decision requested

Approve the central comparison guide, the searchable role inventory, and the staged implementation above. This fills the discoverability gap without teaching users that profession names are immigration categories.
