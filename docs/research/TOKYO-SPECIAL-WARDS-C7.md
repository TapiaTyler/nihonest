# Tokyo special wards: C7 address-registration batch

Source inspection: September 23, 2026. This final code-order batch adds Nerima
(`jp-13120`), Adachi (`jp-13121`), Katsushika (`jp-13122`), and Edogawa
(`jp-13123`) to the address-registration article. Their IDs were already in the
J-LIS administrative registry. With the Shinjuku pilot and C1–C7 batches, all
23 Tokyo special wards have address-registration guidance. No other article
gains ward-specific guidance. These four supplements remain `needs-review`
pending human editorial approval; no Japanese translation artifacts were
created or changed.

| Ward | Current official evidence | Claim boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Nerima | The ward's [domestic](https://www.city.nerima.tokyo.jp/kurashi/koseki/kiroku/tennyu.html) and [overseas](https://www.city.nerima.tokyo.jp/kurashi/koseki/kiroku/tennyukokugai.html) pages, updated April 1 and August 17, 2026, require filing within 14 days after living at the new address. The overseas page lists original passports and entry-date proof for all movers, foreign residents' cards or certificates, and possible translator-identified relationship proof. All six residents' offices receive overseas notices weekdays 8:30–17:00, with arrival by about 16:00 advised; Saturday and Sunday intake are excluded. Groups of five or more foreign movers are asked to call ahead. | Domestic counter hours differ; do not carry Saturday availability over to overseas arrivals. The relationship document is conditional, and Japanese nationals' koseki documents are not required of foreign residents. |
| Adachi | The [combined moving-in procedure](https://www.city.adachi.tokyo.jp/koseki/kurashi/todokede/h-tennyu.html), updated March 26, 2026, requires in-person filing within 14 days. It accepts filing at the Ward Office South Building or any of 16 residents' offices, irrespective of address. Domestic movers generally bring a moving-out certificate unless a My Number route applies. Overseas movers bring passports, entry-date proof where an automated gate left no stamp, and foreign residents' cards or certificates. | The ward says a short temporary return from abroad is not a new resident move-in; do not apply a rigid one-year rule to all foreign residents. Japanese nationals' koseki documents do not apply to foreign residents. |
| Katsushika | The [residence-notification page](https://www.city.katsushika.lg.jp/kurashi/1000046/1001401/1001438.html), updated September 23, 2026, permits filing at the Ward Office or a residents' office, within 14 days after residence begins. For overseas foreign arrivals, it lists passports showing entry date and residence cards or special permanent resident certificates, including the passport route stamped for later residence-card issue. It asks groups of five or more foreign residents filing together to contact the ward in advance. | Its automated-gate fallback paragraph is expressly written for Japanese nationals; do not project that exact instruction onto foreign residents. No family-translation requirement is stated on this page. |
| Edogawa | The [moving-in procedure](https://www.city.edogawa.tokyo.jp/e038/kurashi/todoke/ido_todoke/tennyu.html), updated June 5, 2026, requires in-person filing within 14 days after starting residence and directs residents to the Ward Office or local offices. For overseas arrivals, it lists passports for all movers and other entry-date proof when no stamp is present. Foreign residents bring residence cards or special permanent resident certificates; some households need official relationship evidence with a Japanese translation naming the translator. A [foreign-resident overview](https://www.city.edogawa.tokyo.jp/foreign/tetsuzuki/jumintoroku.html) confirms the counter route. | The general moving-in page also lists koseki documents without distinguishing nationality in that paragraph. Do not apply Japanese nationals' koseki requirements to foreign residents. The [older overseas FAQ](https://www.city.edogawa.tokyo.jp/e031/qa/kurashi/todokede/kaigai_tennnyuu.html) provides more detailed eligibility language, but the supplement relies on the current procedure for its core claims. |

The detailed current pages are Japanese. The English supplements summarize
their route distinctions and link to those originals; they are not official
translations. The next geographic iteration is a coverage and source audit
across the 23 wards before selecting another bounded local subject or
municipality set. Japanese localization remains deferred by the user.
