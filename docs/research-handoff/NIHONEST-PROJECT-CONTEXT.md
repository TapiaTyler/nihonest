# Nihonest project context for external research

## Product purpose

Nihonest is a mobile-first guidance application for people planning to enter, move to, study, work, or live in Japan. It explains immigration, residence, employment, tax, municipal, housing, healthcare, transportation, and daily-life processes in approachable language while routing readers to responsible authorities.

Nihonest provides general educational information, not individualized legal, immigration, tax, medical, or financial advice.

## Audience assumptions

Do not assume that readers:

- speak Japanese;
- understand Japanese government structure or administrative vocabulary;
- share one nationality, profession, family situation, or municipality;
- understand the difference between a visa, status of residence, landing permission, or Certificate of Eligibility; or
- can infer whether a national rule, local procedure, employer requirement, or common practice controls.

## Canonical content model

The repository currently stores canonical English guidance in version-controlled files rather than a CMS or article database. Stable IDs connect:

- articles and content groups;
- glossary terms;
- residence statuses;
- FAQs;
- guided journeys;
- official source records;
- local supplements; and
- annotated document examples.

Formal research findings must reference only IDs supplied by the active brief. Topic-discovery suggestions may use a descriptive proposed title, but must not invent a final repository ID.

## Editorial states

Research and AI drafting do not confer human approval. New or materially changed guidance remains `needs-review` until the project editor explicitly approves it. A source being official does not by itself prove that a summary is complete or correctly applied.

## Core reasoning boundaries

Research must keep these layers distinct:

- consular visa issuance;
- Certificate of Eligibility review;
- landing examination and permission;
- status of residence and permitted activity;
- change, extension, re-entry, and notification procedures;
- employer, school, sponsor, or representative responsibilities;
- professional licensing and regulated activity;
- employment or private-contract restrictions;
- national and local tax;
- social insurance and pension;
- national, prefectural, and municipal implementation; and
- common practice or etiquette.

Approval, permission, compliance, or payment in one layer must not be presented as satisfying another.

## Source and date sensitivity

High-stakes claims require current authoritative support. Dates matter when a program, fee, form, threshold, transition rule, or procedure has changed or is scheduled to change. Future rules must be identified as future; historical rules must not appear current.

Research should prefer exact official pages over home pages, news summaries, search snippets, unofficial explanations, or secondary articles. Japanese-language primary material is acceptable and often necessary.

## Geographic boundaries

National requirements must not be presented as universal local instructions. Prefectural and municipal differences should identify the jurisdiction and responsible body. Unsupported locations should retain national orientation and an official local handoff rather than fabricated local detail.

## Search and discoverability

Nihonest supports ordinary-language discovery, but search aliases are routing metadata—not evidence that a title, activity, or user situation legally belongs to a particular residence status.

## Current sequencing

Canonical English research and review occur before catalog-wide localization. A behavior-preserving structural refactor is planned after Phase 13 closes and before Phase 14 localization. External topic discovery should concentrate on overlooked coverage, edge cases, and useful source leads. Codex is responsible for primary-source verification and repository implementation. Neither workflow should propose a CMS, database migration, native application, or broad architectural rewrite unless the active brief expressly requests it.
